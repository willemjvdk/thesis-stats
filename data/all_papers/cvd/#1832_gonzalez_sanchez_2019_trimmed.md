## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# 2. Methods
## 2.1. Design

The findings shown here are a subanalysis of the EVIDENT II study [16], which was intended to evaluate the effect of using a smartphone application on lifestyle improvement such as diet and physical activity for 3 months. The Evident II study was a randomized, controlled, multicenter clinical trial with 2 parallel groups. It was carried out with a follow-up period of 12 months. Between January 2014 and September 2016, evaluations were made at baseline and after the completion of 3 and 12 months. First results for the EVIDENT II study, can be found in a previously published [17]. The EVIDENT II study is registered at www. clinicaltrials.gov with the ID number: NCT02016014.

## 2.2. Procedures

Each participant completed an initial visit and two follow-up visits at three and twelve months after study initiation between January 2014 and September 2016 (Fig. 1).

## 2.3. Setting and participants

The study included six primary care groups in the Spanish Research Network for Preventive Activities and Health Promotion in Primary Care (REDIAPP). The study population was selected from the EVIDENT I study, whose objective was to analyze the relationship of physical activity and dietary pattern to the blood pressure circadian pattern, pulse wave velocity, and carotid intima-media thickness in individuals without arteriosclerotic disease [18]. The participants were selected by random sampling among the patients who attended a consultation with their family doctor in each participating center. Eligibility criteria included being aged between 18 and 70 years old. Subjects were excluded if they were unable to do exercise or follow the Mediterranean diet, or if they met any of the exclusion criteria of the EVIDENT I study. These criteria were the known presence of coronary or cerebrovascular atherosclerotic disease; heart failure; moderate or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; musculoskeletal disease involving limited walking; advanced respiratory, renal, or hepatic disease; severe mental disease; or a treated oncological disease diagnosed in the last 5 years.

## 2.4. Screening and randomization

From the 1553 subjects recruited in the EVIDENT I study, 250 were excluded for being older than 70 years, 85 did not meet the inclusion criteria, 325 declined to participate, and 60 were excluded for other reasons. The final group included 833 subjects (Fig. 1). The recruited subjects were randomized into two groups with a ratio of 1:1 on the basis of centralization from Salamanca. This included a counseling + application group (intervention group; IG) comprising 415 subjects and a counseling group (control group; CG) with 418 subjects. The allocation sequence was generated through a computer program (Consellería de Sanidade, X.d.G., Epidat 4.0, Santiago de Compostela, Spain) [19] by an independent researcher and was concealed until the trial arms had been assigned. To minimize contamination between groups, the investigator who performed the intervention was different from the investigator who conducted the evaluation. The investigator who performed the data analysis was blinded. Due to the nature of the study, the subjects could not be blinded to the intervention.

## 2.5. Intervention

## 2.5.1. Standardized counseling

Counseling on physical activity. Both groups (control and intervention) received counseling on physical activity with a view to favoring compliance with the current recommendations on physical activity in the general population. The intervention has demonstrated its effectiveness in the PEPAF study [20]. Counseling consisted of an individual visit lasting 15 min in which an explanation was given of the health benefits of physical activity, with the recommendation to perform at least 30 min of moderate activity 5 days a week, or 20 min of vigorous activity three days a week. Counseling was standardized in both groups, and the subjects received an informational brochure on the session. The first part (5 min.) focused on the recommendations on physical exercise in relation to cardiovascular health. The second part (8 min.) consisted on knowledge of the intensity of some specific physical activities such as walking, riding a bicycle, or other activities. The last part (2 min.) was dedicated to answering questions and doubts. Specific advice was given to reduce the number of hours spent sitting.

Nutritional counseling. Both groups (control and intervention) received nutrition counseling aimed at favoring adherence to the Mediterranean Diet. This intervention has been shown to be effective in the PREDIMED study [21]. Counseling consisted of an individual visit lasting 15 min in which the concepts of the Mediterranean Diet were explained, with insistence upon the importance of complying with each of the recommended points. Counseling was standardized in both groups, and the subjects received an informational brochure on the session. The first part (3 min.) developed the concept of the Mediterranean Diet. The second part of the session (10 min.) focused on each particular recommendation, with brief and clear messages. The last part of the interview (2 min.) was dedicated to answering questions and doubts.

Participants were given advice taking into account the motivation stage for the change they were in, following the model of Prochaska and Diclemente [22], reinforcing their attitude in case of preparation (subjects that seriously consider the intention to change their behavior in the near future (within 30 days)), action (subjects that are actively working in behavioral changes that affect their health) or maintenance (subjects adopt the behaviors acquired as routine) and encouraging those who were in pre-contemplation (subjects are aware that certain behaviors are a risk for their health or that they have a health problem) and contemplation (subjects are aware that certain behaviors are a risk for their health or that they have a health problem, and agree to make changes within 6 months).

## 2.5.2. The counseling+application group (IG) intervention

The specific intervention consisted of giving the IG subjects a smartphone with the project app (Evident II) and advising on how to use it for 3 months. The tool developed (intellectual property registry no. SA-81-14) is the result of an agreement between the CGB Computer Company (Salamanca, Spain) and the GIAPCyL research group of the REDIAPP (RD12/0005/0004), through the Infosalud Foundation. The application was designed by software engineers in collaboration with dietitians and physical activity experts with an easy-to-use interface. This app was designed to promote the Mediterranean diet and increased levels of physical activity. The application generated detailed information on the eating deviations (diet composition and calories), with a view to facilitating changes in habits. Participants were shown how to enter a record of the food they consumed (breakfast, lunch, afternoon snack and dinner). Physical activity was evaluated, using an accelerometer included in the smartphone application, and which counted the steps taken over a period of 24 h. The activity reported by the subject during periods in which the device is not used (e.g., swimming and other sports) also was considered, assessing compliance with the exercise objectives and providing recommendations to increase activity and reach the target of 10,000 steps a day defining an active individual.

At the end of the day, the app provided a summary of food intake, physical activity performed and a balance of calories consumed and spent. In addition, the app provided a recommended plan for the following days with the aim of improving eating habits and increasing physical activity. The IG completed two additional appointments. The first one occurred seven days after the baseline in which the application was explained and the second one was a phone call 15 days after the baseline visit to confirm that the application was being used correctly and to clarify any possible doubts. The IG participants returned the electronic device three months later during the assessment visit, which was also carried out with CG participants. The information stored on the device was downloaded for further analysis. In IG, adherence to the smartphone app was assessed through the number of recorded days in the device. We classified recordings into 4 categories, that were (1) 0 days, (2) 1–30 days, (3) 31–60 days, and (4) > 60 days. A food log of > 60 days in the 3 months that the participant had the smartphone represented high adherence.

# 2.6. Primary and secondary outcomes
The main result was the effect on CVRFs and CVR at 3 and 12 months of post-intervention follow-up in the IG compared to the CG. In order to measure CVRFs, we considered SBP, total cholesterol and HDL cholesterol as quantitative variables. A history of diabetes mellitus, pharmacological treatment for hypertension and hyperlipidemia, and smoking were analyzed as dichotomous variables. Three measurements were done of SBP in each arm, and the mean of the last two measurements with the highest values was used. An OMRON blood pressure monitor model M10-IT (Omron Health Care, Kyoto, Japan) was used for this purpose, following the recommendations of the European Society of Hypertension [23]. Hypertension was recorded with figures ≥ 140 mm Hg SBP and/or ≥ 90 mm Hg DBP, or if the subject was on antihypertensive medication [24]. To measure lipids, glucose, insulinemia and HbA1c, a blood sample was taken after the participants had fasted for at least 10 h. Standard automated enzymatic methods were used for serum measurements of total cholesterol, HDL-cholesterol and triglycerides. LDL cholesterol was estimated using the Friedewald equation when the direct parameter was not available. Dyslipidemia was present if total cholesterol ≥ 240 mg/dl or triglycerides ≥ 200 mg/dL, or lipid-lowering treatments were used [25]. Type 2 diabetes mellitus was present with HbA1c ≥ 6.5% or fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dl or 2 h of plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dl during an oral glucose tolerance test, or a random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dl for a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia or hyperglycaemic crisis or under antidiabetic treatment [26]. Information regarding drugs used for hypertension and hyperlipidemia was gathered by accessing data from the electronic medical records and then confirmed by the subjects themselves. Smoking history was assessed through questions about the participant's smoking status (smoker/non-smoker). We considered smokers to be subjects who currently smoke or who stopped smoking less than one year ago. CVR was estimated using the published risk equation (D’Agostino scale) based on the Framingham study [27]. Risk factors used include age, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol and SBP as quantitative variables, and sex, drug treatment for hypertension, smoking and history of diabetes mellitus as dichotomous variables.

## 2.7. Other measurements

A detailed description of the clinical data, anthropometric measurements and analytical parameters collected has already been published [16].

## 2.8. Ethical considerations

As reference committee, the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of

the health care area of Salamanca approved the study (June 21, 2013), and the ethics committees of the 5 collaborating centers did likewise. All participants signed the informed consent form before inclusion in the study in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki [28].

## 2.9. Statistical analysis

We estimated the sample size in relation to Cardiovascular risk as main objective in this work. Assuming an alpha = .05 and beta = .20 with a standard deviation common of change from baseline of Cardiovascular risk of 3.6%, in order to detect a reduction of 1 point in the intervention group with respect to the control group, we would have needed 410 participants (205 per group). We considered that the 833 participants included in EVIDENT II study are sufficient to detect clinically relevant differences in this endpoint.

Intention-to-treat analysis was performed with the baseline values carried forward. In the descriptive study of the baseline data, the results were expressed with the mean ± the standard deviation. The frequency distribution was used for categorical variables. Student's t was used to compare the means between the two independent groups. To analyze the changes within the same group at 3 and 12 months postrandomization in the main and secondary variables, Student's t for paired data was used. To compare the intra-group changes in antihypertensive and lipid-lowering drug treatments, McNemar and Cochran's Q tests were used. The effect of the intervention and the changes observed between the IG and the CG were analyzed using an ANCOVA model adjusted for the baseline value of each variable. Additionally, the interaction between the variables studied and the group (IG or CG) was analyzed. Changes in CVRFs and global CVR (D'Agostino) at 3 and 12 months by sex, were analyzed with the same way that were analyzed the effects of the intervention. For the hypothesis test, a statistical significance level of 0.05 was established. The data was analyzed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA).

# 3. Results
## 3.1. Baseline characteristics of participants and follow-up

The mean age of the 833 subjects in the study was 51.4 (SD 12.1) in the IG and 52.3 (SD 12.0) years in the CG, with women predominant in both groups: IG (249/415, 60.0%) and CG (268/418, 64.1%). Baseline data did not differ between the 2 groups (Table 1). Of the 833 subjects included in the study, 63 had been lost in the IG (15.1%) at 12 months and 55 in the CG (13.1%), meaning that 715 subjects were finally assessed, 352 of them in the IG and 363 in the CG. Flowchart is presented in Fig. 1. The median use of the app was 67 days. 236 participants in the IG group (56%) had high app adherence (61–90 days), and 117 (28.2%) used it for less than a month.

## 3.2. Analysis of repeated measures

Intervention effect: An effect between groups was observed in favor of the CG at 12 months in SBP, DBP, total cholesterol and triglycerides: 2.02 mmHg (95%CI: 0.43–3.61), 1.21 mmHg (95%CI: 0.20–2.24), 5.24 mg/dl (95%CI: 1.22–9.26) and 7.24 mg/dl (95%CI: 0.53–14.32). Nevertheless, no significant changes were observed at 3 or 12 months in terms of CVR (Table 2). Likewise, group interaction was observed in SBP over time ( \mathbf { p } \ < \ 0 . 0 5 )and in total cholesterol (p < 0.05) but not in HDL cholesterol ( \mathbf { p } = 0 . 6 7 5 )nor CRV (p = 0.066) (Fig. 2).

## 3.3. Changes in cardiovascular risk factors and cardiovascular risk

In terms of SBP (mmHg), a reduction of -2.27 (95% CI: -3.37 to -1.18) was found the IG at 3 months with respect to the initial figure, but this is no longer significant at 12 months. In the CG, meanwhile,

there is a reduction of -4.12 (95% CI: -5.26 to -2.99) at 3 months and of -2.75 (95% CI: -4.00 to -1.51) at 12 months.

The only decrease in terms of total cholesterol (mg/dL), of -5.68 (95% CI: -8.67 to -2.69), occurred in the CG at 12 months. We also failed to observe significant changes in HDL cholesterol (mg/dL), with the exception of a reduction of -1.15 (95% CI: -2.00 to -0.29) in the CG at 12 months. There was a reduction in baseline glucose in the CG of -1.35 (95% CI:-2.42 to -0.27) at 3 months.

Cardiovascular risk (CVR D'Agostino 2008) decreased to a similar degree in both groups at 3 months: IG -0.49 (95% CI: -0.83 to -0.15) and CG -0.56 (95% CI: -0.87 to -0.25). At 12 months, this decrease is only maintained in the CG: -0.55 (95% CI: -0.95 to -0.15) (Table 3).

## 3.4. Changes in the use of lipid-lowering and antihypertensive medication

At 3 months, the consumption of lipid-lowering and antihypertensive drugs decreased slightly in the IG (-0.3% and -0.4%) and increased in the CG (1% and 2.4%) with respect to baseline figures. At 12 months, increases in the use of lipid-lowering (0.7% in the IG and 4.1% in the CG) and antihypertensive medication (2.1% in the IG and 3.2% in the CG) were found compared to initial figures. The increase in the CG of antihypertensive and lipid-lowering drugs at 3 and 12 months was statistically significant ( \mathbf { P } \ < \ 0 . 0 5 ), but this was not the case in the IG ( \mathbf { P } > 0 . 0 5 )(Table 4), although no difference was found when comparing both groups at 3 and 12 months.

## 3.5. Analysis of repeated measures by subgroups (sex)

When analyzing the effect of the intervention by sex, it was observed that there are no differences in either CVRFs or CVR, with lower SBP (mmHg) and global CVR in men and women of both groups found at 3 months. Total cholesterol (mg/dL) decreased at 12 months in women in both groups, while this value increased among men in the IG (Fig. 3).
