# Methods
The study was a randomized, double-crossover agreement design, using pairs of face-to-face and remote SLP evaluators. The study was designed to test two effects. First, we tested the effect of using videoconferencing equipment to assess functional communication; that is, does assessment by videoconferencing bias the measurement in one direction or the other? Second, we tested the effect of using videoconferencing equipment to direct the patient through the assessment exercise. We selected a measurement system with known psychometric properties of functional communication that are relevant to stroke patients and that could be administered by videoconferencing. The procedures enhanced inter-rater reliability and eliminated serial correlation bias by the raters.

We recruited two SLPs at two study sites. The four SLPs completed the ASHA self-study course on categorizing patients to each level on the functional communication measures (FCMs). The SLPs submitted their qualifying examinations to ASHA, which scored the examinations. Each SLP qualified for certification, but only one had previously used the tool in usual practice. Certification ensured a relatively high level of inter-rater reliability (at least 80%), but not perfect correlation.

The study used a convenience sample. The participating SLPs extended personal invitations to eligible patients who attended scheduled appointments at the National Rehabilitation Hospital in Washington, DC, and the INTEGRIS/Jim Thorpe Rehabilitation Hospital in Oklahoma City. The eligibility criteria for inclusion were: adults aged 18 years or over with a history of stroke, currently receiving outpatient or inpatient therapy for speech or physical problems, able to understand English spoken directions well enough to complete the physical or speech assessment process, able to comprehend and sign the informed consent documents. The exclusion criterion was candidates having uncorrected visual deficits such as visual-spatial neglect or field cuts, since they will not be able to see the entire video screen. Each patient received a $5 gift card for a local store. The study was approved by the appropriate committees and all study patients signed an informed consent form.

We devised criteria to use in a telemedicine equivalence trial: (1) be appropriate and relevant to people with stroke, (2) have known psychometric properties (high coefficients of validity and reliability) published in peer-reviewed literature, (3) be widely used in research and clinical practice, (4) be visually based (i.e. the SLP can assess the patient’s communication ability without touching the patient) and (5) be capable of completion within 30 min.

We selected the National Outcomes Measurement System (NOMS)15 devised by the ASHA, because it came the closest to meeting all the selection criteria. A key part of the NOMS is the set of FCMs that SLPs use to rate patients’ functional ability. There are 11 singleitem constructs: motor speech, voice, fluency, swallowing, spoken language comprehension, spoken language expression, writing, reading, attention, memory and pragmatics. Each FCM contains seven discrete levels, ranging from low to high function. The NOMS is recognized as a measurement tool by the Joint Commission on Accreditation of Health Care Organizations and is used by over 300 health-care and educational facilities (criterion 3).

The ASHA selected three FCMs that are relevant to the communication problems commonly encountered by post-stroke patients (criterion 1): motor speech, spoken language expression and spoken language comprehension. An unpublished study commissioned by the ASHA of the psychometric properties of the FCMs for adults, including reliability analysis, showed strong support for convergence between 1648 individual SLPs and gold-standard expert panel ratings, with virtually all SLPs scoring within a single point of the gold standard (partially meeting criterion 2). In addition, the level of agreement was as high as might be expected from repeated measures by the same raters.15 The ASHA granted the investigators and participating clinicians full access to the NOMS for the purposes of this protocol.

To fulfil criteria 4 and 5, we elected to administer a subset of the Boston Diagnostic Aphasia Examination (BDAE), which is a standard clinical protocol to evaluate aphasia.16 Each SLP was experienced in using the BDAE to evaluate stroke patients and used it in daily practice. Thus, it served as the script to ensure that each SLP asked each study participant to follow the same instructions and to guard against differences in verbal interactions between each trial. The therapists asked a uniform set of open-ended questions inviting responses from the patients in order to score motor speech and speech expression. The SLPs administered 1-i (presentation of picture) from Conversational and Expository Speech, 2-c (commands subtest) and 2-d (complex ideational material) from Auditory Comprehension to assess speech comprehension.

However, the therapists reported that their scores on the NOMS were a synthesis of the entire patient–therapist interaction over all portions of the BDAE administered.

The research protocol minimized – but did not completely eliminate – bias unrelated to the use of videoconferencing equipment. Test–retest correlation bias on the part of the SLPs and the patients was eliminated by assessing FCM simultaneously. That is, when the patient sat in front of the videoconferencing equipment, the patient was scored at the same time by both SLPs.

The bias of face-to-face administration was minimized by randomizing patients into one of two groups at each site. The first group was given the BDAE by the face-to-face SLP, and the second group was given the BDAE by the remote SLP. The only differences in administration between the face-to-face and remote SLPs were that the remote SLP would ask the face-toface SLP to present the picture card to the patient during subtest I-i, and to present a pencil, card and watch on a table in front of the patient for the commands subtest C.

The videoconferencing equipment (Soundstation, Polycom) was operated at a transmission speed of 384 kbit/s. Face-to-face and remote evaluations were conducted within the same facility. The SLPs recorded their data during the encounter. The data were analysed using Excel 2002. Following Bland and Altman,17,18 the limit of agreement was set at 95%.

# Results
We recruited six women and 18 men aged 25–81 years (median 64). The time since stroke ranged from two months to 15 years (median one year). Table 1 shows their distribution by age, race/ethnicity and average FCM score, as measured by the onsite therapist directing the patient through the assessment examination. Since the telerehabilitation application envisaged was outpatient therapy at home, we focused recruitment efforts on outpatients, although we also included some inpatients.

The percentage within the 95% limits of agreement, percent exact agreement and percent agreement within one point are shown in Table 2. The percentage within the 95% limits of agreement was the same as within a single point of exact agreement and ranged from 92% to 100%. However, the percentage exact agreement was much lower when the BDAE was administered by the remote therapist, ranging from 8% to 25%, than when the BDAE was administered by the face-to-face evaluator, ranging from 50% to 67%.
