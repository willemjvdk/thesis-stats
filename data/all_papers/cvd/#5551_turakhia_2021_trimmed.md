## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
SmartADHERE was a 2-arm, open-label, multicenter, randomized, controlled trial with a primary objective to compare the efficacy of a blended digital and human intervention with standard of care to improve DOAC adherence in participants with atrial fibrillation recently started on rivaroxaban for stroke prevention (National clinical trials identifier # NCT02996435). Participants age 18 years with documented atrial fibrillation who were taking or initiating newly-prescribed rivaroxaban ( 90 days of consumption or a prescription dated in the last 90 days) were eligible. Participants also had to be atrisk for medication nonadherence, based on answering “Yes” to at least 1 of 4 questions on the modified 4-part Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-4), a vali-

dated instrument for estimating nonadherence. For the MMAS-4, answering “Yes” to at least one question has a 73% sensitivity and 49% positive predictive value for low adherence. Answering “No” to all 4 questions has 75% sensitivity for high adherence.15,16 Participants were also required to possess a nonshared compatible iOS or Android smartphone with an active phone number, text messaging, and cellular data plan. Participants had to be willing to save their drug prescription bottles and pharmacy receipts. Major exclusion criteria were anticipated rivaroxaban use <12 months; use of specialized anticoagulation clinics for rivaroxaban medication management; use of specialized pharmacist-led adherence or refill monitoring; enrollment in any medication adherence program; and current use of adherence tracking devices including but not limited to smart pill bottles (such as those that remind patients when to take medicine with beeps or alerts), radiofrequency tagged dispensers, mobile or computer applications, or automated phone reminders. Pharmacy and health care plan automated refill reminders or pill organizers or containers that only compartmentalize medications based on days of the week were not exclusions.

## Study procedures

Consented participants who met inclusion and exclusion criteria had baseline data collected and a pill count performed at the baseline visit. Participants also completed a baseline 8-part Morisky Medication Adherence Survey (MMAS-8) before randomization. Compared to MMAS-4, the expanded questionnaire was developed to identify circumstances related to adherence behavior and has better psychometric properties for outcomes evaluation, with sensitivity and specificity of 93% and 53% for nonadherence.15 Participants were randomized to the intervention or control arm using a centralized interactive web response system, with randomization in permuted blocks and stratified by site. Postcards were sent to all participants in both arms at day 30 and day 90 after the baseline visit reminding them to save any unused rivaroxaban pills, prescription bottles, and pharmacy receipts.

## Study intervention

The study intervention was a centralized, blended process with several personalized components: (1) medication and refill tracking smartphone application (Care4Today version 2.0 or higher, Janssen Health Innovation, La Jolla, CA); (2) semiautomated text messaging platform; (3) coach-to-patient phone call system. Care4Today is platform that includes a mobile application that serves a digital version of a pillbox that addresses patient-related factors of nonadherence, which includes taking medicines as prescribed and ensuring sufficient quantity by reminding patients when pill supply is low. cations taken and reminds the user when to take medication and to obtain a refill based on entered prescription information (Figure 1, Panel A). Adherence data from the applications were securely transmitted from the phone

to a cloud platform and dashboard on which clinicians and caregivers could view the data (Figure 1, Panel B). We created a centralized dashboard of study participant IDs and their adherence data. For the study, the app

was initially configured only to the rivaroxaban dose, frequency and pill supply, but participants could also use the app to manage other medications.

The dashboard data were used to evaluate ongoing participant medication adherence. At the end of each 7- day period, the number of days that the participant had confirmed taking their medication for that period was counted from the Care4Today Patient Dashboard, and these data were used to provide a semiautomated text message using the Sprint Enterprise Messaging Gateway (Chicago, IL) based on level of adherence (Supplemental Table S1). Text messages were used from a rotating selection of prescripted messages designed to provide return of information (# of days adherence that week), positive reinforcement, education, and encouragement for behavior change. Pretesting was performed in a separate pool of healthy participants to ensure that the system was functioning and messages were received and understandable.

If the participant confirmed taking their medication for only 0, 1 or 2 days of the 7 days, then the participant received a phone call from the assigned coach (S.S.) at the central coordinating center, Stanford Center for Clinical Research, who performed a safety and technical assessment to determine whether the adherence data were correct (that medication was not taken rather than simply not entered into the application) and whether there were technical issues or barriers in using the app, and referring the participant to their physician or site study coordinator if there were clinical concerns of taking the medication or for getting refills. If the phone call was not answered by the participant, then a voicemail with the information was left and the information was also texted. The site PI or site study coordinator were also notified.

The weekly semiautomated scripted messaging was designed to address some of the components of the Health Belief Model.17 Participants would receive randomly selected messages from a pool of messages that aimed to address perceived susceptibility, and severity of stroke in atrial fibrillation and perceived benefits of anticoagulation. The reminder served as an external cue to action.

To minimize reminder fatigue which could lead to differential study noncompliance and withdrawal, participants had the option to de-escalate intervention intensity at any time, which was performed according to protocol (Supplement). Site study staff performed app installation, configuration, and intervention education using a strict protocol and were supported by technical support from the app manufacturer. Intervention study data were managed using REDCap electronic data capture platform services made possible by Stanford School of Medicine Research Office. The REDCap platform services at Stanford are subsidized by the National Center for Research Resources and the National Center for Advancing Trans-

lational Sciences, National Institutes of Health, through grant UL1 TR001085.

Participants randomized to the control arm received usual care by their primary AF clinician, who was the DOAC-prescribing clinician. All enrolling sites were cardiology or cardiac electrophysiology practices, and the prescribing physicians were practicing in one or both of these fields. Usual care for DOACs consisted the primary clinician or associated clinician or clinician extenders managing the participant refill requests and prescriptions. There were no refill or adherence reminders send by these clinicians of their offices, and DOACs were not managed by specialized anticoagulation or medication management clinics per the above-mentioned exclusion criteria.

# Outcomes
The primary outcome was adherence to rivaroxaban at 6 months defined by the proportion of days covered (PDC) by rivaroxaban. Medication adherence was estimated based on refill compliance and pill counts using a validated PDC algorithm.8 Consistent with prior literature, PDC is defined as the number of nonhospitalized days during which rivaroxaban was supplied divided by the total observation time during which rivaroxaban should have been taken. The PDC methodology has been previously detailed and validated8 against other direct adherence measures including drug levels.18,19 However, the algorithm requires careful assessment of the numerator and the denominator. Pill count, pill bottle days supply, and collection of pharmacy dispensation records were performed at the baseline and 6-month end of study visit. At the end-of-study, the total days hospitalized and days that rivaroxaban was cancelled, suspended or held was also recorded. Details on the PDC assessment and calculation can be found in the study protocol (Supplement).

Secondary outcomes included the binary outcome of proportion of participants with 6-month PDC ≥ 80%, change from baseline of the average MMAS-8 score, and medication persistence, which was defined as the proportion of participants on rivaroxaban with an active prescription at 6 months.

# Statistical methods
Prior estimates of PDC for rivaroxaban therapies range from 72% (standard deviation 12%)14 to 84% (standard deviation 22%).8 Based on these estimates, a sample size of 142 participants in each arm (for a total of 284 participants) would have 80% power to detect a difference in PDC of 10% between the arms when the common standard deviation equals 30% and with a 2-sided type I error rate of 5%. Assuming an estimated loss to follow-up of 25%, we planned for a sample size of 378 participants (189 participants in each arm).

All analyses were performed on the full analysis cohort based on the intent-to-treat principle. To adhere to this principle, partially observed data for participants who were lost to follow up and not followed for all 6 months were also used. Descriptive statistics were summarized by the study arms. The primary PDC end point was analyzed using an analysis of variance model with study arm and site as variables. The secondary end points of proportion of participants with PDC ≥ 80% and medication persistence of rivaroxaban were analyzed using a logistic regression model with study arm and site as independent variables. The difference in the change of MMAS-8 score from baseline to end of study between the 2 study arms was analyzed using a repeated measures linear model, adjusting for site. The interaction term of study arm and time of assessment (baseline/end-of-study) was used to evaluate this outcome.

To explore which baseline characteristics were most associated with nonadherence (defined as PDC < 80%) we used a LASSO20 (“least absolute shrinkage and selection operator”) regression model on patients with complete data across characteristics recorded in at least 80% of the full analysis cohort. This approach improves upon ordinary least squares estimates in terms of prediction accuracy and interpretation by imposing a constraint on the sum of the absolute value of the regression coefficients which shrinks some coefficients and sets others to 0; in our analysis, the optimal shrinkage parameter was chosen via 3-fold cross-validation. We included all baseline characteristics after assessing for multicollinearity using the variance inflation factor from a multivariable logistic regression model relating nonadherence to all baseline characteristics. LASSO-selected baseline characteristics were then entered into a multivariable logistic regression model to estimate odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, and P values.

PDC calculations, data management, and analysis were independently performed by the data coordinating center (Quantitative Sciences Unit, Stanford University, Stanford, CA). Statistical significance was assessed at the 0.05 level. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC) and R version 3.5 21, and LASSO was implemented via the glmnet 22 R package.

All participants provided informed consent. The study was approved by the institutional review boards of Stanford University, study sites, and a third-party central institutional review board (Western Institutional Review Board (WIRB), Puyallup, WA), which some sites elected to use.

# Results
The first participant was randomized on January 25, 2017 with planned 16 months of recruitment. Enrollment was slower than expected, with screen failures in 61 of the first 196 (31%) potential subjects. The primary

reason was failing to have a qualifying MMAS-4 score (Figure 2). By March 2018, only 139 (37% of planned) participants had been recruited. On September 11, 2017 the steering committee met to review sponsor request to terminate the study and decided to reveal the primary outcome for the 123 of 378 expected participants who had completed the study but with masking of the treatment arm. Based on these data showing high overall adherence, the likelihood to answer the primary hypothesis was only 51% even if target enrollment were achieved. Therefore, because of the slow recruitment, higher-than expected PDC, and lower-than expected variance, the study funding was terminated by the sponsor on March 22, 2018 and the trial stopped.

There were 139 participants enrolled (72 intervention and 67 SOC). Baseline characteristics are shown in Table I. The mean age was 65±9.6 years, 30% were female, median CHA2DS2-VASc was 3 (IQR 2-4), and the mean number of medications was 7±4.4. There were 16 participants (12%) withdrawn and 8 (6%) terminated early (Figure 2). There were no study-related adverse events.

A total of 1,549 text messages were sent to the 72 participants assigned to the intervention. There was one documented transmission failure that was re-sent to the participant on the same day without further error. In 1,654 weekly dashboard assessments from these 72 participants, there was a bimodal pattern of whether rivaroxaban consumption was recorded (0/7 days 284, 1/7=26, 2/7=47, 3/7=67, 4/7=110, 5/7=173, 6/7=308, 7/7=639). There were 303 phone calls made to 48 participants randomized to the intervention arm who documented having 0 to 2 of 7 days adherence in a single week (5-7 of 7 days of data not entered within the app or nonadherence). 49 (16%) of these calls were answered from 22 participants. Of these 49 calls, the most common reason for not entering adherence data were technical issues with phone or software (57%), did not enter data in the app but confirmed taking medication (18%), acknowledgement of missed doses (6%), hospitalization (6%). During the week following each 7-day intervention phone call of the 0 to 2 days adherent participants, the app-reported adherence was increased in 54 (18%) of cases.

## Factors associated with nonadherence

We also performed a post hoc analysis to identify baseline characteristics associated with nonadherence. The variance inflation factor was below 2.0 for all baseline characteristics, suggesting that multicollinearity was not of concern. 124 complete cases with 26 baseline characteristics were included in the LASSO regression which selected age, BMI, and history of hyperlipidemia as being most associated with nonadherence; only hyperlipidemia was associated with nonadherence

in the multivariable model (OR 0.26 (95% CI: 0.09-0.73)) (Table III).

## Intervention de-escalation

There were 3 participants in the intervention arm who opted for de-escalation, all requesting de-escalation to the Tier 3 level (Supplemental Table). All 3 participants confirmed taking their medication within the app for 0, 1 or 2 days of the 7 days each week and received weekly calls and texts from the coordinating center. Two partici-

pants confirmed technical difficulties with the app entry process. The third participant found it inconvenient to enter their medication intake each day and requested deescalation within the first month of enrollment.
