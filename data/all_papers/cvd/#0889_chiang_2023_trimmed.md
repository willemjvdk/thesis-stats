# METHODS
## Design

A randomized controlled trial with a two parallel-group design was conducted in patients with cardiometabolic multimorbidity to compare the differences in effectiveness on PA, cardiorespiratory fitness, and heart rate recovery between the experimental group (EG) and comparison group (CG).

## Participants and settings

Between April 2018 and January 2021, potential outpatients who agreed to participate at a medical center in Northern Taiwan were invited to screen for confirmed cardiometabolic multimorbidity, defined as the presence of two or more cardiometabolic conditions (i.e., hypertension, coronary artery diseases, myocardial infarction, hypertensive cardiovascular disease/ dilated cardiomyopathy, type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia, metabolic syndrome, overweight/obesity, or gout; Garin et al., 2016; WHO, 2016) by a research assistant. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged ≧18 years; (2) able to speak and understand Mandarin; (3) able to walk without assistance; and (4) had mobile phones and could easily access the Internet. Exclusion criteria included recent stroke

within 6 months, a history of arrhythmia, end-stage renal disease with dialysis, cancer, comorbid neurological or musculoskeletal conditions that produce moderate-to-severe physical disability, and being diagnosed with a mental illness. Eighty-three eligible participants from the initial 100 patients approached provided informed consent and were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either a 12-week tele-exercise training plus usual care (the EG) or usual care only (the CG). Sealed, opaque envelopes were used, following computer-generated random serial numbers. A research nurse, blinded to the random assignments, assessed the patients' baseline characteristics, and invited all the participants to undertake graded exercise testing to determine their baseline cardiorespiratory fitness, heart rate recovery, and maximum heart rate to develop an individualized exercise prescription of tele-exercise training in the EG based on the guidelines of the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM, 2017).

A graded exercise testing through a motorized ergometer (Corival Lode, Netherlands) with a standardized ramp protocol by increasing the resistance (10 W/min) at a consistent speed of 60 revolutions per minute (ACSM, 2017) was performed by two observers (a medical/rehabilitation doctor and a research nurse). The testing, which can also identify the potential risk of life-threatening cardiovascular conditions (e.g., acute myocardial infarction), was immediately terminated if a participant complained of exhaustion (rate of perceived exertion between 18 and 20 based on the Borg Scale); reached maximum heart rate or \mathsf { V O } _ { 2 \mathsf { p e a k } } ;; had a respiratory exchange ratio >1.15; developed unexpected symptoms (i.e., chest pain, dyspnea, pallor, diaphoresis, or dizziness); had systolic pressure >250 mmHg or diastolic pressure >120 mmHg; had a decrease in systolic pressure of >10 mmHg compared with the systolic pressure at rest; or requested stopping the test (ACSM, 2017).

Sample size estimation using repeated measures in multivariate analysis of variance (within–between interaction), with an effect size of 0.35, an alpha set at 0.05, and a power of 0.80, and number of measurements 2, resulted in a total of 67 required participants (Cohen, 1992). Considering a possible attrition rate of 10%–15%, the target recruited sample size was 74–78.

## Interventions

Each participant in both EG and CG received a single PA promotion counseling session (15–20 min) based on the ACSM's guidelines (ACSM, 2017). The CG maintained their usual care (regular routine outpatient follow-up per month and usual lifestyles), whereas the EG received a 12-week tele-exercise training program, which encompassed a mobile phone app with incorporated individual exercise prescription, heart rate-sensing clothes that provided telemonitoring of heart rate during each exercise session, and online communication software, with weekly remote reminders. The individual exercise prescription was based on the FITT principle: Frequency, three non-synchronized sessions/week; Intensity, target heart rate of 70% heart rate reserve (resting heart rate + 70% [maximum

heart rate − resting heart rate]) based on the Karvonen method or perceived exertion ratings between 12 and 14 on the Borg Scale (ACSM, 2017) during the entire length of the 12-week exercise training program; Time, 30 min/session of the main endurance training phase excluding the 5-min warm-up and cool-down phase; and Type (based on participants' preferences), brisk walking, jogging, bicycling, \mathtt { o r }home/community/institution/gym-based stationary ergometer exercise. The detailed training protocol has been previously reported (Chiang et al., 2020). The mobile phone app provided auto-feedback of the heart rate and an alarm function that reminded individuals' appropriate exercise intensity (target heart rate) based on their exercise prescription calculated and set up inside the app. The record of each exercise training time and heart rate response was monitored by an experienced and well-trained nurse and research assistant and a physiotherapist in a medical center at Taipei city in northern Taiwan. A free, downloadable online instant communication software, the LINE phone application, provided a platform for interaction and communication through texts, images, video, and audio. The measurement device for the heart rate-sensing clothes included a Bluetooth SMART heart rate transmit module (CARDIOsport Heart Rate Transmitter TP3) with satisfactory reliability and validity (Chiang et al., 2020).

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (Br Med J 1964;ii:177) and was approved by the medical research ethics committee. All participants who provided written informed consent were assured their participation was entirely voluntary.

## Measures

Data including sociodemographics, comorbidities, and treatment with β-blocker were collected using structured interviews and medical records. Outcome data including PA, cardiorespiratory fitness, and heart rate recovery were collected at baseline and 12 weeks.

## Physical activity

This study evaluated PA using the well-validated and reliable Taiwanese version of the 7-item International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ; Craig et al., 2003; Liou et al., 2008). This tool measures the self-reported past 7-day time (minutes) spent engaged in housework, transportation, walking behavior, sitting behavior, leisure activity, moderate-intensity PA, and vigorous-intensity PA. The IPAQ scores, presented as metabolic equivalent of task (MET)- minutes/week, were calculated as the MET level based on the PA types, including walking (3.3 METs), moderate-intensity PA (4.0 METs), and vigorous-intensity PA (8.0 METs; Liou et al., 2008). The total amount of PA was the sum of walking behavior, moderateintensity PA, and vigorous-intensity PA. Participants with more sitting behavior, presenting as weekly sitting time (min/week), indicated having a more sedentary lifestyle.

## Cardiorespiratory fitness

Participants' cardiorespiratory fitness, presented as peak oxygen uptake ( \mathsf { V O } _ { 2 \mathsf { p e a k } } ,ml/kg/min), workload (Watt), anaerobic threshold (AT, ml/kg/min), \mathsf { O } _ { 2 }pulse (ml/beat), resting heart rate (bpm), resting systolic blood pressure [SBP, mmHg], resting diastolic blood pressure [DBP, mmHg], and lung function test parameters (forced vital capacity [FVC, L/min], forced expiratory volume in 1 s [FEV1, L/min], and FEV1/FVC, %), were measured during the graded exercise testing. Participants' oxygen and carbon dioxide concentrations in both inhaled and exhaled air were analyzed to detect oxygen consumption via arterial–venous oxygen differences during the graded exercise testing. { \mathsf { V O } } _ { 2 { \mathsf { p e a k } } } ,calculated by the maximum amount of blood the heart pumps per minute and the amount of oxygen utilization, was determined when oxygen consumption remained at a steady state despite an increase in intensity (ACSM, 2017). A higher { \mathsf { V O } } _ { 2 \mathsf { p e a k } }indicated better cardiorespiratory fitness.

Workload (Watt), the amount of work that an individual could do within a particular period, was also estimated by tolerance of speed and resistance from the cycle ergometer during the graded exercise testing. During exercise testing, oxygen consumption, in which aerobic energy production is supplemented by anaerobic mechanisms, causing a sustained increase in lactate and metabolic acidosis, is termed the anaerobic threshold (AT). Therefore, AT represented individuals' aerobic exercise capacity. \mathsf { O } _ { 2 }pulse, the volume of oxygen consumed by the body per heartbeat, provided an estimation of stroke volume value. A higher workload, AT, and \mathrm { ~ O ~ } _ { 2 }pulse indicated better cardiorespiratory function. Additionally, resting heart rate, resting SBP, and resting DBP were evaluated at the beginning of the graded testing when the participant had been seated quietly for 3–5 min, using an electronic blood pressure monitor device (Terumo, ESP2000). Regarding lung function test parameters, airflow obstruction was defined as a reduced FEV1 and a reduced FEV1/FVC ratio. When patients have an FEV1/ FVC < 70% and 5 0 \% < \mathsf { F E V } 1 < 8 0 \%, a worsening airflow limitation is indicated (ACSM, 2017).

## Heart rate recovery

Heart rate at 1 or 2 min of recovery after exercise has been validated as a prognostic determinant in mortality prediction (Cole et al., 1999). Both the 1- and 2-min heart rate recovery (beat) were evaluated after the graded exercise testing, which lasted 6–12 min to gradually reach the { \mathsf { V O } } _ { 2 \mathsf { p e a k } }and maximum heart rate (maximum intensity) through a motorized ergometer in this study. Immediately after participants stopped the graded exercise testing (baseline) and 1 and 2 min later, the heart rate was checked, and the differences from baseline were calculated. Therefore, heart rate recovery was measured at 1 and 2-min intervals and presented as 1- and 2-min heart rate recovery (ACSM, 2017). A decrease of <14 and < 22 beats/min in 1- or 2-min heart rate

recovery predicts an increased risk of mortality, and the lower 1- or 2-min heart rate recovery a person experiences, the higher the risk of mortality (Shetler et al., 2001).

## Data analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 16.0. Descriptive statistics are presented as means with standard deviations (SD) and numbers with percentages (%) for the study participants. Data imputation of the missing data was handled with the last-observation-carried-forward method, using the baseline observed value of the outcome variable as the subsequently missing observation values if a participant withdrew before the end of the study, for intent-to-treat analysis. A paired t-test was applied to examine the differences between pre- and post-tests. Student's t- and chi-squared tests were used to compare the pre- and postintervention differences between groups. The standardized meandifference effect size (d), designed for contrasting two groups on a continuous variable or estimated based on a dichotomous variable, was presented at the pre- and post-intervention differences between groups. Analysis of covariance was used to evaluate the post-intervention differences between groups, whereas statistically controlling for the covariates (the baseline value), which was

significantly different between groups. Generalized estimating equations (GEE), used to test hypotheses regarding the influence of factors on binary and other exponentially distributed response variables collected within subjects across time for longitudinal data and repeat measures, were applied to appraise the intervention effects of the two groups by significant interaction of group and time (group × time; Liang & Zeger, 1986). When evaluating the intervention effects, the models were adjusted for the significant sociodemographic covariates, characteristics of comorbidity, and treatment with β-blocker from univariate analysis. All the statistical analyses were two-tailed and considered significant at p < .05.

# RESULTS
## Baseline characteristics of participants

One hundred patients were initially approached. Of these, 11 participants declined to participate, and six patients did not meet the inclusion criteria. Of the remaining 83 participants, 68 (81.9%) completed all data collection, three dropped out due to unavailability, and 12 (EG: 7; CG: 5) did not return to complete the post-test (Figure 1). In the EG, 39 participants (92.8%) completed all 36 exercise sessions with an adherence rate of 100%. Eighty-three participants were

included in the data analysis. Sociodemographic and comorbidity characteristics of the participants are presented in Table 1. Most sociodemographic data and characteristics of comorbidity in the EG were not different when compared to the CG, except for the age, gender, diagnosis of heart disease and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and treatment with β-blockers.
