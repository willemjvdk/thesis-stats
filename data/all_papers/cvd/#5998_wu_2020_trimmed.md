# Methods
## Participants

This study was a two-arm parallel and single blind randomized controlled trial. This study has been registered in the China Clinical Trial Center and the registration number is ChiCTR1800018934. We included adult stroke patients who were treated in the Department of Neurology in the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University from December 2016 to December 2017.Inclusion criteria were: (1) 18-80 years old; (2) diagnosis of ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke,according to the Chinese Medical Association Neurology Branch in 2014; (3) were confirmed by CT and/or MRI scans;18 (4) neurological deficit degree score (NIHSS) 5-15 points; (5) limb dysfunction, Brunnstrom function stage II-III; (6) Choose home recuperation after discharge; the ability of caregivers - 40 points according to the caregiver capacity scale; (7) get informed consent. Exclusion criteria were: (1) serious heart, liver, lung, kidney and other organ diseases; (2) severe cognitive disabilities or mental disorder; (3) other diseases that affect motor function, such as osteoarthrosis; (4) complete aphasia; (5) legal blindness or serious visual impairment.

## Interventions

## Control group

During the hospitalization, the patients in the control group received routine early rehabilitation guidance and routine nursing measures. The main contents were the normal limb position, bed position transfer, and joint activity maintenance training. After discharge, Patients in the control group received only routine rehabilitation and nursing measures, including dietary guidance, medication guidance, and rehabilitation guidance, which were conducted by telephone follow-up once a week. Patients can go to the rehabilitation clinic to get rehabilitation instructions as needed.

## Intervention group

Patients in the intervention group received the same intervention as the control group during hospitalization. After discharge, patients in the intervention group received home remote rehabilitation based on a collaborative care model. A collaborative care team consisting of neurologists, nurses, rehabilitation therapists, counselors, and caregivers was established. Rehabilitation

therapists assess the extent of patient dysfunction and work with family caregivers to develop rehabilitation plans and goals. The home remote rehabilitation guidance uses the Internet-based TCMeeting v6.0 video conferencing system. The system consists of a computer, a projector, a camera, and a data storage system. The patient installs the system on a computer at home, and the rehabilitation engineer and rehabilitation nurse perform a personalized remote rehabilitation instruction twice a week. The intervention was as showed in Table 1.

## Data collection

Assessed by two rehabilitation therapists who were blinded to the grouping. Before starting the study, the Fugl-Meyer Assessment, Berg Balance Scale and Timed “UP & GO” test were tested and the evaluator was trained for 3 days. The main investigator who has more than 10 years of experience in these assessments evaluated the assessor's assessment ability. The training evaluator is assessed according to the standardized assessment process of the assessment tool, and in future studies it is recommended that the subject not provide the assessor with a means of rehabilitation. Changes in clinical functional performance and motor control ability were assessed at 4 weeks, 8 weeks, and 12 weeks after enrollment of the subjects using FMA and BBS. This study used a single blind approach by the evaluator, but blinding was not possible to intervention practitioners and participants in this study.

Outcome measures

## Fugl-Meyer Motor Function Assessment

FMA is a cumulative quantitative evaluation method designed by Swedish scholar Fugl-Meyer to assess sensorimotor function in stroke patients.21 The assessment includes limb movement, balance, sensation, joint mobility and pain. The Fugl-Meyer motor function rating scale is based on Brunnstrom's theory of limb function recovery,22 and is evaluated sequentially from the flaccid phase, the stagnation period, the combined movement period, the partial separation period, and the separation movement period. Each project is divided into three levels, which are 0 points (not completed), 1 point (partially completed), and 2 points (fully completed). The total score of the upper limbs is 66 points, and the total score of the lower limbs is 34 points, out of 100 points. The Fugl-Meyer Motor Function Assessment Scale has a high degree of inter-rater and assessor internal reliability and structural validity, and is the preferred scale for assessing post-stroke motor dysfunction in clinical and scientific research.23

## Berg Balance Scale

Berg Balance Scale first reported by Katherine Berg in 1989,24 consists of 14 projects, standing up, sitting down, standing alone, holding eyes closed, stretching the upper arms, turning around for a week, alternate steps on both feet, standing on one leg, each The minimum score is 0, the highest is 4, and the total score is 56. The high score indicates that the balance function is good. The result can predict the occurrence of adverse events such as falls, and scores below 40 points, indicating a risk of falling. BBS is currently the most commonly used balance scale in clinical practice. Relevant research shows that the intra-group reliability and inter-group reliability of BBS in assessing the balance function of patients with cerebrovascular accident are above 0.96.25

## The Timed “UP & GO” test (TUG)

The Timed “UP & GO” test (TUG) is an objective method for measuring basic mobility and balancing actions,26 the test procedure is for the subject to stand up from the chair and move forward by 3 meters. Turn around and walk back to the seat of his chair; the subject wears usual shoes and allows the use of a walker if needed. Allow to practice once before testing. The result is recorded in seconds, and if it exceeds 20 seconds, it indicates that there is a movement obstacle. The test is quick and easy, the results are objective, no special equipment is required, just the right chair, stopwatch or watch.27

## The 6-minute walking test (6MWT)

The 6-minute walking test (6MWT) as a sub-maximum test method, means that the patient completes the test in a stable state at a rhythm he can master, unlike the maximum load test.28 The test is characterized by high safety and simple operation. In a review of functional walk tests, 6MWT is better tolerated than other walk test tests and more reflective of daily activities.29 As a functional walk test, it reflects the level of walking endurance and practical walking in stroke patients.30

## Modified Barthel Index Scale1

Modified Barthel Index (MBI). The activities of daily living (ADL), which is the activity that must be carried out to meet daily life, reflects the degree of self-care ability of patients, and is often evaluated by the index.31 The Chinese version of MBI was translated and tried by scholars such as Leung and showed high reliability and validity in stroke patients. The evaluation included ten items including eating, personal hygiene, dressing, bathing, toileting, toilet control, transfer, walking, and going up and down the stairs, using a likert 5 rating, totaling 100 points. -20 points means that life is completely dependent; 21-40 points means severe dysfunction, life dependence is obvious; 41-59 points means moderate dysfunction, life needs help; 60 points, indicating that life can be self-care.32

## The Stroke-Specific Quality of Life Scale

The Stroke-Specific Quality of Life Scale(SS-QOL) was developed in 1999 by Williams.33 The scale is highly targeted, easy to use, and has high reliability and validity and snsitivity. SS-QOL includes 12 aspects of physical fitness, family activities, language, exercise, emotion, personality, self-care ability, social activities, thinking ability, upper limb function, vision and work ability, and comprehensively evaluated the quality of life of stroke patients. Wang Yi long improved and revised the SS-QOL scale, and conducted reliability, validity and sensitivity studies in the Chinese population, and concluded that SS-QOL has good reliability, validity and sensitivity.34 That can be used to measure stroke outcomes have become a specialized scale for evaluating the quality of life of stroke patients.

## Randomization

Randomization was conducted after baseline assessment. The patients were allocated in a 1:1 ratio into intervention group(n=32) and control group(n=32). The randomization list was generated using a computer randomizer (https:// www.randomizer.org). The random number is managed by the head nurse and can only be accessed by entering a specific password. The participants are screened and enrolled by the rehabilitation therapist, while another research nurse assigned participants to interventions.

## Statistical analysis

We used Epidata3.1 software to build a database and SPSS20.0 software to process data. The measurement data obtained in this study were analyzed by Chi-square test; the results of multiple measurements were analyzed by two-factor repeated measures analysis of variance; the two-sample t-test was used for comparison between the two groups, and the Bonferroni method was used for comparison between groups; P<0.05 indicates that the difference was statistically significant.

The interaction test of time factor and grouping factor (F=65.135, P<0.001) indicating that there was interaction between them.

Independent sample t-test was used to compare the measurement values of each dimension between the two groups at each time point. The results showed that there were significant differences in family role, language, activity ability, self-care ability, social role and upper extremity function between the two groups at the 4th, 8th and 12th week after intervention (P<0.05). There was no significant difference in personality, thinking and vision between the two groups at the 4th, 8th and 12th week after discharge (P>0.05). There was no significant difference in energy and work between the two groups at the 4th week (P>0.05), but there was significant difference at the 8th and 12th week after discharge (P<0.05). There was no significant difference in emotional dimension between the two groups at the 8th week after discharge (P>0.05), but there was significant difference at the 4th week and the 12th week after discharge (P<0.05).
