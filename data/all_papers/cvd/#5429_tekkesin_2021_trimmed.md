# 2. Materials and methods
## 2.1. Study design and organisation

Lifestyle intervention using mobile technology in patients with high cardiovascular risk: The pragmatic randomised clinical trial (LIGHT) was a single-centre trial conducted at the Dr. Siyami Ersek Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery Training and Research Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey. The study protocol complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and it was approved by the local ethics committee of the Haydarpas¸a Numune Training and Research Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey (approval no. 2017/78), and was registered with clinicaltrials.gov (NCT03397849). The study design has been described previously [7].

The trial was sponsored by Türk Telekomunikasyon A.S¸ . The sponsor was only involved in the device supply and not in the study design, protocol, data acquisition and analysis.

## 2.2. Study population

The eligibility criteria required the participants to be between 20 and 79 years of age and at high risk for CVDs, as indicated by a 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk score ≥7.5%.

Patients with an active infection, inflammatory disease (active inflammatory bowel diseases and active rheumatologic diseases such as vasculitis, rheumatoid arthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus), a prior CVD event (myocardial infarction, percutaneous coronary intervention, coronary artery bypass graft operation, stroke, or peripheral artery disease), pregnancy, communication difficulties, severe neuropsychiatric problems, and chronic kidney disease were excluded from the study. Patients who were under anti-inflammatory treatment, antibiotherapy, and/or statin therapy were also excluded. Finally, patients who were unable to use a smart phone were excluded. The patients were recruited from an office visit. All the participants provided written informed consent.

## 2.3. Study procedures

The participants were randomly allocated to either the intervention plus usual care or the usual care arm. Randomisation was conducted independently using a central computer-based randomisation service. The randomisation sequence was generated by study statisticians using a computer software to select randomly permuted blocks of four to six patients. Given the nature of the intervention, the patients were aware of their respective allocations.

The participants randomised to the intervention group received a set of smart devices, namely a smart phone, wristband, scale and blood pressure monitor (Fig. 1).

The participants entered daily data regarding their diet, blood pressure, weight and step count in an application (Mediup) compatible with smart phones. Moreover, motivational messages were delivered every day to direct patients towards a healthy lifestyle. The entered data were tracked from the main server, and automatic messages were sent to the non-compliant patients in order to invite them for an outpatient visit should specific conditions occur according to the pre-specified design criteria [7].

## 2.4. Study endpoints

The primary outcome of this study was the 1-year ASCVD risk score adjusted to the baseline ASCVD risk score. The ASCVD risk scores were calculated for every study participant at the randomisation and final (1- year final visit) stages.

Secondary outcomes were evaluated in the clinical visits at baseline and 1-year visits. These outcomes included CVD risk factors, namely smoking cessation, blood pressure, weight/body mass index (BMI), blood lipids (lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c) and triglycerides) and glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) levels determined at 1 year and adjusted for their baseline values. The other secondary outcomes were quality of life, highsensitivity troponin (hs-troponin) and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels at 1 year adjusted for their baseline scores. In addition, all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, myocardial infarction, stroke, and hospitalisation for CVDs were evaluated as secondary outcomes.

## 2.5. Statistical analysis

The analysis was conducted according to the CONSORT guidelines [8]. A sample of 270 participants (135 per group) were required to detect a minimal clinically important difference with 90% power for a 2-sided type I error of 0.05. We defined the minimal difference as a small effect size (0.2). To account for an estimated 20% loss to follow-up, at least 160 participants were needed for recruitment into each group (amounting to a total of 320 participants).

Continuous baseline characteristics of the allocated participants were presented as the median and interquartile range, while categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was used to evaluate the primary and secondary outcomes (excluding smoking). The 1-year ASCVD score was determined as the dependent variable in ANCOVA. The baseline of the ASCVD score, age and gender were determined as the independent variables, and the treatment effect was considered to be the adjusted variable. The logistic regression method was used to analyse the smoking status at the final visit. The results were reported as the adjusted odds ratio (95% confidence interval (CI)). The simplified covariate-adjusted model (subgroups, baseline ASCVD, age, gender and treatment arms) without the interaction term was built (base model), following which, the interaction term was allowed between the subgroups. Baseline ASCVD, age, gender and treatment arms and treatment-covariate interaction adjusted model was built to test the heterogeneity in treatment effect of the primary endpoint (semi-saturated model). Both models were compared using the likelihood ratio test and the corresponding p values. Heterogeneity of the treatment effect was detected if the p value was less than 0.05. Multiplicity adjustment was not performed in either the secondary analysis or the subgroup analysis (heterogeneity of the treatment effect) due to the exploratory nature of this research. All the numerical variables were transformed by restricted cubic spline with four knots.

Analyses were performed using the open-source statistical environment R [9] with the package ‘rms’ for regression modeling [10] and ‘ggplot2’ for graph construction [11].

# 3. Results
## 3.1. Characteristics of the participants

Between February 2018 and February 2019, 540 participants with high cardiovascular risk were randomised to either the intervention plus usual care or the usual care groups. Fifty-nine patients were excluded from the analysis due to withdrawal, loss to follow-up or discontinuation. In the final analysis, 483 patients were enrolled in the intervention plus usual care or the usual care arms (Fig. 2).

The baseline characteristics of the study groups are summarised in Table 1. The median age of the study population was 59 (53–64) years. A total of 235 participants (48.7%) were female, 227 (47%) had diabetes mellitus, 234 (71.4%) had hypertension, 102 (21.1%) had hyperlipid emia and 213 (44.1%) were active smokers. The baseline ASCVD score was 14.8 (9.8–20.9). The medication statuses at baseline and 1-year visits are summarised in Table 2.

## 3.2. Endpoint analyses

## 3.2.1. Primary endpoint

In the intervention plus usual care arm, the baseline ASCVD score was 14.6% (9.5–21.2) and the 1-year ASCVD score was 13.4% (8.6–18.2). In the usual care arm, the corresponding values were 15.1%

(9.8–20.8) and 16.1% (10.8–22.7), respectively. In the intervention plus usual care arm, the ASCVD score either remained unchanged or decreased for 185 (76.4%) patients. In the intervention arm, there was a significant decrease in ASCVD score at 1-year while in the usual care there was a significant increase in ASCVD at 1-year compared to baseline ASCVD. In the usual care arm, the ASCVD score either remained the same or decreased in 92 (38.2%) patients. The estimated treatment effect of intervention plus usual care over usual care on the 1-year ASCVD score according to regression modelling was 2.7% (adjusted treatment effect − 2.7, 95% CI -2.2, − 3.3, p<0.0001) (Fig. 3 and Table 3).

## 3.2.2. Secondary endpoints

Similarly to the primary endpoint, an improvement in favour of the intervention plus usual care arm was observed in all the pre-specified secondary endpoints at 1 year. The exclusions were hs-troponin and hs-CRP(CVD risk factors, namely smoking cessation, blood pressure, weight/BMI, blood lipids, glycated haemoglobin level and the World Health Organization Quality of Life (WHOQOL) scores). Hs-troponin and hs-CRP levels at 1 year were similar between the two arms. Allcause and cardiovascular mortality, myocardial infarction, stroke and hospitalisation for CVDs were detected in 2,1,0 and 9 participants, in the intervention plus usual care arm and 2,1,1, and 5 participants in the usual care arm, respectively.

## 3.3. Heterogeneity of the treatment effect

Considering treatment-covariate interaction in the covariate adjustment, the treatment effect was homogenous for diabetes mellitus, gender, systolic blood pressure, LDL, HDL, triglycerides and BMI. However, the heterogeneity of treatment effect was noticed for age (Fig. 4).
