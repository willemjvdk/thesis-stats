## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
Full details on the design and results of the mAFA-II trial have been previously published elsewhere [14, 21]. Briefly, the mAFA-II was a two-arm, prospective, cluster randomised trial, conducted in China, that enrolled AF patients 18 years across 40 participating centres between 1 June 2018 and 16 August 2019. All patients enrolled had AF confirmed with a 12-lead ECG or 24-hour Holter monitor, and a CHA DS -VASc score 2. Patients with mechanical prosthetic valves, those with moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis and those unable to be followed up for 1 year for any reason or unable to provide informed consent were excluded. For this ancillary analysis of the trial, we included AF patients who were aged ≥75 years at baseline. The study was approved by the Central Medical Ethic Committee of Chinese PLA General Hospital and by local institutional review boards and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guideline; all patients gave written informed consent. The study was registered on the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (registration number: ChiCTR-OOC-17014138).

## Randomisation and procedures

Participating centres were randomised in a 1:1 ratio to the mAFA intervention or usual care clusters. Clusters were identified based on hospital size, patients’ volume,

doctors–patients interaction time after discharge, patients’ smartphone usage and general education level of the patient population. Randomisation procedure was performed with a computer-generated randomisation list and based mainly on hospital size and proportion of enrolled patients. The study followed an open-label design. All physicians from the sites randomised to the mAFA intervention received specific training about the app usage.

## Intervention and implementation of ABC pathway

The mAFA platform provides clinical decision tools to physicians to support guideline-based treatment implementation, as well as educational materials and self-care protocols for patients. The implementation of the ABC pathway through the mHealth technology in the mAFA intervention group was defined as follows, in accordance with the primary analysis of the trial: the ‘A’ criterion was attained through the administration of anticoagulant according to regular and dynamic assessment of thromboembolic and bleeding risk, with dose adjustment also according to renal and liver function reassessment. The ‘B’ criterion consisted in the regular monitoring of patient-reported symptoms (which were evaluated according to the European Heart Rhythm Association classification), along with symptoms-directed management that included antiarrhythmics and rhythm control therapies. Finally, the ‘C’ criterion was pursued through proactive management and treatment optimization of concurrent comorbidities (e.g. hypertension management according to blood pressure monitoring). Conversely, patients allocated to ‘usual care’ were managed according to local practices.

# Outcomes and follow-up
All patients were followed up for the occurrence of clinical outcomes at 6 and 12 months after the inclusion. The primary endpoint, consistently with the primary trial’s analysis, was the composite of ischemic stroke or systemic thromboembolism, all-cause death and rehospitalisation. Other endpoints investigated were thromboembolism (including ischemic stroke and other systemic thromboembolism), bleeding events (intracranial and extracranial), cardiovascular outcomes (recurrent AF, heart failure, acute coronary syndrome), all-cause death and rehospitalisation.

## Secondary analyses

We performed two secondary analyses. In the first, we evaluated the effect of the mAFA intervention using different cut-offs of age to define older patients (aged ≥60 years, 65 years, 70 years, 80 years). In the second, using the whole population enrolled in the mAFA-II trial, we evaluated the interaction between age as a continuous variable and the effect of mAFA intervention.

## Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics for the primary analysis population were reported as mean and standard deviation (SD) or

median and interquartile range [IQR] for normally and non-normally distributed continuous variables, respectively; frequencies and percentages were reported for categorical variables.

Cox proportional hazard models were used to evaluate the effect of mAFA intervention on the outcomes investigated. All models were adjusted for sex, type of AF, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease (CAD), history of heart failure, history of stroke), previous AF treatment and cluster effect. Subgroup analyses for the primary analysis population were performed according to sex, baseline thromboembolic and bleeding risk (as encompassed by CHA2DS2- VASc and HAS-BLED scores, respectively), history of prior thromboembolism, hypertension, CAD, congestive heart failure, diabetes mellitus, renal dysfunction and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

A two-sided P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using R 4.1.2.

# Results
Among the 3,324 patients enrolled in the trial, there were 1,163 patients (mean age: 82.6 5.3 years, 43.1% females) who were aged ≥75 years and were included in the present analysis (Figure 1). Of these, 520 (44.7%) were allocated to the mAFA intervention, with a median [IQR] followup of 395 [221–395] days, while 643 (55.3%) to the usual care group, with a median [IQR] follow-up of 340 [238– 395] days. Baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1, and treatments according to group allocation are reported in Supplementary Table 1.

Patients allocated to mAFA intervention showed a statistically significant reduction of the risk of the primary composite outcome of stroke or thromboembolism, all-cause death and rehospitalisation (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR]: 0.58, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.35–0.97, P 0.039). Patients in the mAFA intervention also showed a reduced risk of rehospitalisation (aHR: 0.47, 95% CI: 0.24–0.91, P  0.026), while no statistically significant differences were observed for the other secondary outcomes, including thromboembolism, bleeding, other cardiovascular events and all-cause death (Table 2).

## Subgroup analyses

The results of the subgroup analysis by sex, risk scores and baseline comorbidities are reported in Figure 2A and B for the primary composite outcome and rehospitalisation, respectively. A significant interaction was observed for both outcomes (the primary composite outcome, and rehospitalisation) according to CHA2DS2-VASc ( 3 versus 3 or more) and HAS-BLED (0–2 versus 3 or more) scores, history of thromboembolism and renal dysfunction, and for the composite outcome according to the history of hypertension. No significant interactions were observed for the other subgroups investigated.

In the other secondary analysis, performed on the whole trial population, we modelled the interaction between age (as a linear continuous variable) and mAFA intervention on the risk of the composite outcome and rehospitalisation alone

after the adjustment for baseline risk factors. The results of this analysis are shown in Figure 3 (panels A and B, for the composite outcome and rehospitalisation, respectively). A significant interaction between age and mAFA allocation group was observed for both the composite outcome (P 0.002) and rehospitalisation alone (P 0.015). Regression curves showed the beneficial effect of the mAFA intervention decreasing with increasingly higher age, particularly at age 80 years old.
