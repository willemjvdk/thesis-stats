# Method
## Study Design

The TELEREH-HF study was a prospective randomized controlled trial (Clinical Trials.gov NCT 02523560), which consisted of 5 centers in Poland, The study compared HCTR added to UC with only UC in 850 clinically stable HF participants [New York Heart Association (NYHA) class I, II or III] Other inclusion criteria were left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤ 40%, and hospitalization due to worsening HF within 6 months prior to randomization. A detailed description of the TELEREH-HF study design, inclusion,

and exclusion criteria, as well as main results were published in previous research papers (Piotrowicz et al., 2019, 2020b).

## Intervention

Patients randomized to the intervention group were ofered a 9-week HCTR program, which comprised a hospital stage (1 week exercise training and education) and a home-based HCTR (8 weeks). The intervention consisted of telecare, with individualized home telerehabilitation and remote monitoring of CIEDs. HCTR was supervised by experienced medical staf (physicians, physiotherapists, nurses, and psychologists). Patients randomized to the UC group underwent baseline clinical examinations during 3 days hospitalization and then were under observation until the end of the 9th week and received usual care appropriate for their clinical status and standardized within a particular center. After the 9th week, patients underwent fnal assessments during a 3-day hospitalization. All patients, regardless of the treatment group, received recommendations for suitable lifestyle changes and self-management according to guidelines.

The comprehensive cardiac telerehabilitation consisted of exercise training planned according to current ESC guidelines for heart failure, tailored individually for each patient during hospitalization. The telerehabilitation program encompasses three training modalities: endurance aerobic Nordic walking training, respiratory muscle training, and light resistance and strength exercises. Education program was designed and run by the telerehabilitation team. Patients were taught how to self-evaluate, how to measure heart rate heart rate, blood pressure, body mass, how to perform all modalities of exercise training, how to evaluate the level of perceived exertion according to the Borg scale, and how to operate a telerehabilitation set. Education also encompassed nutritional counseling, lipid management, smoking cessation, vocational and psychosocial support. A detailed description of the intervention and the devices has been published previously (Piotrowicz et al., 2019, 2020b).

Each patient provided written informed consent and the study was approved by the local ethics committee (IK-NP-0021-85/1402/13). The conduct of the trial was guided by good clinical practice, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the applicable regulations in Poland.

All patients underwent evaluations at entry and after completing the 9-week training program (HCTR group) or completing the 9-week observation (UC group). It consisted of clinical and psychological examinations, including the assessment of HR-QoL, depressive symptoms, and anxiety.

## Psychological Assessment and Intervention

All patients (from the HCTR and UC group) obtained a standard psychological evaluation on overall psychosocial

functioning and adjustment to treatment using a clinical interview and self-assessment (questionnaires regarding HR-QoL, depression, and anxiety). They were also ofered psychological support (1–2 counseling sessions) during the initial hospitalization with the focus on the adaptation to illness and self-care. The initial psychological assessment allowed to identify patients from the HCTR group who were referred to additional psychological support. The HCTR patients who met the selection criteria, based on clinical evaluation, and the presence of symptoms of depression and anxiety assessed with psychological questionnaires (cutof points: Beck Depression Inventory II (BDI-II) ≥ 14 points, Spielberger State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) ≥ 7 sten), were referred to additional psychological telesupport. These patients were ofered psychological intervention via mobile phone during a 9-week telerehabilitation program.

The psychological support for HCTR patients (tele-psychology) had form of supportive one-to-one psychological counseling sessions via mobile phone according to the schedule established with patients (usually once a week). Since patients in HCTR group were equipped with a mobile phone, which they used to contact the telerehabilitation team, psychologists contacted this number according to the patients’ training schedule and agreed setting. Telepsychology sessions were performed by experienced psychologist or clinical psychologist working in the cardiac department in each of 5 medical centers involved in conducting the study. Psychological interventions were aimed at patients’ experience with HCTR training, enhancing patients’ motivation to exercise and coping with emotional problems that were reported by patients during the frst assessment or occurred during the telerehabilitation process. Interventions were based on cognitive-behavioral paradigm, as it is recommended for patients with somatic illness and comorbid psychological problems, and used strategies from Motivational Interviewing, as it was proven to be efective form of support for heart failure patients (Halford & Brown, 2009; Orzechowska et al., 2021; Poudel et al., 2020; Rebora et al., 2021; Sakane et al., 2021; Vellone et al., 2020). Patients from the UC group received standard care appropriate for each center.

## Measures

## Assessment of the Anxiety Level

To assess anxiety, the Polish version of the Spielberger State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) was used. It was chosen because of the availability of reliable Polish adaptation and norms for the individual assessment, as well as being widely used in studies regarding anxiety in cardiac patients. The STAI questionnaire consists of 40 items in two separated subscales: X1 scale is used to assess anxiety as a state

(intensity in response to specifc external stimuli), whereas X2 scale assesses anxiety as a trait (a relatively constant personality trait). State anxiety is characterized by subjective, consciously perceived feelings of fear and tension accompanied by stimulation of the autonomic nervous system. It is easily changed under the infuence of various types of threatening factors. Trait anxiety is described as a theoretical construct, which represents a motive or an acquired disposition in behavior that makes a person susceptible to perceiving objectively harmless situations as threatening and reacting to them with anxiety. Those states of anxiety are disproportionately strong compared to the size of the objective danger.

The patient’s task is to respond to the list of statements by indicating the number on a scale from 1 to 4, which best describes their subjective feelings “at this particular moment” (for X1 subscale) or “usually” (for X2 subscale). For a reliable interpretation of the results, the raw scores are normalized and converted to the 10-unit sten scale. The sten norms in STAI refer to the sex and the age of the respondents. The results in the 1–4 sten range are considered low, the 5–6 range—average, and scores within the 7–10 range are interpreted as high (Wrześniewski et al., 2011).

## Assessment of Physical Capacity: Cardiopulmonary Exercise Treadmill Test

The symptom-limited CPET on a treadmill was performed according to a ramp protocol and ESC guidelines (Balady et al., 2004; Piepoli et al., 2006). Obtained peak \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 }value was presented per kilogram of body mass per minute (ml/ kg/min).

## Statistical Analyses

The sample size was calculated assuming 1:1 treatment allocation ratio and an overall two-sided level of signifcance of 0.05. Assuming a diference of the 21 days alive and out of hospital between study arms, with a standard deviation of 100, a sample size of 400 evaluable subjects per study arm (a total of 800) would be required to achieve 80% power. Due to an assumed 5–6% loss to follow-up, the total number increased to 850.

Subgroup analyses focused on the diference between the study arms in the level of anxiety as a state (X1) and a trait (X2). Unless otherwise stated, continuous variables are expressed as \mathrm { m e a n } \pm \mathrm { S D }and nominal or ordinal variables as counts and percentages. Two-sample comparison were performed using Student’s t test, the \chi ^ { 2 }test of independence, Fisher’s exact test or Cochran-Mantel–Haenszel test, as appropriate. Within-group comparisons were conducted using paired Student’s test (quantitative variables) or Bowker’s test of symmetry (nominal variables). In addition, the strength of interrater agreement of sten score was expressed

by the simple Cohen’s kappa coefcient with 95% confdence intervals and compared between groups with Qk test (Kappa test for agreement between two raters).

A two-tailed value of p < . 0 5was considered statistically signifcant. All analyses were performed using SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

# Results
The study group consisted of 850 patients with HF (of 2333 screened), with a 1:1 HCTR-UC ratio, so 425 participants were assigned to the HCTR group and 425 to UC group. All patients were able to fnish the program, there were no patients who withdrew from the study. The most common reasons for dropping out of telerehabilitation were technical difculties, comorbidities, and time limitations (related to returning to work). The baseline clinical characteristics of both groups are presented in Table 1.

At the stage of the initial psychological assessment, 153 patients indicated that they required psychological support—73 patients from the HCTR group (19.4%) and 80 patients from the UC group (20.5%) \left( { p = . 7 0 4 } \right). 34 (75.5%) of patients from the HCTR group who consented to additional psychological care during the initial evaluation agreed to participate in psychological telesupport via mobile phone during the 9-week HCTR program. Eleven patients could not participate or withdrew from a supportive psychological session during the program due to severe cognitive impairment or technical difculties. Between 1 and 9 teleconsultations per patient during home telerehabilitation were performed. Most of the patients preferred to receive psychological telesupport at least once every 2 weeks; 24 participants (70.59% of patients who received psychological counseling via mobile phone) underwent 5 to 9 teleconsultations during the 9-week program and 10 patients preferred less frequent teleconsultations (1 to 4 phone calls from the psychologist during the telerehabilitation program).
