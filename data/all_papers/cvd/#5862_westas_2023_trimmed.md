## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
## Design and study participants

This study was a secondary analysis of data that were collected in a randomized controlled trial (RCT) that primarily aimed to evaluate the effect of a 9-week iCBT programme on depressive symptoms in patients with CVD. The study, including the recruitment process, has been described in detail elsewhere.21 In brief, the CVD patients were recruited from January 2017 to February 2018. Invitations (n = 11 992) were sent by post to all patients with at least one diagnosis of atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter, coronary heart disease, and heart failure, and at least one outpatient visit or hospitalization at the medical and cardiology clinics at four hospitals in south-eastern Sweden during the previous year.

Patients who were interested in participating were instructed to register on the study website (n = 272) and complete a web-based screening form. To be included, the patients had to be at least 18 years old, receiving CVD treatment, having a stable CVD (NYHA classes I–III), no hospitalization related to CVD in the past 4 weeks, and at least mild depressive symptoms [Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) score ≥523]. A total of 144 CVD patients with at least mild depressive symptoms were included

and randomized to either 9 weeks of iCBT (n = 72) or ODF (n = 72). The regional ethical review board in Linköping, Sweden, approved the study (ref. no. 2016/72-31), and the study was registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT02778074).

## The intervention

The iCBT programme has been described in detail previously, and the main findings have been published.21 The iCBT programme was developed to target depression in CVD patients and consisted of seven modules. All texts, videos, and figures in the modules were adapted to fit the context of CVD. The programme also included weekly homework assignments with written feedback provided by nurses with experience in cardiac care and a short course in iCBT. The feedback was based on affirmation, encouragement, and self-disclosure.24 Before being used in the RCT, the programme had been developed together with a patient representing a cardiac patient organization and evaluated in proof of concept and pilot studies. 25,26

We used an active control group as the comparator (i.e. ODF), which participated in a web-based moderated discussion forum over the 9-week treatment period. After 9 weeks, the participants in the ODF were offered the iCBT programme, and the randomization was thereby also broken after the 9-week study period.

## Data collection

Data for the present analysis were collected at baseline and at postintervention (9 weeks) at the end of the treatment period (i.e. short-term), and at 6- and 12-month follow-up (i.e. long-term). All data were collected using questionnaires on the study website.27

## Depression

The PHQ-9 was used to measure symptoms of depression.28 The questionnaire consists of nine items and assesses the frequency and severity of depression in the previous 14 days. The PHQ-9 has been used for measuring depression in the general population,23 as well as in patients with CVD,29 and is reported to be valid in a computerized format.30 Cronbach’s Alpha in this study was 0.78, indicating good reliability.

## Symptoms of a distress disorder

The GAD Questionnaire-7 item scale31 measures GAD and was used to measure symptoms of distress disorder. This questionnaire consists of seven items reflecting the symptoms of GAD according to the Diagnostic Statistical Manual-IV. Higher scores reflect more symptoms of GAD, and a score ≥5 suggests at least mild GAD.31 The GAD-7 is considered a valid reliable instrument for measuring GAD.32 In this study, Cronbach’s Alpha was 0.88, which indicates good reliability.

## Symptoms of a fear disorder

The Cardiac Anxiety Questionnaire (CAQ)8 was used to measure symptoms of fear disorder. The questionnaire consists of 18 items that cover fears and worries about heart disease, chest pain, heart-focused attention, heart-related sensations, and avoidance of activities believed to provoke symptoms from the heart.8 The CAQ can be categorized into a total scale and three subscales, i.e. fear, avoidance, and attention. The CAQ has been shown to have good psychometric properties.33 The Cronbach Alpha in this study was 0.85 for the CAQ total scale.

## Statistical analyses

Categorical variables on background characteristics are presented as proportions and continuous variables as means ± standard deviations. The chisquare test was used for categorical data. Continuous data were analysed using independent samples t-test.

The analyses regarding short- and long-term effects were carried out on an intention-to-treat basis. In all analyses, the CAQ was handled as a total score as well as three subscale scores (i.e. fear, avoidance, and attention). Both GAD-7 and CAQ met the assumptions of normality. In the evaluation of the short-term effects, we compared the mean difference in the GAD-7 and CAQ scores from baseline to the post-intervention between the

groups (i.e. iCBT vs. ODF). We used analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) since it allows for adjusting baseline scores and regression to the mean.34 The response rate was 88% (with a drop-out of n = 7 for iCBT and n = 10 for ODF) at post-intervention, and the analysis was therefore performed on original data.

The long-term effects (i.e. after 6- and 12-month follow-ups) were evaluated using linear mixed models (LMMs). Since the randomization was broken after the post-intervention and the ODF group was offered iCBT, the analysis was performed on the iCBT group only. The response rate for the PHQ-9, GAD-7, and CAQ at 12-month follow-up was 80%. LMM is a robust method when analysing repeated measures data with an ability to handle missing data.35 The LMM analysis was performed with an unstructured covariance type and estimations based on maximum likelihood, using time as fixed effect. Missing data from the dependent variables (i.e. GAD-7 and CAQ) met the assumption of missing at random (Little’s Missing Completely at Random test: chi-square = 79.01, df = 90, P = 0.79).

We also explored associations between changes in depression and changes in symptoms of distress and fear disorder. For this analysis, we performed a full factorial univariate analysis of variance (ANOVA) for GAD-7 and CAQ using PHQ-9 as a covariate and time (baseline, post-intervention, and 6- and 12-month follow-ups) as a fixed factor. The ANOVA tests how much one point of change in GAD-7 and CAQ corresponds to a change in PHQ-9 at the same time point. The magnitude of the associations was estimated using Pearson correlation coefficients, which were extracted by taking the square root of R-square from the respective ANOVA.

To assess the minimal clinical importance of the iCBT intervention, we estimated the numbers needed to treat (NNT) within the participants with elevated GAD symptoms ≥5 in both groups ODF and iCBT ( n = 4 3 / 4 8 ). The minimal clinically important changes in GAD were defined by a decrease of four points or more on the G A D - 7 ^ { 3 6 }between baseline and post-intervention.

# Results
## Study population

The demographic and clinical characteristics of participants are presented in Table 1. The mean age was 63 years (SD, 12), and 62% were men (n = 89). The most frequent CVD diagnosis was atrial fibrillation or flutter 45% (n = 65), followed by myocardial infarction or angina 34% (n = 49), and heart failure 26% (n = 38). Almost one-third (28%) of the participants had more than one CVD diagnosis, and the vast majority (80%) had at least one more comorbidity. A total of 63% had a score of five or more on GAD-7, indicating at least mild GAD. There were no statistically significant between-group differences (iCBT vs. ODF) in the characteristics or diagnoses (Table 1).

Those who did not complete the post-intervention (12%) did not statistically significantly differ regarding background characteristics, depression, or anxiety. Those with missing data in the iCBT group at the 12-month follow-up did not reveal any statistically significant differences in background characteristics or depression or symptoms of distress or fear disorder, except for heart failure, which was more common compared to those with no missing data (50% vs. 19%, P = 0.001),

## The short-term effect on symptoms of distress and fear disorder

The analysis of the short-term effects on symptoms of distress and fear disorder is reported in Table 2 and visualized in Figure 1. Symptoms of distress disorder, as measured using the GAD-7, showed a statistically significant improvement in the iCBT group compared to the ODF group [mean difference, −1.69, (95 CI −2.98 to −0.39), P = 0.011]. No short-term differences were found between iCBT and ODF regarding CAQ total (P = 0.18), CAQ fear (P = 0.71), and CAQ attention (P = 0.27). However, on the subscale CAQ avoidance, a statistically significant reduction was found with a mean difference of −0.23 (95 CI −0.46 to −0.004, P = 0.046) favouring the iCBT group.

## The long-term effect on symptoms of distress and fear disorder and their associations with changes in depression

The evaluation of the long-term effects in the iCBT group is presented in Table 3 and visualized in Figure 1. We found a statistically significant reduction in symptoms of distress disorder as measured using the GAD-7 between the 12-month follow-up and baseline (P = 0.001). There was no difference between 12-month follow-up and postintervention (P = 0.27), nor between 12- and 6-month follow-up (P = 0.64). Thus, most of the reduction in symptoms of distress occurred between baseline and post-intervention, and the reduction was sustained from post-intervention to 12-month follow-up.

Regarding symptoms of fear disorder, a statistically significant reduction in the CAQ total score was found between 12-month follow-up, post-intervention (P = 0.002), and baseline (P = 0.001), but there was no difference between 12- and 6-month follow-up (P = 0.35). Thus, most of the reduction in the CAQ total score occurred from baseline to 6-month follow-up. The same pattern was found in the CAQ subscale of fear, but the score was also statistically significantly reduced between 6- and 12-month follow-up ( P = 0 . 0 4 )). Thus, symptoms of fear decreased during the whole follow-up period. Regarding avoidance and attention, both were statistically significantly reduced between baseline and 12-month follow-up (P = 0.001 and P = 0.001). For both two subscales, there were no differences between 12-month follow-up and post-intervention (P = 0.80 respectively, P = 0.19) or between 12- and 6-month follow-up (P = 0.81, respectively, P = 0.71). Thus, most of the reductions in avoidance and attention occurred between baseline and post-intervention.

The correlation between overall change in distress disorder (i.e. GAD-7) and depression was r = 0.71 (P = 0.001). The correlations between the overall change in fear disorder and depression were as follows: CAQ total r = 0.49 (P = 0.001); CAQ fear r = 0.43 (P = 0.001); CAQ avoidance r = 0.40 (P = 0.001); CAQ attention r = 0.31 (P = 0.001).

In a subgroup analysis of participants with elevated GAD symptoms of ≥5 at baseline, the NNT for potential clinical impact on GAD-7 was 5. A decrease of 4 points or more on the GAD-7 in the ODF group was 16% (7 of 43) and 38% (18 of 48) in the iCBT at post-intervention.
