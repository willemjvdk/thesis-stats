# METHODS
## Study Design

This study was conducted at the Clinic and Polyclinic of the Department of Cardiology at a university hospital in Turkey between April 2018 and June 2019. This study was a prospective, randomized, controlled, single-blind experimental study. The study population included patients who were treated for heart failure and met the inclusion criteria. Patients were then categorized into control and intervention groups using randomization. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) having been treated for a diagnosis of heart failure in the Cardiology Clinic; (2) being class I, II, or III based on the New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification; (3) having Internet access and the ability to use a computer/tablet or smartphone; and (4) agreeing to participate in the study. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) having cancer or chronic obstructive lung disease; (2) having a visual or hearing impairment; (3) living outside the city; (4) having a functional NYHA class number IV; (5) having a planned surgical operation that requires long hospital stay; and (6) requesting to leave the follow-up. This study is based

on recommendations from the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials Statement18 (Figure 1). Before starting the research, permission was obtained from the university hospital. Ethics committee approval (approval no. 70904504/396-446) was obtained from the Ethics Committee for Clinical Research of the University. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant. The research conformed with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The trial was registered with the US National Institutes of Health Clinical Trials Registry under NCT04210167.

## Sample Size and Randomization

The most important factors affecting the power of a study are type I error, effect size, and sample size. It is reported that studies comparing averages between two independent groups should have at least 80% power, 5% type 1 error rate, and an effect size 0.5 and greater. The G*Power 3.1.9.2 software (Heinrich Heine University, Dusseldorf, Germany) was used to determine the sample size, which was found to be 64 patients. They were divided into two groups with 32 patients each, and the size was calculated

based on two-way hypothesis, medium effect size, 80% power, 0.05 margin of error, and 1:1 intervention-to-control ratio (http://www.gpower.hhu.de/, accessed June 16, 2017). However, considering the use of parametric tests in data assessment and possible sample loss, the optimal sample size was determined to be 70 patients in total, with 35 patients in each group.

As a randomization method, “simple randomization” was chosen to provide an equal number of samples in the two groups. Randomization into the intervention and control groups was done using a software on the Internet (https:// www.randomizer.org/, accessed December 16, 2017). Two sets (set 1 = intervention group, and set 2 = control group) were created in this software, and numbers from 1 to 70 were assigned to these sets randomly. Thus, starting from number 1 in the randomization list (eg, 1: intervention, 2: control, 3: control, etc.), up to 70 experimental and control group members were determined. The list of the patients was based on their date of hospitalization in the clinic (from distant date to near date). Patients who met the inclusion criteria in the study were interviewed, starting from the patient with the oldest hospitalization date in the list. Each participant declared in writing that they agreed to participate in the study. The participants did not know which group they were in, and the single-blind nature was provided. The patients were told there were two parallel groups followed, but no information was given about the interventions applied to the other group.

## The Intervention

## Preparation of the Web Site

The Web site was prepared to meet the informational needs of patients with heart failure and to assist them with managing their heart failure symptoms. The Web site contains disease information about heart failure, control of symptoms, and recommendations for lifestyle changes, health behaviors, and practices. Written texts, visuals prepared in picture format, shapes, and photographs were used as training material. Also, body mass index and a calorie-calculating engine were added to the Web site for the patients to assess their weight control. User and administrator interface pages were created. The Web site was accessible both from computer and smartphone.

## Telephone Follow-up

Telephone interviews were carried out at the 1st, 4th, 8th, and 12th weeks after discharge between 9:00 AM and 6:00 PM; if the patients had not been available for the initial call, they received another one as soon as possible. Patients could also contact the researcher whenever they needed. Telephone interviews were developed based on the structured telephone interview protocol form. Telephone follow-up and interview steps were included in this form prepared by the

researcher. The first section of the form evaluated the frequency and severity of possible symptoms and included suggestion and planning sections. The second section of the form included assessment of lifestyle changes such as the patient's use of the Web site; eating habits; use of medicine, weight control, and exercising; and planning and suggestion sections based on a common goal. Each interview lasted 5 to 10 minutes and was continued right after the previous one. A reminder was made by text message for both teaching and access to the Web site. A text message was sent to the patients each week (Monday) that included suggestions and reminders regarding Web site visit, symptom follow-up, weight follow-up, use of medications, diet, exercise, eating habits, and salt consumption. All patients were sent standard message contents.

## Procedure

All patients who met the inclusion criteria were included in the study until the determined sample number was reached. Basic data regarding health status, symptom burden, and lifestyle changes were collected in the hospital after the randomization. Study data were collected in approximately 40 to 60 minutes through face-to-face meeting in a hospital room. Patients in the intervention group were trained to access the Web site and use the educational materials on the Web site. A username and password were created for each patient. The patients visited the Web site at least once per week for 12 weeks after discharge. They were provided consultancy for 12 weeks after discharge and were sent text messages once a week. Patient access to the Web site was recorded in a database as dates and hours during the follow-up period. After the intervention phase, data were collected at the 12th week after the discharge when patients visited the outpatient clinic for follow-up. The interviews were ended after data were collected from patients in the intervention group.

No intervention was performed on patients in the control group during the follow-up period. According to standard hospital protocol, patients visit the hospital for a checkup after 3 months from discharge unless they experience any problems. Standard hospital protocol does not include a planned training program, telephone follow-up, or Web-based training. They were able to access the Web site after completing the follow-up condition. The Web site was introduced to the patients, and the usernames and passwords created for the control group were shared with them in the last interview.

## Data Collection

The Patient Information Form was developed by the researcher and included 12 items. The Memorial Symptom Assessment Scale–Heart Failure (MSAS-HF) was used to assess the patients' symptom burden. MSAS-HF assesses patients' symptoms in the last week based on frequency,

severity, and distress in three subscales: physical, psychological, and heart failure symptoms. The total MSAS-HF score constitutes the mean score of all symptoms. Higher total scores and subscale scores indicate increased frequency, severity, and distress experienced by the patients. The Turkish validity and reliability study of the MSAS-HF was carried out by Aşık Özdemir19; the Cronbach's α coefficients for the total scale and the physical, psychological, and heart failure subscales were .70, .38, .55, and .39, respectively.

The Left Ventricular Dysfunction Scale (LVD-36) was used to measure patients' health status. LVD-36 was developed to measure the effect of left ventricular dysfunction on daily life and well-being in patients with heart failure. The scale is composed of 36 questions that patients can answer with yes or no. The total number of “yes” answers is calculated as a percentage in the scale. The lowest possible score on the scale is 0, and the highest 100. A score of 100 is identified as the worst possible score, and 0 as the best possible score on this scale; that is, higher scores indicate lower health status. The Turkish validity and reliability study of

the LVD-36 was performed by Özer and Argon,20 and the Cronbach's α coefficient was found as .90.

## Data Analysis

Statistical Analysis System 9.4 package software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) was used for data analysis. Descriptive statistics constituted the quantitative variables, whereas numbers and percentages constituted the qualitative variables. The t test was applied for independent groups in two-way comparison between two-category variables, and the analysis of variance was applied to find the differences between variables with three or more categories. The pairedsample t test was used to test the time of two different measurements obtained from the same individuals. Repeated-measures variance analysis was used to examine whether there were differences over time and between the groups in terms of mean scale scores, and the \chi ^ { 2 }or Fisher's exact \chi ^ { 2 }test was used to see whether there was a difference in categorical variables between the experimental and control groups. The significance level was taken as .05 for all analyses.

## Characteristics of Study Participants

Characteristics of study participants are shown in Table 1. The distribution analysis of the groups in terms of the characteristics in the Patient Information Form revealed a statistically similar distribution. Mean age of the participants was 60.29 ± 10.91 years, 69% were male, and 66% were in NYHA III classification.

# Symptom Burden Results
There was a significant relationship between the intervention and control groups, from the beginning of the study to the 12th week, in terms of psychological ( F = 2 5 . 9 1 , P < . 0 0 0 1 )physical ( F = 2 1 . 3 6 , P < . 0 0 0 1 ), and heart failure symptoms ( F = 2 1 . 6 9 , P < . 0 0 0 1 )and MSAS-HF total symptom burden ( F = 3 3 . 2 9 , P < . 0 0 0 1 )(Table 2). After the intervention, the mean subscales scores and the symptom burden of the intervention group had a tendency to decrease. There was no statistically significant difference in the control group ( P > . 0 5 ).
