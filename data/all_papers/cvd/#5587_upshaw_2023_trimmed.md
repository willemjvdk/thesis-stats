# Methods
## Participants

Individuals 18 years of age or older with a diagnosis of HF were recruited from 3 hospitals in Massachusetts. Eligible participants had a diagnosis of HF with at least one of the following risk factors for hospitalization: (1) hospitalization for HF within the prior year; (2) New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class III to IV symptoms or (3) B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) of at least 200 pg/mL or N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) of at least 1200 pg/mL measured within 90 days prior to enrollment. Participants were enrolled regardless of left ventricular ejection fraction and there was no upper age limit. Full exclusion criteria can be found in the Supplementary Appendix (Protocol) and included acute myocardial infarction or revascularization within 30 days before enrollment, end stage renal disease requiring renal replacement therapy, an illness other than HF deemed by the study team to be the principal limitation to life expectancy, and inability to participate in the disease management program. The study was approved by the Tufts Medical Center institutional review board and all participants provided written informed consent. The study was investigator-initiated and partially supported by funding from the Verizon Foundation and the Heart

Failure Society of America. The funders had no role in the design or conduct of the study, in the interpretation of the results, or preparation of the manuscript. The study was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02084992).

## Randomization

Participants who fulfilled the eligibility criteria and signed informed consent were randomly assigned in a 3:1 ratio to a tablet technology-based HF disease management or a telephone-based disease management program without telemonitoring capabilities in addition to their usual therapy for HF. Randomization was stratified according to study center and the presence or absence of a HF hospitalization within the previous 30 days. Randomization was executed using sealed, coded envelopes containing the randomization assignment and provided to each study site. 3:1 randomization was selected to maximize experience with the technology; however, uneven randomization reduces statistical power. Due to the nature of the intervention, patients and providers were not masked; however, all hospitalizations were adjudicated by a clinical event committee that was masked to the treatment allocation. Hospitalizations were adjudicated according to prespecified definitions, which are provided in the Supplementary Appendix.

## Study setting and description of the intervention

Participants were identified at the time of admission for HF or at the time of clinic visit for individuals without a recent HF hospitalization. Of note, we enrolled participants from 3 hospitals; however, only 2 subjects were enrolled from 1 hospital before the site discontinued participation due inadequate personnel resources and another hospital only enrolled subjects from 2015 to 2019. Eligible participants were enrolled during a home visit by the HF nurse care manager. Telephone-based Active Control Disease Management Arm (managed using a previously validated HF disease management program): A nurse with training in HF management met with the patient at his or her home to introduce the program. During this initial meeting, the methods for selfmonitoring, review of dietary and medical management of HF, early reporting of changes in weight and clinical status were reviewed using a “teach-back” method of education15 and the participant was provided with a packet of HF educational materials. A scale and blood pressure cuff were provided to participants. After this initial visit, the HF nurse telephoned participants weekly for the first month followed by either every 2 weeks or monthly calls depending on clinical status with the goal of transitioning all participants to monthly calls. During these phone calls the nurse manager focused on identifying changes in clinical condition and education reinforcement. Participants were instructed to check and record their weight, heart rate and blood pressure daily and encouraged to call if there were any changes in

their clinical status. The nurse communicated with the primary care and cardiologist providers at regular intervals and when there were any changes in clinical status. Intervention arm (tablet technology-based HF disease management): Participants received all the components of the validated disease management program, as described above, with the addition of home monitoring via tablet computers. Among the first 83 participants enrolled, 63 were randomized to telehealth monitoring us-

ing the Health Buddy application (Robert Bosch Healthcare Systems) installed on tablet computers. Beginning in September 2015, the Health Buddy application was no longer available and the Genesis Touch remote patient monitor (Resideo Life Care Solutions, Brookfield, WI) was used for the remaining participants (129 participants enrolled, 96 randomized to telehealth monitoring). In both telemonitoring programs, participants were asked to interact with the system daily with transmission

of weight, heart rate, blood pressure and symptom reports to the HF nurse monitoring the participant. Participants were asked daily about new or worsening HF symptoms and asked to enter vital signs into the application. Parameters to be flagged were established for both vital signs and symptoms. A nurse manager reviewed the data daily from Monday to Friday and contacted patients if any parameters exceeded pre-specified parameters. In the event of weight changes, abnormal vital signs or concerning symptoms, the nurse manager spoke with the patient to gather more information, contacted the patient’s cardiologist and/or primary care provider to develop a plan of care, and then communicated these recommendations back to the patient. Providers were contacted by email and/or phone call and a clinical summary was also faxed to the office by the nurse. Nurses also called participants at the same regular intervals as in the control arm and communicated with the primary care and specialty providers at regular intervals and with any changes in clinical status. CONSORT and TIDieR checklists for the randomized trial and description of the intervention can be found in the Supplemental Appendix.

## Study endpoints

The primary endpoint was the number of days hospitalized for HF per patient-90 days.16,17 Key prespecified secondary endpoints included: all-cause mortality, number of unplanned hospitalizations for HF, cardiovascular causes and all causes, change in health status as assessed by the short form-12 (SF-12) health survey including the physical component summary (PCS) and the mental component summary,18 change in self-care maintenance, management and confidence as assessed by the Self-Care of Heart Failure Index version 6 (SCHFI),19 adherence with the daily telehealth sessions, and satisfaction with the disease management program as assessed by the visual analog scale. Adherence to the daily telehealth sessions was defined as the number of sessions completed divided by the number of days alive and out of the hospital.

## Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed at 90 days of patient follow up. All endpoints were analyzed on an intent-to-treat basis. Descriptive statistics of baseline demographic and clinical variables were summarized for each treatment group and compared to check for possible imbalances in distribution that may have occurred by chance. Continuous variables were examined graphically to assess skewness and summarized with mean, standard deviation, minimum and maximum values for normally distributed data and with median and interquartile range for non-normally distributed data. Categorical data were presented as frequency and percentage. The primary efficacy endpoint of number of days hospitalized for heart failure per patient-90 days was assessed using Poisson regression

models without adjustment for covariates. The number of hospitalizations was analyzed using Poisson regression models and the incidence of mortality using Cox Proportional Hazard Models. With Poisson regression, subjects who are lost to follow up or die only contributed the number of days that they are alive and followed in the study. All subjects who completed the study contributed 90 days of follow up. Generalized linear models were used to analyze other continuous endpoints. Prespecified subgroup analyses of the primary endpoint included age, gender, hospitalization within prior 30 days, baseline left ventricular ejection fraction, and baseline NYHA functional class. Additional subgroups were added after the completion of the study but before the analysis of the results and included diabetes, atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, and randomization date before or after September 5, 2015 (the date the telehealth system changed from Health Buddy to Genesis Touch). Assuming a control rate of days hospitalized for HF of 2 days per patient-90 days, a standard deviation of 14 days,4 an alpha of 0.05 and 3:1 randomization, we estimated that we would need 212 participants (53 in the control arm and 159 in the intervention arm) to achieve 90% power to detect a 33% reduction in the primary endpoint.

# Results
## Participants

From August 2014 to April 2019, 212 participants were enrolled from 3 hospitals. As shown in the consort diagram (Figure 1), 682 patients with HF were screened, of whom 570 (84%) were eligible and 212 (37% of eligible participants) were randomized 3:1 to telemonitoringbased HF disease management (n=159) or telephonebased HF disease management (n 53) with 98% of individuals in both study groups completing the 90 days of follow-up. Baseline characteristics are displayed in Table I. Participants reflected a higher risk HF population with an average of more than 1 HF hospitalization in the past year and NYHA Class III/IV symptoms present in 49% at baseline. Comorbidities were highly prevalent including mitral regurgitation (31%), chronic kidney disease (36%), diabetes (47%), and atrial fibrillation (52%). Baseline demographics were generally well balanced between the 2 groups with a few exceptions including younger age but higher NYHA Class and more prevalent diabetes in the telemonitoring arm (Table I ).

## Primary endpoint

There was no significant difference in the number of days hospitalized for HF in the telemonitoring disease management arm (0.88 ± 3.28 days per patient-90 days) compared to the telephone-based disease management arm (1.00  2.97 days per patient-90 days; incidence rate ratio 0.82, [CI] 0.43-1.58; P = .442) (Table II).

## Secondary mortality and hospitalization endpoints

Although numerically lower, HF hospitalizations, cardiovascular hospitalization and all-cause hospitalizations were not significantly different between the 2 disease management interventions (Table II). All-cause mortality occurred in 1% of the participants (n = 2) in the telemonitoring disease management arm and no participants in the telephone-based disease management arm (P  .415).

## Subgroup analysis

Exploratory subgroup analyses of the primary endpoint (Figure 2) suggested potential heterogeneity of treatment effect with more benefit in the early time period when the Health Buddy was used compared with less benefit after September 2015 when the Genesis Touch tablet was used (interaction P value .0038). Additional subgroup analyses suggested potential interactions by diabetes status, history of atrial fibrillation and history of chronic kidney disease.

## Adherence

Among the 159 participants in the telemonitoring arm, 57 (35%) completed at least 80% of the daily sessions

and 95 (59%) completed at least 50% of the daily sessions during the 90-day intervention. Adherence analysis by early versus late randomization showed higher adherence with daily sessions in later time period when the Genesis Touch was used compared with earlier period when the Health Buddy was used (mean 65 ± 23 sessions vs 48 ± 31 session, P-value .0006).

## Health status

There was no difference in the baseline mean SF-12 PCS scores in the treatment and placebo groups (Table III). PCS scores increased slightly from baseline to 90 days in both groups with no significant difference between groups. Similar results were seen with the SF-12 mental component summary with slight improvements in both groups and no significant difference between groups.
