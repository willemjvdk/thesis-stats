# Methods
The SMARTphone-based, early cardiac REHABilitation in patients with ACS (SMART-REHAB) trial is a single-blinded, two-arm, parallel, randomized control trial comparing a S-CRP as an adjunct to usual care (UC) vs. UC alone. It was conducted across six university hospitals in the state of Victoria, Australia.

The study protocol has been described in detail previously [26]. The Austin Health Human Research Ethics Committee granted approval for the trial (HREC/14/AUSTIN/128) and it was registered in the Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry (ACTRN12616000426482).

## Study population

Patient over the age of 18 years with a diagnosis of an ACS and documented coronary artery disease on angiography (coronary artery stenosis >50%), treated either medically or with percutaneous coronary intervention were eligible for enrollment. ACS was defined according to the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction with a combination of detection of an elevated troponin, new ischemic electrocardiographic changes or symptoms of myocardial ischemia [27]. The other major inclusion criterion was personal ownership of a smartphone. The exclusion criteria included untreated ventricular tachycardia, severe heart failure, significant residual coronary artery disease requiring revascularization, treatment with coronary artery bypass surgery, coexisting disease with a life expectancy less than 1 year or significant exercise limitations for reasons other than CHD.

Eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1 fashion through a computerized randomization program. Randomization was stratified by study site and was overseen by the Centre of Cardiovascular Research and Education in Therapeutics at Monash University, an independent research body within the School of Public Health and Preventive Medicine at Monash University,

Melbourne, Australia. Baseline assessment, including the 6-minute walk test, occurred prior to randomization. Clinical follow-up and assessment was performed at 8 weeks. Assessors of the primary outcome were blinded to treatment allocation and all patients provided written informed consent.

## Smartphone-based cardiac rehabilitation

The smartphone-based secondary prevention program has been previously described in detail [26]. In brief, it is a multi-faceted intervention, delivered through a smartphone application, with particular emphasis on early physical activity. It incorporates all core aspects of cardiac rehabilitation; namely exercise prescription, dynamic tracking of cardiovascular risk factors, assessment of dietary habits, heart health education, education on secondary prevention pharmacotherapy as well as interactive and personalized feedback and support (Fig. 1). Regardless of the treatment arm, all patients received standard of care treatment post-ACS which included inpatient cardiology review, pre-discharge planning, formal communication with general practitioner through a discharge summary letter, referral to a traditional cardiac rehabilitation program, promotion of self-care and a chest pain action plan.

# Study outcomes
The primary outcome was the change in 6-minute walk distance between groups, measured at discharge and at 8 weeks, in standardized fashion [28]. The initial test was undertaken prior to hospital discharge and it was repeated at 8 weeks as an outpatient.

Secondary outcomes included uptake, adherence and completion of cardiac rehabilitation, change in fasting lipid levels, hemoglobin A1c, blood pressure, BMI, waist circumference and smoking status. Other pre-specified endpoints were major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), use of guideline-directed secondary prevention therapy, hospital re-admissions, depression, anxiety quality of life and health outcome assessments.

Cardiac rehabilitation uptake, adherence and completion were defined in accordance with previous studies [15]. Uptake was defined as attending at least one exercise session for traditional cardiac rehabilitation or uploading 1 day’s physical activity in the smartphone application. Adherence was defined as attending 4 weeks’ or uploading 12 days of activity over 4 weeks of physical activity. Completion was defined as showing adherence to cardiac rehabilitation and attending the 8-week assessment session. MACE was defined as the composite of death, myocardial infarction, stroke or unplanned revascularization.

Assessment for depression was undertaken with the Cardiac Depression Scale and Depression Scale-Short Form [29]. Assessment of anxiety was undertaken with the use of the hospital anxiety and depression scale.

Change in quality of life was assessed by the SF-36 questionnaire and EQ5D [30].

## Statistical analysis

To detect a minimally important difference in the 6-minute walk test of 25 m with 80% power at 5% level of significance, a sample size of 152 participants was required [31,32]. Given an estimated 20% rate of loss to follow-up, at least 100 patients were enrolled in each arm [15].

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA). Baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables were described as mean and SD and compared using

Student’s t-test. Categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages and compared using Fisher’s exact or chi-square tests as appropriate. Treatment evaluation was performed using an intention to treat analysis. A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

# Results
A total of 206 patients admitted to hospital with an ACS across six tertiary hospitals underwent randomization. There were 38 (18%) patients lost to follow-up and excluded from analysis (Fig. 2). Of the 168 patients included in the analysis, 83 were randomized to S-CRP and 85 to UC.

Fig. 2.

The mean age of patients was 56 ± 10 years, 16% were female and 51% presented with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction. Other baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences in demographic or clinical factors between the two groups. There were no differences between randomized patients included in the analysis and those lost to follow-up.
