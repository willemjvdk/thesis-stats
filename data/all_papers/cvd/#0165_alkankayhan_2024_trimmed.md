# 2. Methods
## 2.1. Patients and protocol design

The study was conducted as a randomized controlled experimental study. Between January 1, 2017, and December 31, 2018, 698 patients were admitted to the Cardiology Clinics in a Turkish hospital with the diagnosis of CAD, and a total of 175 patients took part CR program. Our hospital has four cardiology clinics with a capacity of 53 beds. In our institution’s CR program, an initial assessment is conducted by a physiotherapist and cardiologist. Once deemed suitable for the program, the patient is integrated into the exercise regimen and also receive counseling on various aspects, including nutrition, smoking cessation, and stress management. Over the course of six weeks, a total of 30 sessions are administered. Since the study was carried out during the pandemic period, it was aimed to benefit from this health service online, especially for patients who could not participate in the CR program in our institution during this period.

The sample was determined with the difference between means method using the G-Power 1.3 program [22]. The number of samples to be included was calculated as 70 patients, 35 experimental and 35 control, with an effect size (d) = 0.94, the margin of error of 0.05 at a 95% confidence interval, and at a 95% power analysis. The randomizer. org program was used for the randomization method. Two series of 35 experimental and 35 control groups were randomly created. Once the patients who met the study criteria were identified from the patient lists at the clinics, they were allocated to either the experimental or control groups based on the predetermined order generated by the program. In situations where the next patient declined participation, the subsequent

patient was approached and interviewed to ensure the inclusion of the required number of participants in the respective group. Four patients in the experimental group registered on the website but did not read the training despite reminders and did not answer the calls, and one patient had coronary artery bypass graft surgery with the decision of the council after the interview. Four patients in the control group did not answer the calls, so their final evaluations could not be made, and one patient’s phone number was out of use. Two more sets were created with the randomization program for the excluded 10 patients. The study was completed by interviewing 35 patients from the experimental group and 35 patients from the control group. The study sample flow diagram is shown in Fig. 1.

The inclusion criteria were being a patient diagnosed with CAD or patients with stent implantation after angiography, being an adult patient (18 and over), not having a planned cardiac surgical operation, being clinically stable (a prerequisite for being suitable for the CR program), not participating in the CR program, being literate, being able to communicate verbally, being able to use the internet, having a computer, tablet, or smart phone, and agreeing to participate in the study. Exclusion criteria were having a disease that was not suitable for the CR program (for example, stage IV heart failure, unstable angina), not volunteering, not logging in again after registering on the website, not reading the training, and not being able to communicate during the study.

Ethics committee permission and institutional permission was obtained. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients participating in the study.

## 2.2. Instruments

The data were collected using the “Patient Information Form”, the “Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors Scale II (HLBS-II)”, the “Medication Adherence Questions”, the “EQ-5D Quality of Life Questionnaire (QLQ)”, and the “International Physical Activity Questionnaire-Short Form (IPAQ-SF)”.

## 2.2.1. The Patient Information Form

The form consists of 10 questions, including demographic (gender, age, marital status, education level) and clinical characteristics of the patients (family history of heart disease, comorbidity status and if any, smoking status, alcohol use, and hospital admission status during the study period) and LDL, triglyceride, height, weight, and body mass index (BMI) values.

## 2.2.2. The healthy lifestyle behaviors scale II

The scale, developed by Walker in 1987, was revised in 1996 and became HLBS-II. It has 52 items and six factors: spiritual development, interpersonal relationships, nutrition, physical activity, health responsibility, and stress management [23]. It is a 4-point Likert-type scale: never (1), sometimes (2), often (3), and regularly (4). A healthy lifestyle behaviors score is obtained from the overall score of the scale. The lowest and the highest scores to be obtained from the scale are 52 and 208. As the total score increases, the level of healthy lifestyle behaviors increases. The validity and reliability study for the Turkish population was conducted by Bahar et al. in 2008. The Cronbach alpha coefficient of the scale was found to be 0.92. The Cronbach alpha values for the six factors of the scale were reported as 0.77 for health responsibility, 0.79 for physical activity, 0.68 for nutrition, 0.79 for spiritual development, 0.80 for interpersonal relationships, and 0.64 for stress management [24]. In this study, both pre-test and post-test Cronbach’s alpha values on the scale were found to be 0.92. The pre-test Cronbach’s alpha values of the sub-dimensions were as follows: health responsibility 0.82, physical activity 0.81, nutrition 0.74, spiritual development 0.71, interpersonal relationship 0.71 and stress management 0.67. The post-test Cronbach’s alpha values of the sub-dimensions were as follows: health responsibility 0.85, physical

activity 0.83, nutrition 0.63, spiritual development 0.77, interpersonal relationship 0.73 and stress management 0.58.

## 2.2.3. Medication adherence questions

Three questions were included to assess patients’ adherence to medication therapy: “Do you have problems remembering to take your medication regularly, if your complaints decreased/ended, would you give up taking your medication without your physician’s advice, and would you stop taking your medication if you did not feel well after taking your medication (side effects, etc.)”.

## 2.2.4. The EQ-5D quality of life questionnaire

The questionnaire was developed by the EuroQol Group. It consists

of five dimensions. The mobility dimension assesses individuals walking around or traveling, the self-care dimension assesses self-care activities, usual activities assess everyday chorus, pain/discomfort, and pain/anxiety assess feelings of anxiety, sadness, or unhappiness. The options for each dimension are “no problem, some problem, and major problem”. A score between − 0.59 and 1 is calculated for the five dimensions of the scale. A value of 0 indicates death, a value of 1 indicates perfect health, and negative values indicate unconsciousness and bedridden life, etc. [25]. The scale also includes an easily understandable version of the graded Visual Analog Scale (VAS). Respondents rate their overall health on a scale of 0–100, with 0 representing the worst and 100 representing the best health they can imagine [26]. The validity and reliability study for the Turkish population was conducted by Süt in

2009, and the reliability coefficient was found to be 0.86 [27]. In this study, the pre-test and post-test Cronbach’s alpha values on the scale were 0.65 and 0.61.

## 2.2.5. The international physical activity questionnaire-short form

The scale investigates walking, moderate-intensity and vigorous activities performed for at least 10 min in the last 7 days, and sedentary time spent on an average day and calculates metabolic equivalent (MET) scores. Total duration (minutes) and frequency (days) are required to calculate the score. Standard MET values have been created for each activity (for walking: 3.3, for moderate-intensity physical activities: 4.0, for intensive activities: 8.0, for sitting: 1.5).

MET score of activity= MET value*day*duration (minutes).

Total MET value= walking+moderate intensity+intensive+sitting.

The results are evaluated in three categories: low level, moderate level, and high level. The low level is < 600 METs, the moderate level is 600–3000 METs, and the high level is > 3000 METs. The acceptable value in terms of health is expressed as the moderate level, i.e., 600–3000 METs [28]. The validity and reliability study of the scale for the Turkish population was conducted by Saglam ˘ et al. in 2010. The test-retest reliability coefficient of the scale was 0.69 [29]. In this study, the pre-test and post-test Cronbach’s alpha coefficients were found to be 0.39 and 0.46.

## 2.3. Creating the website

The website was created under the name “Cardiac Rehabilitation Support Program”. The language of the website is Turkish. It was designed for computer, tablet, and mobile phone use. Patients log in to the website with their e-mail address and a personalized password. Education contents and visuals were prepared by the researcher in line with literature review [30–36] and with the consultation of cardiology and cardiovascular surgery specialist physicians, two nurses and nurse academicians specialized in internal medicine. These educations: the main components of the CR program included diet, exercise, risk factor management, medication, and psychosocial management. Other education topics included CAD, risk of having a heart attack again, symptoms of the disease, risk factors of the disease, in which cases should I consult a health. There is an admin control section on the website under the management of the nurse researcher. This section allows for the addition of new education, facilitates the answering of patient queries through the “Ask a question to the researcher” feature and enables the administrator to monitor whether users have accessed and read the assigned education. Information such as the number of times each education has been accessed, which specific education has been accessed, and the most recent login date and time of each user can be viewed (see Apendix 1).

## 2.4. Pilot application

Once the website was developed, three patients were selected for interviews to evaluate the usability and effectiveness of the website. These patients were registered on the website and were asked to provide feedback. Interview was conducted with each patient 30–45 min. The evaluation criteria consisted of 6 multiple choice and open ended questions (see Apendix 2). Based on the feedback received, revisions were made to resize the images and use everyday terms instead of medical terminology. These three patients were not included in the formal study.

## 2.5. Intervention

The interviews were conducted with patients by the nurse researcher in a private room allocated to them before discharge. The educational/ information needs of the patients in the experimental group were individually assessed. Which education they should receive were

determined with the patient. And then, the website was introduced, and they were registered. Patients in the experimental group received both education via the website and telephone counseling for 12 weeks. 3 phone calls were made with the patients in the 1st, 4th, and 8th weeks, which lasted approximately 20 min. The patients’ adherence to reading the education was monitored through the website. If it was determined that some patients had not read the education, reminders were provided to encourage them to do so. The importance and benefits of the education were explained to them, emphasizing the significance of following the recommended guidelines. In each phone call, specific questions were asked to patients, such as inquiring about their smoking habits, do you measure your blood pressure regularly, do you take a walk. For example, if patients confirmed that they were still smoking or did not measure blood pressure regularly or did not take a walk, the nurse researcher made suggestion in line with the guidelines. Patients who exhibited disruptions in adopting healthy lifestyle behaviors received more frequent telephone interviews. Patients’ questions were addressed through the website or during the telephone interviews.

The control group received standard care. Patients received education from both physicians and clinical nurses before being discharged. The nurse researcher did not provide any education. The flow diagram of study implementation phases is shown in Fig. 1.

## 2.6. Data collection

The data were collected as pre-test and post-test in patients with CAD in Cardiology Clinics in a Turkish hospital between November 2019 and February 2021. The pre-test data were collected by face-to-face interview method before the discharge. The post-test data of the experimental group were collected through the website or a telephone interview, while the control group were collected via telephone interview. LDL and total cholesterol values were recorded through the hospital information system.

## 2.7. Data analysis

The data were evaluated using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) 25 program. Independent variables were age, marital status, education level, comorbidity, family history of heart disease, smoking, and alcohol consumption. Scores obtained from the HLBS-II, medication adherence questions, EQ-5D QLQ, IPAQ-SF, and BMI were the dependent variables. Percentages, mean, and standard deviation methods were used to evaluate the descriptive data. Kolmogorov Smirnov test was performed to check for conformity to normal distribution. The difference between categorical variables was analyzed by chi-square test. Independent t-test and paired t-test were used for normally distributed data, and Mann-Whitney U test and Wilcoxon tests were used for data not conforming to normal distribution. Wilcoxon test, paired ttest, Mann-Whitney U test, and chi-square test were used for repeated measurements. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Cohen’s d test and r effect value were used for effect size calculation. Calculations were made using the following website: https://www. psychometrica.de/effect_size.html#cohen. The d, r and Phi effect values were calculated [37].
