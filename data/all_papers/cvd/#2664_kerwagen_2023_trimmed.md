## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods and results
TIM-HF2 was a prospective, randomized, multicentre trial investigating the effect of a structured RPM intervention versus usual care in patients who had been hospitalized for HF within 12 months before randomization. The primary endpoint was the percentage of days lost due to all-cause death or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization. Key secondary endpoints were all-cause and cardiovascular mortality. Outcomes were assessed by LVEF in guideline-defined subgroups of ≤40% (HF with reduced EF [HFrEF]), 41–49% (HF with mildly reduced EF [HFmrEF]), and ≥50% (HF with preserved EF [HFpEF]). Out of 1538 participants, 818 (53%) had HFrEF, 224 (15%) had HFmrEF, and 496 (32%) had HFpEF. Within each LVEF subgroup, the primary endpoint was lower in the treatment group, i.e. the incidence rate ratio [IRR] remained below 1.0. Comparing intervention and control group, the percentage of days lost was 5.4% versus 7.6% for HFrEF (IRR 0.72, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.54–0.97), 3.3% versus 5.9% for HFmrEF (IRR 0.85, 95% CI 0.48–1.50) and 4.7% versus 5.4% for HFpEF (IRR 0.93, 95% CI 0.64–1.36). No interaction between LVEF and the randomized group became apparent. All-cause and cardiovascular mortality were also reduced by RPM in each subgroup with hazard ratios <1.0 across the LVEF spectrum for both endpoints.

## Conclusion

In the clinical set-up deployed in the TIM-HF2 trial, RPM appeared effective irrespective of the LVEF-based HF phenotype.

# Methods
## Study design and participants

The characteristics of the randomized, controlled, multicentre TIM-HF2 trial have been reported in detail previously.9 Briefly, patients were eligible if they had been hospitalized for worsening HF within the 12 months preceding randomization, were in New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II or III, and exhibited either LVEF ≤45% or LVEF >45% with the demand for oral diuretic therapy. Patients with major depression, haemodialysis, or admission to hospital within 7 days prior to randomization were excluded. After obtaining written informed consent, eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the RPM group or usual care (UC) group. The trial was conducted in 113 study sites across Germany, had obtained approval by of the responsible local Ethics Committees, and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

## Telemedical intervention

The RPM intervention consisted of a physician-led telemedical support on a 24/7 basis by a telemedical centre (TMC) located at Charité–Universitätsmedizin Berlin. The TMC team consisted of physicians and specialized HF nurses. All patients received a home telemonitoring system including a mobile phone, a digital tablet and four external devices for the assessment of three-channel electrocardiogram (ECG), peripheral capillary oxygen saturation, non-invasive blood pressure, and body weight. After installation and on-site instruction of the telemonitoring system, all patients underwent a comprehensive assessment and HF education programme by the trained HF nurses. The TMC used the CE-marked telemedical analysis software (‘Fontane’) for the transmission of patient data, review of electronic health records, and communication between TMC, patient, and the patient’s general physician and/or local cardiologists. Following an algorithm- and biomarker-guided treatment approach, patient data including vital signs and medication were reviewed daily. Furthermore, the HF nurses contacted patients monthly or more often if needed to perform a structured telephone call that included teaching and monitoring elements (for details refer to9,20). The combination of telemonitoring with external devices and needs-based telephone contact with the patients by specialized HF nurses ensured multidimensional, individualized decision-making, for example emotional support, titration of HF medication or initiation of hospitalization, if necessary. Patients in the UC group received standard treatment in accordance with the current guidelines for HF during the time period when TIM-HF2 was conducted.21

# Outcome
The primary endpoint was the percentage of days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization or death from any cause, comparing RPM with UC during the individual patient follow-up time. The main secondary outcomes were all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality during the individual patient follow-up time plus 28 days after the last study visit, to a maximum of 393 days; percentage of days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalizations; and percentage of days lost due to unplanned HF hospitalizations.

## Data analysis

Baseline characteristics between the three LVEF subgroups were compared using one-way ANOVA and Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. In order to analyse the distribution of randomization groups within the LVEF subgroups, we used the Welch two-sample t-test, Pearson’s chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test, respectively.

The primary endpoint of the trial was log-transformed (and eventually resulting zeros replaced by a small positive amount), and compared using a weighted linear model with the individual observation time as weight. Homogeneity of the treatment effect across the LVEF subgroup was tested by including an interaction term. Cumulative incidence curves for all-cause mortality were constructed using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences between curves were examined using the log-rank statistic. For cardiovascular mortality, a competing risk analysis was performed to account for the fact that the event in question could not occur due to another fatal event. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to estimate (cause-specific) hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). An interaction term between randomized group and LVEF subgroup was included to test for 2-way and 3-way subgroup-specific treatment effects. Further, LVEF was used as a continuous variable to test the homogeneity of the treatment effect across the whole spectrum and we allowed a non-linear relationship with the outcome by using piecewise-cubic splines. All analyses were performed according to the intention-to-treat principles on the full analysis set using R version 4.2.1.

# Results
## Study population

The characteristics of the TIM-HF2 study population have already been reported in detail previously.9 In brief, the majority (70%) of the 1538 patients was male, the mean age was 70 ± 10.0 years. The baseline characteristics were similar in the RPM and UC groups and the distribution of the randomization groups within the three LVEF subgroups were well balanced (online supplementary Table S1).

Out of 1538 participants, 818 (53%) had HFrEF, 224 (15%) had HFmrEF, and 496 (32%) had HFpEF. The LVEF distribution is depicted in Figure 1. As expected and shown in Table 1, there were distinct clinical profiles between the three subgroups. The proportion of men was significantly higher in patients with HFrEF (78%) and HFmrEF (72%) when compared to patients with HFpEF (55%). By contrast, patients with HFpEF were older (73.0 ± 9.0 years) than patients with HFrEF or HFmrEF (68.4 ± 11.0 and 71.5 ± 10.1 years, respectively), and had a higher body mass index and systolic blood

pressure value. Current atrial fibrillation (AF) (on ECG) at baseline was more frequent in HFpEF (42%) than in HFrEF (32%) or HFmrEF (35%). There were no significant differences between the three groups regarding NYHA functional class. In contrast to HFpEF, the primary cause of HF in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF was predominantly ischaemic (i.e. coronary heart disease or history of myocardial infarction) and they had higher rates of cardiovascular interventions than patients with HFpEF.

## Primary and key secondary endpoints

The event rate for the primary outcome was highest in the HFrEF cohort: 146 of 409 patients (36%) in the RPM group and 167 of 409 patients (41%) in the UC group were admitted to the hospital due to unplanned cardiovascular reason or died of any cause. For HFmrEF, the number of patients with events was 35 of 102 (34%) versus 41 of 122 (34%), and for HFpEF 84 of 254 (33%) versus 82 of 242 (34%).

As shown in Table 2 and Figure 2, the primary endpoint percentage of days lost was 5.38% versus 7.58% for HFrEF (ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.54–0.97), 3.30% versus 5.92% for HFmrEF (ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.48–1.50) and 4.68% versus 5.41% for HFpEF (ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.64–1.36). The respective interaction term yielded p = 0.558. The ratio reached statistical significance in the

HFrEF subgroup (p = 0.029). The burden of days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular reason or death of any cause was highest in the HFrEF cohort (RPM: 19.7 days vs. UC: 27.7 days), lowest in in HFmrEF (12.1 vs. 21.6 days) and intermediate in HFpEF (17.1 vs. 19.8 days). The largest absolute gain in survival and reduced time in hospital for cardiovascular reasons (‘days alive and out of hospital for cardiovascular reasons’) by RPM was observed in the HFmrEF subgroup (9.5 days), followed by HFrEF (8.0 days) and HFpEF (2.7 days). Of note, the burden of unplanned cardiovascular hospitalizations was highest in the HFrEF group and lowest in the HFpEF group: incidence of unplanned cardiovascular hospitalizations (60.9 vs. 50.1 per 100 patient-years) and number of days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalizations (8.0 ± 18.1 vs. 5.5 ± 12.4 days) (online supplementary Tables S2 and S3). For both key secondary endpoints, i.e. all-cause death and cardiovascular death, the respective HRs were consistently <1 for each subgroup (Table 2), and we found no significant treatment-by-LVEF interaction for all-cause death (p = 0.974) or cardiovascular death (p = 0.737). As shown in Figures 3 and 4, cumulative incidence analyses confirmed the reduced all-cause and cardiovascular mortality for all LVEF subgroups and the respective HRs remained <1, when using LVEF as a continuous variable. Furthermore, the findings for all three endpoints were consistent when applying

the LVEF cut-off of 45% for dichotomization into two subgroups (LVEF ≤45% and >45%) (online supplementary Table S4). With respect to the observed discrepancies in AF at baseline, no three-way interaction between LVEF, AF at baseline, and treatment was detected with regard to percentage of days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalization or death of any cause (p = 0.15), all-cause mortality (p = 0.91), and cardiovascular mortality (p = 0.17).
