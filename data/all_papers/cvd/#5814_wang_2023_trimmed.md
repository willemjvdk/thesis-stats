## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
The study was conducted per the institutional guidelines and approved by the Committee for Medical Ethics. This study’s Clinical Trial registration number is [2018]-(037). This was a pragmatic, single-blinded, randomized controlled trial. The study lasted from January 2021 to January 2022.

## Subjects

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met all of these inclusion criteria: (1) aged 18 or over; (2) frst-time ischemic stroke; (3) swallowing dysfunction assessed by a standardized screening test (water swallowing test) in addition to a clinical or instrumental (Videofuoroscopic Swallowing Study (VFSS) or Flexible Endoscopic Examination of Swallowing (FEES)) examination by a speech-language therapist; (4) hospitalized with a stroke for at least one week before inclusion; (5) expected to be discharged; (6) have no cognitive impairment or speech disorder; (7) familiar with internet use; (8) not participating in other clinical trials at the same time and willing to participate in this study. In addition, exclusion criteria were unstable health conditions and a history of previous other diseases that might lead to dysphagia. Two well-trained research assistants, blinded to the intervention allocation, screened patients for eligibility and did all outcomes measurements.

## Intervention

Patients randomized to the intervention group received an individualized digital coaching program six weeks after the inclusion, in addition to standard care.

## Stroke coach

The stroke coach is a specialized nurse with at least fve years of working experience as a swallowing rehabilitation manager at a stroke unit. The stroke coach works under

the supervision of a therapist. The coaching intervention included two phases: education and incentive. In the frst (education phase), the coach explained the benefts of a regular swallowing rehabilitation practice, the swallowing rehabilitation recommendations of the Chinese expert consensus group on dysphagia rehabilitation, and self-care skills concerning dysphagia. The coach provided at least one educational session of 20 min to every hospitalized participant at the stroke unit. In addition, the coach described the digital platform to each patient. In the second phase, WeChat video consultations with the stroke coach were scheduled every week after discharge to encourage and incentivize regular swallowing rehabilitation practice and to set goals for the next week. Besides, the coach discussed with the patients and helped resolve specifc daily swallowing-related problems.

Outline of health coaching activities: (1) An individualized swallowing rehabilitation plan will be developed. To be specifc, the coach would assist the patients in analyzing their rehabilitation, then guide the patients to set feasible and attainable goals and action plans by themselves. (2) The coach discuss with patients over an implementation schedule for action plans and check the progress via a weekly WeChat while reinforcing patients’ values and responsibilities of self-care skills concerning dysphagia, empowering patients to regular swallowing rehabilitation practice. (3) Patient dyads discuss a safe home or social eating strategies, such as the choice of food. In addition, the coach demonstrates how to monitor daily nutrition or water intake by themselves. (4) To ensure the patient’s views are heard, the patient shared experience, hindrance factor or promotion factor in the implementation of swallowing rehabilitation. (5) A appropriate method to record the daily swallowing rehabilitation exercise was determined through discussions between the coach and patient.

## Digital platform

The digital platform was developed through WeChat applets (lightweight apps that form part of the WeChat ecosystem, which could be used independently) with swallowing features customized at the request of the speech therapist investigators of this study. Instructional video workouts for swallowing rehabilitation practice, such as efortful swallow, pitch glides, and ice stimulation, were included in the WeChat applet. The application included reminders to complete swallowing rehabilitation exercises and a logging feature to capture the completion of exercises. In addition, the application had interactive queries if a patient did not complete their exercises to determine the reason for non-adherence. Communication between patients and the coach was possible through WeChat messaging system to adjust exercise patterns and frequency.

Besides, there was an online check-in forum for patients to record and share daily exercises and food in the form of texts and photos, and patients could appreciate and comment on tweets to encourage peers.

## Control group

The patients in the control group received usual standard care, including information of swallowing rehabilitation practice, such as efortful swallow, pitch glides, and ice stimulation, and recommendations before discharge and a medical appointment at one month after hospital discharge. However, the control group did not receive video consultations or digital intervention. For control group, other registered nurses, who are not intervention nurses, will conduct the swallowing rehabilitation plan and follow-up.

# Outcome measurements
## Functional oral intake scale (FOIS)

FOIS is a reliable, valid parameter widely used to measure a patient’s functional eating abilities [6]. FOIS is based on the level of oral intake of food and liquid consistency recommended by an objective swallow assessment with clinical/ instrumental testing, which a speech-language therapist evaluates. Swallowing assessment consisted of the examination of oral musculature strength, agility, and symmetry, as well as examination of protective refexes and sensation. Testing included the 30mL water swallow test and volume-viscosity swallow test. Instrumental testing with videofuoroscopy or fberoptic endoscopy was performed in patients with indeterminate results of the clinical evaluation or when deemed necessary by the physician. FOIS ranged from 1 to 7, and a higher score indicates a better swallowing function. FOIS score reached 7, indicating normal swallowing function.

## Swallowing quality‑of‑life questionnaire (SWAL‑QOL)

SWAL-QOL is considered a gold standard for evaluating the quality-of-life of individuals with dysphagia [22]. The questionnaire consists of a single symptom-frequency scale (14 items) and ten subscales: burden, eating desire, eating duration, food selection, fear of eating, communication, mental health, fatigue, social functioning, and sleep. SWAL-QOL exhibited good internal consistency, reliability, and short-term reproducibility.

## Pneumonia

The physician’s diagnosis of pneumonia was recorded using the Centres for Disease Control and Prevention’s pneumonia criteria [8].

## Statistical analysis

GraphPad, PRISM, and SPSS version 17.0 were used for statistical analysis. Continuous data with a normal distribution were presented as mean and standard deviation and compared using either unpaired or paired t tests. Non-normally distributed continuous data were given as a median and interquartile range and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test or the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Categorical variables were described as counts and percentages, and Chi-square or Mann–Whitney U tests

were used to compare them. P values less than 0.01 were deemed statistically significant.

# Results
In total, 109 patients met the entry criteria. Fifty-fve patients were assigned to the intervention group, with 54 patients in the control group. However, three patients had aggravated stroke, recurrence, or found an organic mass, one patient had a severe psychological disorder, three dropped out of intervention, and one dropped out of the study. Finally, 101 patients were included, precisely 51 patients in the intervention group and 50 in the control group. Both groups’ demographic and baseline characteristics were similar (all P > 0.05, Table 1).

Figure 1 displays the functional oral intake scale from baseline to six weeks. Among 101 patients, the number of

patients graded 7 in the intervention group (n = 13, 25%) was signifcantly more than that of the control group (n = 5, 10%) at three weeks (P = 0.036). However, the functional oral intake scale was comparable between the intervention and control groups at six weeks (P = 0.083).

Table 2 displays the swallowing quality-of-life questionnaire from baseline to six weeks. Again, the change in the swallowing quality-of-life questionnaire of the intervention group was signifcantly more signifcant than that of the control group (P = 0.032).

Table 3 displays the incidence of pneumonia during six weeks. Again, the incidence of pneumonia was comparable between intervention and control groups (P = 0.153).
