# 3. Methods
## 3.1. Design

This was a randomized clinical trial with a control group. Patients with hypertension were selected using a convenient sampling method and were allocated to four groups as follow(i) control, (ii) selfmanagement training without follow-up, (iii) telephone follow-up and (iv) smartphone-based social networking follow up.

## 3.2. Ethical considerations

After achieving permissions from the Ethics Committee affiliated with Iran University of Medical Sciences (decree code: IR.IUMS.REC1394.9313677003) and registering the research protocol in the website of clinical trials (registration code: IRCT2016012426173N1), the researcher selected a teaching hospital for conducting the study. The patients were completely informed about the research aim and method and those who were willing to take part in this study were requested to sign the informed consent form.

## 3.3. Participant

A convenience sampling method was used to choose patients with HTN who visited the emergency ward of the selected hospital. Inclusion criteria for the selection of the patients were: the diagnosis of primary HTN by a physician, blood pressure higher than 140/85 mmHg, the age of 35-80 years, neither the patient nor any of patients’ family members should be healthcare staff, admitted to the emergency ward and no hospitalization after the completion of treatment, no history of auditory, visual, and cognitive problems, able to read and write in Farsi, have access to smart phone, ability to use smart phones and mobile phonebased social network (Telegram®) and have access to internet.

The sample size was determined as 25 patients in each group given alpha 0.05, power 80% and the effect of SM education using telephone follow-up and smart phone social network on SM behaviors in patients with HTN compared with the control group would be at least 0.4. The process of the study was reported in Figure 1.

## 3.4. Random allocation

Allocation of the patients to the groups was conducted using the block randomization without permutations with the size of four and a ratio of 1:1. Different allocation conditions were written on cards. They were placed in separate sealed opaque envelopes, which were placed in a box. The researchers did not know about allocation prior to selection of the cards. Each patient was requested to pick up one envelope out of the box. Also, the ward nurse who was fully unaware of the study process and groups determined which four patients would fall under which group. Next, the patients who met the inclusion criteria were allocated to the groups. This process continued until the desired sample size was accomplished.

# 3.5. Outcome measurement
The following tools were used for data collection: Demographic data form; Health information form; Hypertension SM Behavior Questionnaire (HSMBQ).

The demographic data and health information forms were filled out before the intervention through reviewing the patients; files or interviewing them. The HSMBQ was derived from a study on SM among patients with HTN in Bangladesh (Akhter, 2010) and consisted of 40 items about different aspects of SM as follow: thirteen items about self-integration, eight items about self-regulatory, nine items about interactions with healthcare professionals and significant others; four items about self-monitoring and five items about adherence to the recommended regimen. The items had a four-point scale from never (1) to always (4). The mean score of the items was considered the score of each dimension and was calculated as the total score to analyze data. The study by Akhter (2010) in Bangladesh reported an appropriate validity and reliability of this instrument with a Cronbach’s alpha 0.70 and an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) 0.91 (Akhter, 2010). In this study also, this instrument had an appropriate validity and a Cronbach’s alpha and ICC 0.87 and 0.93, respectively. This questionnaire was filled out by the patients before the intervention and six weeks after that.

## 3.6. Intervention

The SM without telephone follow-up group participated in sessions, that lasted for 30-45 minutes in the hospital. They were taught by the researcher using the face-to-face method along with discussions. The contents of education were related to the definition of normal blood pressure, definition of HTN, causes of the disease, risk factors, symptoms of the disease, SM behaviors in HTN including adherence to the dietary regimen, proper exercises, adherence to medication regimen, drug side effects, and how to measure blood pressure. The patients were requested to inform the researcher or visit the patient education’s department at the hospital, if they had any question.

The SM with telephone follow-up group received SM education similar to the group of patients without telephone follow up. The researcher contacted the patients via the telephone once a week for a period of six weeks to collect data about the implementation of the content of SM education, provide the patients with information about healthcare tips, give them with necessary reminders and answer their questions. The phone calls were made by the researcher between 8 AM and 8 PM and lasted up to 15 minutes. The content of the phone calls involved asking questions regarding the patient’s health status, daily monitoring of blood pressure (it was measured and recorded by the patient and/or their family members using a digital sphygmomanometer at a specific time of day on a weekly basis), adherence to the

therapeutic regime, providing them with health advice, assessing their needs for referral, encouraging them to use what they have been taught, and determining a time for making the next phone call. Also, the patients were requested to contact the researcher in case of any question and problem whenever they wanted.

The group of SM education with smartphone-based social networking (Telegram®) underwent SM education using the education method and content similar to the group of SM education without telephone follow up. Questions were asked about the patients’ health status and adherence to the therapeutic regime in order to improve their health behaviors and assess their needs for referral on a weekly basis. The researcher sent the patients necessary information and advice in the form of key tips using educational images and videos on a weekly basis. The patients were encouraged to keep in touch with the researcher, put the information related to their daily monitoring at the researcher’s disposal and receive necessary feedbacks from the researcher. The patients in the control group only received routine education. They were asked to visit the patient education department of the hospital, if necessary. The HSMBQ was completed by the patients six weeks after the implementation of the education program.

## 3.7. Data analysis

Descriptive and inferential statistics were used to analyze data via the SPSS software version 16. For descriptive statistical analysis, frequency, frequency percentage, mean, and standard deviation were used. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to ensure the normality of the data before analysis. The Chisquare test was used to compare the qualitative variables between the groups. The one-way ANOVA test was used to compare mean quantitative factors including age, SM behaviors, and their dimensions between the groups. Also, the Scheffe ad hoc test helped with the paired comparison between the groups. The paired t-test was used to compare the means of SM behaviors and its dimensions before and six weeks after the intervention in each group.

# 4. Results
## 4.1. Demographics and clinical characteristics

In this study, 100 patients with HTN participated. No statistically significant differences were reported between the groups in terms of demographic and health data (Table 1). All the participants were Shiite Muslims and were covered by the insurance.

## 4.2. Self-management behaviors

Before the intervention, the groups had no statistically significant differences in terms of total SM behaviors and its dimensions (Table 2). Six weeks after the intervention, the patients in the groups, had statistically

significant differences in terms of total SM behaviors and all dimensions. According to the Scheffe ad hoc test, statistically significant differences were reported in the mean scores of SM behaviors and related dimensions in those who underwent SM training (with and without follow up) compared to the control group (p< 0.001). Those in the telephone and smart phone social networking follow-up groups had statistically significant differences in SM behaviours and its dimensions (p<0.001) compared to the group without follow-up. There were no statistically significant difference between the telephone and smart phone social networking follow-up groups in terms of SM behaviors and its dimensions.

In addition, the total SM behaviors and its dimensions were significantly enhanced six weeks after the SM education without follow up, with telephone follow-up and with smart-phone social networking follow-up compared to before these interventions. A significant difference was also observed in the self-regulation dimension before and after the study in the control group.
