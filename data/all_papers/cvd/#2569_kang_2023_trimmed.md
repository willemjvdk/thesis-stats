## A B S T R A C T

Objective: The aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of the WeChat platform-based health education on patients with stable coronary artery disease (CAD) compared with usual care.

Methods: We conducted a randomized controlled trial that included patients with stable CAD who were admitted to the Bin Hai Wan Central Hospital of Dongguan between January 2020 and December 2020. Participants in the control group received a standard regimen of care. In the WeChat group, the patients were provided with the WeChat platform-based health education by multidisciplinary team members in addition to usual care. The coprimary outcome of the study was the blood pressure, lipid profile, fasting blood glucose, Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAMA) scores, Hamilton Depression Scale (HAMD) scores and Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ) scores at 12 months, relative to baseline levels.

Results: Between January 2020 and December 2020, 200 eligible CAD patients were randomly assigned to WeChat group (n = 100) or usual care group (n = 100). After 12 months, the number of participants who knew the risk factors, symptoms, diagnostic criteria, management methods and treating target of CAD was significantly larger in the WeChat group than at baseline (P < 0.05) and also larger than the post-intervention level of the control group (P < 0.05). The systolic blood pressure after intervention of the WeChat group significantly decreased compared to those of the control group (132.06 ± 8.87 mmHg vs 140.32 ± 9.42 mmHg; P < 0.05). After intervention, the triglycerides, total cholesterol, and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol of the WeChat group significantly decreased compared to those at baseline and significantly decreased than those in the control group (all P < 0.05). After the intervention, scores of HAMA and HAMD both significantly decreased in the two groups. Moreover, the decreases were more significant in the WeChat group than in the control group (5.78 ± 0.98 vs 8.54 ± 1.24; 6.27 ± 1.03 vs 8.63 ± 1.66; P < 0.05). The SAQ scores of WeChat group were significantly higher than those of the control group in all 5 dimensions at the 1-year follow-up (72.71 ± 10.83 vs 59.32 ± 9.86; 80.01 ± 11.56 vs 61.98 ± 11.02; 76.76 ± 12.64 vs 65.22 ± 10.72; 83.17 ± 13.06 vs 67.01 ± 12.86; 71.82 ± 12.78 vs 55.79 ± 11.90; all P < 0.05).

Conclusion: This study showed the high efficacy of the WeChat platform-based health education in improving health outcomes in patients with CAD.

Practice implications: This study highlighted the potential of social media as a helpful tool for health education among patients with CAD.

# 2. Methods
## 2.1. Participants

We conducted a prospective cohort study that included patients with stable CAD who were admitted to the Bin Hai Wan Central Hospital of Dongguan between January 2020 and December 2020. All consecutive patients with documented CAD during their index admission were eligible for inclusion. The inclusion criteria were as follows: participants were required to be aged 18 years or older and to have a diagnosis of stable CAD according to the guidelines [12,13]. All participants underwent coronary computed tomography angiography or coronary angiography. Patients were excluded from the study if they met any of the following criteria: (1) had a life expectancy of less than a year because of comorbidities; (2) inability to participate in the study due to physical disability, mental illness or cognitive functional difficulties; (3) pregnancy; (4) refused to provide signed informed consent; or (5) incomplete personal data.

Participants were randomly assigned (1:1) to either WeChat group (observation group) or usual care group (control group) by permuted block randomisation. The randomisation sequence was computergenerated by staff who were not involved with the recruitment of

study participants.

Following randomisation, baseline clinical characteristics were extracted from patients’ medical records and collected using a questionnaire. The baseline data mainly included the following contents: patient demographics, disease course, history of smoking, education, monthly household income, marital status, job status, medical insurance, frequency of WeChat use, medical history and medications.

## 2.2. Intervention

Control group (usual care group). Participants in the control group received a regular regimen of care. The standard of care for patients with CAD includes brief inpatient health education from a ward nurse, medication management, and improvised follow-up visits to a cardiologist depending on the patient’s self-evaluation of their cardiovascular health.

Observation group (WeChat group). In addition to usual care, patients in the WeChat group received health education from multidisciplinary team members via the WeChat platform. The multidisciplinary team included cardiologists, psychologists, nurses, physical therapists, and nutritionists. Participants in the WeChat group were requested to join a WeChat virtual community comprising of patients and members of multidisciplinary teams. Consequently, the patient may receive health education from the multidisciplinary team and converse with team members. Using WeChat’s built-in features, the patient could also begin a chat or phone call with any member of the multidisciplinary team. The members of the multidisciplinary team regularly publish relevant information regarding the self-management of CAD, such as basic knowledge of CAD, diet, nutritional consumption management, exercise, medication administration, smoking cessation, alcohol restriction, risk factor management, daily behavior observations, psychological guidance, etc. This information was provided in the form of photographs, texts, and videos, three times a week. In addition, we recommended participants in the WeChat group whose conditions were deteriorating to seek urgent medical attention. Details were shown in the screenshots of the WeChat platform in Fig. 1.

# 2.3. Outcomes
The coprimary outcome of the study was the blood pressure, lipid profile, fasting blood glucose, Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAMA) scores, Hamilton Depression Scale (HAMD) scores and Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ) scores at 12 months, relative to baseline levels. Psychological status was assessed by the HAMA and HAMD scores [14,15]. HAMA ≥7 points indicate a possibility of anxiety, and HAMA <7 point indicate no anxiety. With a total of 17 items, HAMD showing a score of 7–17 points indicates a possibility of depressive symptoms, and a score of <7 points indicates no depressive symptoms. Higher score reflects more severe anxiety and depression. The self-administered SAQ was originally designed in English and validated in angina patients [16]. The SAQ was developed to quantify the frequency and effects of angina over the previous four weeks. It consists of 19 questions scored on a scale from 1 (severe limitation) to either 5 or 6 (no limitation) across 5 domains (physical limitation, angina stability, angina frequency, treatment satisfaction, and disease perception). Scales are calculated by summing items within a dimension and transforming it to a 0–100 scale. The scores for these five dimensions ranged from 0 to 100, signifying the worst to best health-related quality of life.

Secondary outcomes were: the knowledge about CAD (which included risk factors, symptoms, diagnostic criteria, management methods and treating target), lifestyle improvement (which included healthy diet, smoking cessation, alcohol abstinence, regular exercise, blood pressure monitoring and regular medication) and adverse events (which included unstable angina pectoris, acute myocardial infarction, heart failure, malignant ventricular arrhythmias, sudden cardiac death and all-cause death).

## 2.4. Statistical analysis

SPSS version 22.0 was used to analyse data. The basic characteristics of the participants were described by frequency and mean ± standard deviation (SD). Baseline characteristics of the two groups were compared using \chi ^ { 2 }tests for categorical data and independent t tests for continuous variables. Wilcoxon signed-rank sum test was used for nonparametrical variables. A P value < 0.05 was considered significantly different. Analyses for both primary and secondary outcomes were done according to the intention-to-treat principle.

# 3. Results
Between January 2020 and December 2020, 301 inpatients with coronary artery disease were invited to participate in the study, of whom 53 did not meet the inclusion criteria, 32 declined to participate, 13 did not complete baseline assessments, and 3 were excluded for other reasons. 200 eligible patients were randomly assigned to WeChat group (n = 100; observation group) or usual care group (n = 100; control group). During the study, 4 patients either refused follow-up data collection or were lost to follow-up after randomization. 196 participants completed the 12-month follow-up (Fig. 2). Participants in the two

groups were well matched at baseline (Table 1). There was no statistical significance when comparing the baseline data between the two groups ( \mathbf { P } > 0 . 0 5 ;Table 1).

## 3.1. Awareness of CAD knowledge and lifestyle improvement

There was no significant difference in the knowledge about CAD between the two groups at baseline ( \mathbb { P } > 0 . 0 5 ; \mathbb { F } 1 \mathrm { g . ~ } 3 ). After 12 months, the number of participants who knew the risk factors, symptoms, diagnostic criteria, management methods and treating target of CAD was significantly larger in the WeChat group than at baseline ( \mathbf { P } < 0 . 0 5 )and also larger than the post-intervention level of the control group ( \mathbf { P } < 0 . 0 5 ). Participants in the WeChat group had a healthier diet, reduced their alcohol consumptions, improved their physical activity, monitored their blood pressure more regularly, and adhered to their medication more frequently. Except for smoking cessation, all variables were significantly better than those at baseline and significantly better than those in the control group ( \mathbf { P } < 0 . 0 5 ). In contrast, variables did not substantially improve between baseline and follow-up in the control group ( \mathrm { P } > 0 . 0 5 ; \mathrm { F i g . ~ } 4 ).

# 3.2. Health outcomes
Before the intervention, blood pressure, lipid profile, fasting blood glucose, HAMA scores, and HAMD scores, of these two groups showed no differences ( \mathbf { P } > 0 . 0 5 ;Table 2). Following the intervention, the systolic blood pressure (SBP) decreased in both groups; the SBP after intervention of the WeChat group significantly decreased compared to those of the control group ( \mathbf { P } < 0 . 0 5 ;Table 2). At the 12-month followup, the diastolic blood pressure (DBP) after intervention of the WeChat group significantly decreased compared to those at baseline and significantly decreased than those in the control group. No differences in DBP were observed between pre-intervention and post-intervention in control group ( \mathrm { P } > 0 . 0 5 ;Table 2). After intervention, the triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) of the WeChat group significantly decreased compared to those at baseline and significantly decreased than those in the control group ( \mathbf { P } < 0 . 0 5 ;Table 2). Before the intervention, no significant differences in scores of HAMA and HAMD were observed between the two groups ( \mathbf { P } > 0 . 0 5 ;Table 2). After the intervention, scores of HAMA and HAMD both significantly decreased in the two groups. Moreover, the decreases were more significant in the WeChat group than in the control group ( \mathbf { P } < 0 . 0 5 ;Table 2).

## 3.3. SAQ scores between the two groups

There were no statistically significant differences in the 5 SAQ scores between the 2 groups at baseline. At the 1-year follow-up, the SAQ scores of WeChat group were significantly higher than those of the control group in all 5 dimensions: angina stability, physical limitation, angina frequency, cognitive perception of disease, and satisfaction with treatment (P < 0.05; Table 3).

## 3.4. Adverse events

During the follow-up period of 12 months, there were no obvious differences between the two groups in terms of adverse events (P > 0.05; Table 4).
