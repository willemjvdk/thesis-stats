## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
Full details of the randomised controlled trial comparing CAP2 with conventional centre-based cardiac rehabilitation have been published elsewhere.9 Participants in the telehealth group received a mobile phone, Wellness Diary

and a Wellness web portal, with daily text messaging. Participants in the usual care group received the standard 6-week hospital-based outpatient cardiac rehabilitation programme, including gym sessions. Both groups received comprehensive rehabilitative care encompassing exercise, risk modification and mentoring.

At the start of the intervention, both groups undertook a clinical review to determine demographic and previous clinical, procedural and risk factor characteristics. Baseline measurements were obtained, and the participants were set goals addressing modifiable lifestyle behaviours. The clinical outcome measures were repeated at the 6-week and 6-month follow up points.

The costs and benefits for each group were evaluated using cost benefit analysis and cost effectiveness analysis.12 Cost and benefit data were collected from assessments, semi structured interviews, model consultations, financial reports and system outputs. Costs and benefits for each group were compared on a per patient basis, using a cost/ benefits model developed by Whittaker.13

The direct and indirect costs were those of a 6-week rehabilitation programme. Participants who undertook the programme were followed up for a re-assessment after 6 months. The benefits were measured in terms of health outcomes, quality and safety, access, participation and effectiveness outcomes (Table 1). Quality and safety of the programme included choices in service, complaints, feedback, errors and reportable incidents. Reportable incidents were defined as an incident that could have a material impact on a participant or staff member.

# Results
A total of 120 participants were enrolled, with 60 randomised to the telehealth group and 60 randomised to usual care.

## Costs

A summary of the comparative costs of each arm of the trial is shown in Table 2.

The main activities undertaken by the Rehabilitation Units were defined by cardiac rehabilitation staff during interviews. The average time spent performing each activity was based on interviews with 6 staff involved in the programme, as well as additional feedback obtained from Queensland Health and the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO). Cost values were obtained from the Finance Department of the Prince Charles Hospital and the CSIRO.

The costs of each programme were quite similar, but there were reduced Facility and Gymnasium costs in the telehealth programme. From the provider’s perspective, the telehealth intervention could be delivered for a slightly lower cost of $1633 per patient, compared to $1845 for the usual care group. From the participant’s perspective, as would be expected, patient travel costs for home rehabilitation were substantially less than for hospital attendance (calculated at $20 per trip).

Modelling indicated that doubling the number of patients using the home-based programme would reduce the costs by approximately $80 per patient.

## Benefits

Health outcomes. There were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between patients in the two groups. After 6 weeks, there were significant improvements in weight/BMI, waist circumference, 6 MWT, Depression and Anxiety scores, K10, EQ5D-Index, EQ5D-VAS, DHQ (fat, fibre and salt) and triglycerides in both groups.11

Quality and safety. There were 7 reportable incidents for the usual care group and 10 for the telehealth group. Taking into account the increased number of patients in the telehealth group, this was considered a similar rate.

Access. Access to the programme was not a significant factor, as the participants selected for the programme lived within 45 km of the centre. Members of the project team agreed that the telehealth programme could assist rural and remote, and indigenous and culturally and linguistically diverse people to attend the programme.

Participation. The completion rate for patients in the telehealth programme (80%) was almost twice that of patients in the centre-based programme (47%).11 The provision of technologies, including a smart phone, daily sending of motivational text messages, capturing of personal measures via smart devices and an interactive health and wellbeing portal were important factors in this improvement. The telehealth technologies gave participants additional flexibility to attend work or travel, reduced the burden of attending the gymnasium at the centre, and encouraged them to become more involved in their health and wellbeing.

Effectiveness. The cost of one cardiac re-admission was estimated to be $39,670.14 Canyon and Meshgin reported a significantly smaller number of total re-admissions in patients participating in a community-based programme compared to patients who did not attend the programme (8% and 28% respectively).15 Based on this and other evidence suggesting that completing a formal rehabilitation programme significantly reduces the risk of a secondary event and re-admission,16 the net-present value was calculated at $4008 per patient. Taking into account the cost of the telehealth programme $1633, this would equate to a savings in health care costs of $2375 per patient.

In Queensland, over 15,000 patients are discharged each year with a cardiac diagnosis. Assuming the same reduction in re-admissions and a 20% increase in participation, the cost/benefits model showed that this could create savings of approximately $24 million per year in Queensland.
