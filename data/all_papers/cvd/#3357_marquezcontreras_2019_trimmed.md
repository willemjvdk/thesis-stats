# Methods
## Design

An observational, prospective, randomized controlled trial, full study and multicenter study was designed, involving 154 patients treated with an antihypertensive drug for the treatment of mild to moderate arterial hypertension, according to usual clinical practice. The study was carried out in four primary care centers in Huelva, involving 22 researchers.

## Calculation of the sample size

The necessary sample size was calculated for studies that have proportions as main results and requite analysis by

bilateral contrast. Differences of 25%8 of non-adherence between the groups and an estimated prevalence of nonadherence of 35% in the control group were considered of clinical value. Losses of 10% were considered possible, resulting in a sample of 77 individuals in each of the two groups. The final whole sample was 154 patients.

## Randomization

Cluster randomization was performed. Researchers were randomized, their patients being assigned to the control or intervention group. The randomization was carried out in a centralized manner, by an independent person and using random number tables.

## Intervention groups

The groups were:

1. Control group (CG): 77 patients, who received the usual intervention in HBP (control every 6 months of blood pressure, annual control of therapeutic adherence, annual analysis and biannual electrocardiogram).

2. Intervention group (IG): 77 patients who owned and used a mobile phone with iPhone or Android systems. A free app was installed on their mobile phones with the aim of promoting health education in AHT and reminders of both appointments and medication intake time. This app is called AlerHTA: it has been specially created for the Hypertensive Club of the Spanish Society of Hypertension (SEH-LELHA). It is easily accessible and free to obtain. After the patients downloaded the application, the physician instructed them on how to use it and they were given, in addition, the operating instructions of the app in writing. The app allows you to record personal data, recommended BP levels as objectives, the doctor’s advice about the prescribed treatment and posology, and set reminder alarms, a calendar of appointments or events, and record the results of the BP measurement.

Medication Event Monitoring Systems (MEMSs) are electronic monitoring devices that use a digital record in the form of a microchip in the lid of the drug container that automatically controls its opening and registers the time and date. Through this method we measured how many times the MEMSs were opened, assuming that one pill was taken with each opening.

The study lasted 18 months, with an inclusion period of 6 months and a follow-up of 12 months (January 2016 to June 2017). The selection of patients was made consecutively. The researcher enrolled the first seven patients attending her or his service, and they were included in the study on the same day.

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were: (1) outpatients aged over 18 years; (2) patients diagnosed with mild to moderate hypertension (ESH-ESC 2013 criteria), in pharmacological treatment

with an antihypertensive tablet at least 1 month before its inclusion.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) pregnant or lactating women; (2) patients whose pathological situation could interfere with the development of the study (acute myocardial infarction, cognitive impairment, terminal illness, etc.); (3) participants in other research studies; (4) hypertensive patients who had a partner taking the same antihypertensive medication.

Patients could be withdrawn from the study when the treating physician considered an increase of more than 30% of the visits originally programmed in the study. Patients who lost or did not use the MEMS and those who decided not to continue with the study at some point were also withdrawn.

In the case of abandonment/modification of the prescribed antihypertensive drug at the beginning of the study, patients were not excluded from the study. In this case, the prescribed antihypertensive agents were introduced into the MEMSs again.

## Visits and measurements

Three visits took place: the inclusion visit plus two follow-up visits after 6 and 12 months.

## Variables

The average adherence percentage (AP) was calculated using the following formula:

The individual with AP between 80% and 100% was considered adherent. The final AP of the study was considered for each patient and we study the AP accumulated at the time of the end of the follow-up (at the end of the last visit or when the patients were withdrawn from the study).

Mean AP was calculated using the MEMSs as: (a) global percentage of doses taken (global AP) – percentage of all doses taken in an established period; (b) percentage of correct days (daily AP) – percentage of days on which antihypertensive drugs were taken correctly (one daily dose); (c) percentage of correct time (time AP) – percentage of patients taking the medication at the prescribed time (dosage taken at the

recommended time, between 7 and 9 am); (d) percentage of therapeutic cover – AP of therapeutic coverage assuming a 24 hour therapeutic effect of antihypertensive drugs.

Adherence was defined as: (a) global adherence – global AP between 80 and 100%; (b) daily adherence – daily AP between 80 and 100%; (c) correct time adherence – time AP between 80 and 100%; (d) therapeutic cover adherence – therapeutic cover AP between 80 and 100%. The main endpoint was daily adherence (the efficacy of the investigated interventions was measured by this variable).

We calculated the relevance of the intervention made by reducing the absolute risk (ARR), the relative risk reduction (RRR) and the number of patients needed to treat to avoid non-adherence (NNT 1/ARR).

Different non-adherence patterns were defined15–20 according to AP as: adherence – a) absolute adherence with AP of 100%, b) masked adherence with AP >80% and daily AP of <80%, c) adherence with sporadic non-adherence when AP >80% and less than 100%, and d) over-adherence with AP >100%; non-adherence – a) absolute non-adherence when AP <50%, b) partial non-adherence when AP between 50% and 80%, c) treatment abandonment when the patient stopped taking the medication (as declared by the patient and as recorded in MEMS); other patterns (adherents or non adherents) - a) pharmacological holidays of at least 3 days without taking the medication, b) schedule/time non-adherence when the medication was not taken between 7 and 9 am, c) mixed non-adherence when two or more non-adherence patterns were met and d) white robe adherence, when the medication was taken the days before and after the visit, with non-adherence the rest of the time.

The following variables were also analyzed: age and sex, number of concomitant diseases, number of drugs consumed and mean BP (SBP and DBP). The percentage of controlled patients was also calculated (BP <140 and 90 mmHg).

## Statistical analysis

The variables were calculated and compared overall and by groups. The Paradox 3.5 database and a standard statistical computer package were used. Chi-square test, Student’s t test and McNemar test were used to compare quantitative variables for paired and unpaired data. For all hypotheses tested two-tailed p values <.05 were considered to be significant. The 95% confidence intervals were calculated.

The study was carried out following the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki. The guidelines of the European Community and the Spanish Medicines Agency were followed. The study was cataloged by the Spanish Agency of Medicine as EPA-SP UN-HTA-2015-01 and was evaluated by the Coordinating Committee of Ethics of Biomedical Research of Andalusia.

# Results
A total of 154 patients were included, and 148 hypertensive patients were valid for the analysis (96.1%). The overall mean age was 57.5 ± 9.9 years. There were 71 men (47.9%) and 77

women (52.02%) (mean age: 57.9 ± 10.3 years and 57.2 ± 9.8 years respectively (p NS). Of the 6 patients not included (4 in IG and 2 in CG) the reasons were in 2 cases due to technical problems in the readings of the MEMSs, 2 cases did not show up to the control visits; 1 moved to another city and 1 had to abandon due to illness that required long hospitalization.

Seventy-three individuals of the IG and 75 of the CG were evaluated. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in age, sex, number of diseases suffered, number of drugs consumed, initial weight, vascular risk factors and other alterations related to cardiovascular disease (Table 1).

Significant differences could be observed between the groups in global AP, AP of a single daily dose, AP in the correct schedule and AP of therapeutic coverage, in favor of the IG (Table 2). Adherence once daily was 93.15% and 86.3% in IG and 70.66% and 62.66% in CG after 6 and 12 months, respectively, the adherence percentage being significantly higher in the IG (Table 2).

The mean BP was similar at the beginning in the two groups (134.7 ± 14 mmHg in IG vs. 134.47 ± 8 mmHg in CG). Significant differences were observed in the mean decreases in BP in the IG throughout the study (final BP: 132.2 ± 12, p <.05). In the CG blood pressure was not modified throughout the follow-up (134.4 ± 11 mmHg) (Table 3).

Regarding the control of blood pressure, in the whole group at the end of the study, 77.02% (CI  70.25–83.79) of the global sample were adherent. The daily AP was 74.32% (CI 67.29–81.35). The percentage of uncontrolled BP patients at the end of the study was 28.3% (CI 21.05–35.55%).

The results by groups were as follows. At baseline 35.6% and 32% in the IG and CG respectively were controlled (p NS); at 6 months this was 20.5 and 44% (p < .05) and finally the results were 17.8% in CG (CI  9.03–26.57) and 38.6% in IG (CI 27.6–49.6) (p < .05).

The ARR at 12 months was 23.64%, giving a RRR of 63.61%. The NNT was calculated to be 4.23 patients.

When assessing the adherence patterns (Table 4), it can be observed that among adherents the pattern of sporadic non-adherence is the most frequent and among non-adherents, the partial default pattern predominates. We found other predominant patterns to be pharmacological holidays (66.3%) and non-adherence with the pill schedule (85.34%).
