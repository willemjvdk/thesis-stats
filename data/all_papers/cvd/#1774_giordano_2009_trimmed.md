## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# 2. Methods
## 2.1. Centres and patients

Cardiovascular rehabilitation departments of “Salvatore Maugeri” Foundation that have a comprehensive heart fail-

ure management organisation were selected. Five departments in Lombardy (two), Piedmont, Campania and Apulia were identified.

Patients hospitalised with confirmed diagnosis of CHF, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) b40% and at least one hospitalisation for acute HF in the previous year, were candidates for inclusion in the study. To be eligible patients had to be clinically stable with optimised oral therapy including maximally tolerated doses of both an angiotensinrenin inhibitor and beta-blocker. The stability criteria required that patients were symptomatically improved, without intravenous therapy for at least 7 days, had stable oral therapy with no dose changes for 5 days and had stable weight (no change N1 kg) for 5 days. The criteria for exclusion included non-cardiac debilitating illness such as active malignancy, severe renal insufficiency (creatinine N3 mg/dl), cognitive impairment clinically evident, myocardial infarction or revascularization procedure within the preceding 30 days, planned coronary revascularization or valvular surgery or heart transplantation.

The study was approved by the institutional ethics of Human Research Committee of Fondazione “Salvatore Maugeri”. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

# 2.2. Methods
Patients were screened for eligibility 48 h before hospital discharge. After having obtained the informed consent, patient's baseline data (Tables 1 and 2) were collected by staff nurses on standardized forms and sent to Biostatistical Unit of Pavia. Patients were then assigned randomly to receive home-based telemanagement (HBT) or usual care (UC) programme. Random permuted blocks for each center were used to allocate patients to treatment groups.

Before discharge, all patients across all centres were educated about heart failure, including advice on daily weights, daily self-measurement of blood pressure, rate of carrying out blood examinations, dietary restrictions, including sodium and fluid, and signs and symptoms of a heart failure decompensation.

Patients assigned randomly to UC were referred to their primary care physician. A structured follow-up with the cardiologist at 12 months in the hospital outpatient department and the appointment with the primary care physician within 2 weeks from the discharge were planned.

The HBT programme in details has been reported in our previous paper [17]. Home-based telemanagement is a multidisciplinary care approach referring to medical/nursing interventions made over the telephone, with the possibility to transmit an ECG trace to a workstation at each investigator site through a single Call Center. Patients assigned to the HBT strategy, received before hospital discharge a portable device (Card-Guard 2206) transferring by a fixed or mobile telephone, a one-lead trace to a receiving station, where a nurse or doctor were available 24 h, 7 days/week.

The structure of the Call Center had been implemented with new broad band technologies (HDLS). The nerve center was equipped with 4 Hewlett Packard servers (with back-up hardware to avoid activity interruptions), a WEB server for Internet connection, firewall for data security, a computerized call center, 15 LAN workstations with 4 printers (over a network) and a central fax-maker. Terminals had been implemented in the 5 departments, functionally linked with the Call Center, configured to share the application programme interface of the Central Station with “on site/on line” license. The Call Center provided the technological and organisational support, while health the cardiologists and the nurses of the hospital managed the clinical activity. Every telephone call was recorded by the computerized call center. The system information flow was the following: a phone call arrived from the patient, automatically identified through the stored telephone number. Then the phone call was addressed to a free operator who recalls the patient's data and activated the “new call procedure”, inserting new data. At this point the ECG trace could be received and the user was put in contact with the cardiologist or the nurse on duty (a three actor's call took place) who was connected to the central data base through Internet. The specialist or the nurse examined the stored informatic clinical report providing consultation or nursing triage. At the end the reported ECG trace and all patient's data were transferred to the web-server and were available “on the net” in the informatic clinical report, in an anonymous way and in cryptography for password owners only.

Telemanagement included two different procedures:

1. Scheduled appointments (telemonitoring), were done every week or every 15 days for patients with severe (III–IV NYHA) or moderate HF (II NYHA). During these appointments the trained hospital nurse carried out a standardised interview on general clinical condition of the patients and

dietary treatment (daily intake of fluids, patient's knowledge of fluid restriction, weight surveillance, salt and alcohol intake, intake of analgesics and smoking habits). The nurse asked about the self-measurement of weight and blood pressure, registered the episodes of hypotension (dizziness with a systolic blood pressure b90 mmHg). The patients were asked the names and the doses of their prescribed drugs and whether they took them regularly. If necessary, the patients sent the ECG trace by telephone. The sent trace was compared with the basal one, to show or to exclude the presence of arrhythmias as important information for the patients' follow-up; the ECG trace also permitted to check the heart rate before to increase or to reduce the beta-blocking dosage as pre-planned by the physician. During the subsequent telemonitoring phase, the nurse reinforced the initial educational intervention and offered strategies to improve patient's compliance.

2. Occasional appointments (teleassistance) were done when the patient, in the presence of symptoms or signs of possible decompensation (as systolic blood pressure b90 mmHg, increase in weight N2 kg etc.) or with any doubt about therapy, called the nurse. The operative flow-chart is the same for telemonitoring with particular attention to the symptoms and signs referred by the patients.

In both telemonitoring and teleassistance at the end of the call phone the nurse took one of these actions: – in presence of stable conditions, fixed a new scheduled appointment, – determine drug modification pre-planned with the cardiologist or with general practitioner – asked for further investigations or scheduled cardiological consultation, – contacted the GP and or the cardiologist of the patients in presence of ECG trace modifications or of signs or symptoms of hemodynamic instability. The cardiologist supervised implementation of therapy proposed by nurse and delegated the right to adjust treatment with specific drugs, as diuretics, ACE inhibitors and beta-blockers. Only the cardiologist and/or the patient's GP could decide to send the patients to the emergency or to a cardiology department. Once a week the cardiologist and the nurse met together to sum up a clinical course of the enrolled patients. In that occasion the cardiologist supervised all the calls received in the previous week. The nurses underwent tailored education and training on heart failure, before they were considered competent to make independent decisions.

Clinical stable condition was defined as absence of: orthopnea, ankle oedema, N2 kg increase of body weight, N1.5 mg/dl serum creatinine (or 2.5 mg/dl in patients with chronic renal insufficiency), diuretic dosage increase in the previous 48 h.

# 2.3. Outcome measures
The primary outcome was the frequency of unplanned hospital readmission for cardiovascular reasons during 365 days after randomisation. The assumption was that the

primary effect of HBT would be to reduce the rate of new readmission. Secondary outcomes included hospitalisation for HF, the occurrence of episodes of hemodynamic instabilization and cardiovascular mortality.

The data and the cause of readmission were obtained from the GP and confirmed by hospital records. Episodes of clinical instability were confirmed by the GP.

Cardiovascular deaths were ascertained through the GP or hospital records.

## 2.4. Sample size

From the results of our previous study [17], we assumed that one-year hospital readmission rate would be 24% in the HBT group and 36% in the UC group. Based on 0.8 power to detect a significant difference (p = 0.05, two-sided) 230 patients were required for each study group. The power calculations appeared robust to a 3% rate of discontinuation of HBT.

## 2.5. Statistical analysis

Analyses were conducted according to the intention-totreat approach. Baseline characteristics were expressed as mean and standard deviation or percentages. Characteristics of the two study groups were compared by the unpaired t test for the continuous variables and by chi-square test for categorical variables.

Outcome measures (rate of readmission and cardiovascular mortality) were compared using Mantel–Haenszel chi-square and expressed as relative risk (RR) with 95% confidence interval [CI]. Time to first readmission was compared using the log–rank test.

To assess the independent effects of management strategy on readmission-free survival and cardiovascular mortality we used Cox-proportional hazard model. The following baseline variables, potential correlates of the primary endpoint, were included in the model as covariates: age (continuous

variable), gender, aetiology (ischemic heart disease vs. other), NYHA functional classification (II vs. III–IV), number of HF-related admissions in the previous year (1–2 vs. N 2), number of comorbidities (0–3 vs. N3), LVEF (continuous variable), peak VO2 (continuous variable), sodium, urea, creatinine (continuous variables), use of digitalis and use of beta-blockers.

A p value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.

Patients who died without hospital readmission were considered as censored observation at the time of death.

Urgent heart transplantation was classified as equivalent to cardiovascular death. All patients who died from no cardiac causes and those who underwent elective cardiac transplantation or other surgery procedures were considered as censored observations.

The number needed to treat (NNT) was estimated by oneyear readmission rate in UC group and adjusted for the hazard ratio [18,19].

Statistical analyses were conducted with the SAS System version 8.2 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina).

## 2.6. Cost analysis

Cost of readmissions during the follow-up was calculated using Medicare Diagnosis-Related-Group (DRG) reimbursement. In the HBT programme the costs were classified as either fixed or variable. Fixed costs included equipments purchase and installation, installation of telecommunication lines, monthly line charges, maintenance costs. The annual equivalent costs of equipments were calculated incorporating depreciation and opportunity cost over a period of 5 years. Variable costs included Call Center services, nurses and cardiologist's costs and telecommunication costs. The average annual salaries for nurse and cardiologist were obtained from Salvatore Maugeri Foundation and reduced to a wage per minute. This wage per minute value was multiplied by the recorded duration of consultations to calculate the total costs of health service staff.

Total costs (fixed and variable) were then divided by the duration (days) of follow-up in order to calculate the daily cost and the mean cost per patient of the intervention. The costs are expressed in Euro.

# 3. Results
A total of 620 patients were assessed for eligibility over a period of 24 months, from January 2002. Of these, 112 (18%) met one of the exclusion criteria and 48 (7.7%) refused to participate (Fig. 1).

Four hundred-sixty patients were therefore randomised; one patient in UC group and 4 in HBT group were lost to follow-up. The median duration of follow-up was 296+/ − 91 days.

The baseline characteristics of this population are shown in Tables 1 and 2.

The randomisation groups differed significantly only with regard to use of digitalis and beta-blockers, which was respectively higher and lower in the UC group. It is important to note, however, that both variables were identified a priori as covariates in the study protocol and included in the multivariate analysis. On the whole, although 60% reported well-controlled symptoms (NYHA class II) at the time of randomisation, several variables indicated a poor prognosis. For instance, 50% of patients had 2 or more HF-related hospitalisations in the previous year, 78% of patients were aged N65 years (32% N70 years), ischemic heart disease was common, LVEF was severely depressed (b25%) in 47% of patients, mean systolic blood pressure was low and mean VO2 peak reduced. Patients were managed intensively as demonstrated by the frequent use of ACE inhibitors and beta-blockers.

# 3.2. Outcomes
During one-year follow-up, all-cause hospital readmissions occurred in 67 patients in HBT group and 96 patients in UC group (RR = 0.57, 95% [CI]: 0.39–0.84; p= 0.03). Fiftyfive patients (24%) in HBT group and 83 patients (36%) in the UC group had at least one readmission due to cardiovascular reasons (RR = 0.56, 95% [CI]: 0.38, 0.82; p = 0.003).

These patients accounted for 91 readmissions in HBT group and 142 in UC group ( p { < } 0 . 0 1 ), with a 36% decrease in total number of readmissions. The likehood of hospital readmission at 1 year was 28% in HBT group compared to 42% in UC group (Fig. 2).

The number of patients experiencing HF readmissions was 43 (19%) in HBT group and 73 in UC group (RR = 0.49, 95% [CI]: 0.31–0.76; p= 0.0001).

Fifty-five patients in HBT group (24%) and 93 in UC group (41%) experienced episodes of hemodynamic instability ( p { < } 0 . 0 0 1 ). These patients accounted for 101 episodes in HBT group and 147 in UC group ( p { < } 0 . 0 0 1 ), for a 31% decrease in these events.

One-year total mortality was 9% HBT group and 14% in UC group.

One patient in HBT group and 4 in UC group had urgent heart transplantation.
