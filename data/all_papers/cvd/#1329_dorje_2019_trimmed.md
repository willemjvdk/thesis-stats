# Methods
## Study design and participants

SMART-CR/SP was a parallel-group, single-blind, randomised, controlled trial done in a large tertiary hospital in Shanghai, China. Full details of the study protocol have been previously published.17 However, we summarise it herein, including two protocol deviations. Although we initially anticipated that the requirement for owning a smartphone and using WeChat would exclude many people older than 70 years, during consumer engagement before the trial it became apparent that this would not be the case, so the upper age limit was removed. We also amended the proposed 12-month follow-up process (described in the Procedures subsection).

All consecutive patients aged 18 years or older with documented coronary heart disease (including myocardial infarction and unstable or stable angina) who were treated with percutaneous coronary interventions during their index admission were eligible for inclusion. Potentially eligible patients were identified from the Cardiology Department’s daily list of percutaneous coronary interventions and invited to participate in the trial by study coordinators. Participants were required to own an operational smartphone, have an active WeChat account or be willing to create one, and have sufficient Chinese language proficiency to enable communication with the cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention coach via WeChat. Participants were excluded if they had contraindications to exercise rehabilitation, an inability to operate a smartphone for the purpose of the trial (eg, vision, hearing, and cognitive or dexterity impairment), no internet access at their place of residence, or preexisting comorbid disease with a life expectancy of less than 1 year. All participants provided written informed consent. Ethics approval was granted by Fudan University Zhongshan Hospital (Shanghai, China; B2016–058) and Curtin University (Perth, WA, Australia; HRE2016–0120).

## Randomisation and masking

Participants were randomly assigned (1:1) to either SMART-CR/SP (SMART-CR/SP group) or usual care (control group) by permuted block randomisation (block size of 10). The randomisation sequence was computergenerated by staff from the study coordinating centre at Curtin University who were not involved with the recruitment of study participants. To maintain blinding of research personnel involved in follow-up assessments to group allocation, participants received a WeChat message before each follow-up visit, to remind them not to reveal their allocation to study personnel. Study personnel who helped participants to set up the SMART-CR/SP system on their smartphone or provided technology training before the commencement of the trial were not involved in assessments.

## Procedures

Following randomisation, baseline clinical characteristics were extracted from patients’ medical records and demographic characteristics were collected using a questionnaire to document smoking status (never smoked, current smoker, or ex-smoker), education level (number of years of education), and employment status (working full time, unemployed, retired, or other and household income <¥5000 or ≥¥5000).

The SMART-CR/SP system involved a smartphonebased home cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention programme delivered via the WeChat platform for patients with coronary heart disease after percutaneous coronary interventions (figure 1). This novel system employed WeChat’s powerful functions in social media communication, online education, physical activity tracking, blood-pressure and heart-rate monitoring (linked to an external monitor), wireless data transfer, and multimedia—to support the delivery of a comprehensive home cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention programme. The patient-centred programme consisted of core components of contemporary cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention, including education about cardiovascular health and disease, physical activity prescription, healthy nutritional advice, and support for medication adherence, psychological wellbeing, and modification of coronary heart disease risk factors. A simplified and culturally sensitive WeChat-based education programme consisting of 32 cartoon-format modules and 32 cartoon pictures (accompanied by motivational messages) addressing coronary heart disease knowledge and awareness was developed by the research team (appendix p 1). Each module consisted of 25–30 slides of cartoon images and introduced a key knowledge theme through dialogue between a patient avatar (Mr Li) and the SMART-CR/SP coach avatar (Dr Kang, an abbreviation of rehabilitation in Chinese). This programme covered a broad range of topics relevant to coronary heart disease (appendix p 2) and the contents were based on cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention recommendations

for coronary heart disease from contemporary clinical guidelines.6–8 Before the trial, consumers and cardiology staff reviewed the modules, which were subsequently refined before the commencement of the study. To facilitate physical activity monitoring and supervision, SMART-CR/SP used WeChat’s built-in pedometer function to monitor participants’ step counts. Individualised exercise prescription and remote supervision were provided on the basis of regular reviews of monitoring data. A WeChat-interfaced blood-pressure and heart-rate monitor (C-health XY-10, Sky Innovation Technology, Shanghai, China) was used for remote monitoring and management. Furthermore, support for medication adherence and risk factor modification, such as dietary change, lipid control, and smoking cessation were provided by the cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention coach via WeChat-based consultations, as required by participants.

To maximise participant engagement, readings for blood pressure, heart rate, and daily step count were automatically transmitted to a secure data portal and reviewed by the cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention coach on a regular basis (once a week during the intensive phase and every fortnight during the step-down phase). Individualised feedback and cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention recommendations were provided after each data review, and additional alerts were sent to participants when key measurement readings such as blood pressure and daily step counts were outside the target levels (blood pressure <140/90 mm Hg; daily step count >10 000). No target was given for heart rate.

Following each alert, a WeChat message was sent to participants to reinforce behaviour change. In extreme cases (systolic blood pressure ≥180 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure ≥120 mm Hg, or both, or resting heart rate <55 beats per min or >100 beats per min), automated alerts generated by the data portal were sent to the cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention coach, who then reviewed the alerts within 24 h, and a WeChat-based interview or an outpatient clinical visit was arranged accordingly.

Participants in the control group received standard care, as provided by their community doctors and cardiologists after hospital discharge. In China, current care following percutaneous coronary interventions typically involves brief inpatient health education provided by a ward nurse, medication management, and ad-hoc follow-up visits to a cardiologist or other health-care providers according to the patient’s self-assessment of their own cardiovascular health. In the control group, WeChat was only used for sending follow-up visit reminders. In addition to standard care, participants assigned to the intervention group received a 2-month intensive SMART-CR/SP programme, followed by a 4-month step-down stage. During the intensive phase, participants received four educational modules per week via WeChat. In the step-down phase, participants received only two cartoon pictures with key motivational message per week.

At baseline, 2 months, and 6 months, participants from both groups were assessed at the hospital by research coordinators blinded to participants’ allocation status. During each assessment: functional capacity was assessed

by 6-min walk test, done in a secured 30-m straight corridor of a cardiology ward, according to a standard protocol;18 participants’ knowledge and awareness of coronary heart disease was evaluated by a questionnaire (score range 0–32; higher scores indicated better knowledge) developed from the Heart Disease Fact Questionnaire19 and a validated Chinese coronary heart disease knowledge questionnaire;20 resting heart rate was measured by pulse oximetry (Yuwell X303, Yuyue Medical Equipment & Supply Co, Danyang, China); blood pressure was measured by auscultation using a mercury sphygmomanometer after 10 min of seated rest; cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention needs were assessed with a modified assessment tool;21 plasma glucose (mmol/L) and full lipid profile (mmol/L) were assayed after an overnight fast in the hospital’s pathology laboratory; adherence to cardioprotective medications was self-reported; body mass index (BMI) and waist-to-hip ratio measured according to standard protocols; psychosocial wellbeing was assessed by the Generalised Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale22 and Patient Health Questionnaire 9-item scale;23 and quality of life was measured with the 12-item Short-Form Health Survey.24 Behaviour change for smoking status (self-reported), dietary habits (WHO Steps Instrument),25 and physical activity (International Physical Activity Questionnaire)26 were also reported.

Additionally, at 6 months, participants in the intervention group completed a questionnaire administered via WeChat to assess the acceptability and perceived utility of SMART-CR/SP (appendix p 4). We proposed, in the protocol paper, to do WeChat-based video interviews at the 12-month follow-up. However, we subsequently determined this would have created an unreasonable burden on participants given the volume and complexity of the assessments. These assessments had been made in person at the earlier time-points, which was a more participant-friendly approach than a prolonged video interview, but in-person interviews weren’t feasible at 12 months. Therefore we limited the 12-month assessment to full lipid profile, smoking status, and adherence to core cardioprotective medications. Blood samples were collected and analysed by accredited local laboratories and self-reported medication adherence and smoking status were collected by WeChat-based video interview.

All data were collected and managed using the REDCap electronic data tool hosted at Fudan University. Data were de-identified and were accessible only by researchers required to enter or analyse data. All researchers underwent data safety training before the study.

# Outcomes
The primary outcome was change in functional capacity from baseline to 2 months and 6 months, measured by 6-min walk distance. Secondary outcomes were: participants’ knowledge and awareness of coronary heart disease; resting heart rate; systolic blood pressure; cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention needs; full

lipid profile; adherence to cardioprotective medications; smoking status; obesity (presented as BMI and waist-tohip ratio); psychosocial wellbeing; and quality of life.21 Additional outcomes included fasting plasma glucose, dietary habits, low physical activity, major adverse cardiac events including death, recurrent cardiovascular events, and SMART-CR/SP programme-related safety issues. Analyses of changes in dietary habits and physical activity and plasma glucose will be reported elsewhere.

## Statistical analysis

A 25-m improvement in the 6-min walk distance is considered clinically meaningful.27 We estimated a sample size of 246 patients were required to have 90% power (two-tailed) to detect a mean difference of 25 m (SD 60) between groups for the primary outcome with a significance level of 0·05. To allow for 20% attrition, a sample of 312 patients (156 per group) was was estimated to be necessary. According to a post-hoc calculation, this sample size would also provide 80% power (two-tailed) to detect a mean difference of 2 points (5) in knowledge on the coronary heart disease questionnaire score (0–32 points) between groups (effect size=0·4), and 90% power to detect a clinically significant increase by 16% in adherence to four core cardioprotective medications in the treatment group, assuming 75% adherence in the control group.28

Analyses for both primary and secondary outcomes were done according to the intention-to-treat principle. Maximum-likelihood estimation methods were used in the models to account for missing data. The changes in 6-min walk distance between groups at 2 months and 6 months were analysed using generalised linear mixed models (GLMM) with identity link and random subject effects, adjusting for baseline, and included data available on all randomly allocated patients at baseline and followup visits. To test model fit, residuals were checked for normality and homoscedasticity using histograms and normality plots and predicted estimates were plotted against residuals. Results were summarised using adjusted between-group mean differences and 95% CIs in the 6-min walk distance from baseline to 2 months and baseline to 6 months. Raw mean differences and SDs of the outcomes at each timepoint were reported. To examine the consistency and heterogeneity of the intervention on the change in 6-min walk distance at 6 months, an exploratory subgroup analysis comparing clinically relevant factors was done using GLMM.

Continuous secondary outcomes were analysed similarly to the primary outcome. Changes in adherence to all four core cardioprotective medications and smoking cessation between groups at 2 months, 6 months, and 12 months were analysed using GLMM with logit link and random subject effects, adjusting for baseline. Results were summarised as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs. Frequency distributions were also used to summarise these data.

All statistical analyses were two-sided and p values smaller than 0·05 indicated significance. Analyses were performed with STATA version 15.1. This trial is registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, number ChiCTR-INR-16009598.

# Results
Between Nov 17, 2016, and Mar 18, 2017, 567 inpatients with coronary heart disease who had undergone

percutaneous coronary interventions were invited to participate in the study, of whom 139 did not meet the inclusion criteria, 46 declined to participate, 10 did not complete baseline assessments, and 60 were excluded for other reasons (figure 2). 312 eligible patients were randomly assigned to SMART-CR/SP (n=156; SMART-CR/SP group) or usual care (n=156; control group). A confirmation WeChat message was received from all participants within 24 h of the first WeChat article introducing the study being done. 271 (87%) of 312 patients completed the 2-month in-person follow-up and 265 (85%) completed the 6-months in-person follow-up; all participants completed the 12-month WeChat video interview. The final 12-month follow-up assessments were completed on May 21, 2018. Participants in the two groups were well matched at baseline (table 1).

Changes in 6-min walk distance from baseline to both 2 months and 6 months were significantly different between the groups, with a greater improvement in the SMART-CR/SP group than in the control group (table 2). At 2 months, the mean between-group difference in change of 6-min walk distance from baseline was 20·64 m (95% CI 7·50–33·77; p=0·034). This improvement was maintained at 6 months, with a mean betweengroup difference of 22·29 m (8·19–36·38; p=0·027).

Participants in the SMART-CR/SP group had significantly higher coronary heart disease knowledge scores than those in the control group. At the 2-month follow-up, the difference in coronary heart disease knowledge score was 4·02 (95% CI 2·84 to 5·44; p<0·0001). This improvement was maintained at 6 months, with a between-group score difference of 4·09 (3·12 to 5·06; p<0·0001). Although no differences in resting blood pressure and heart rate were observed at 2 months between the groups, at the 6-month follow-up, systolic blood pressure and heart rate were significantly lower in the SMART-CR/SP group than in the control group (adjusted mean difference in systolic blood pressure –9·41 mm Hg [–19·38 to –0·56; p=0·029] and in heart rate –6·08 beats per min [–11·9 to –0·29; p=0·039]). Participants in the SMART-CR/SP group also had a higher score on the cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention needs assessment tool compared with the usual care group. The difference in cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention needs score was 2·62 (1·81 to 3·46; p<0·0001) at 2 months, and 2·32 (1·38 to 3·24; p<0·0001) at 6 months (table 2).

No differences in lipid concentrations were observed between groups at 2 months and 6 months. However, at the 12-month follow-up, both total and LDL cholesterol were significantly lower in the SMART-CR/SP group than in the control group (adjusted mean difference in total cholesterol –0·22 mmol/L [95% CI –0·40 to –0·04; p=0·018] and in LDL cholesterol –0·20 mmol/L [–0·36 to –0·04; p=0·016]; table 2). Furthermore, participants in the SMART-CR/SP group were more likely to be adherent to the four core cardioprotective medications at 2 months (OR 1·99, 1·98 to 2·05;

p=0·0048), 6 months (1·79, 1·76 to 1·87, p=0·019), and 12 months (1·82, 1·78 to 1·93, p=0·011; table 2). There was no difference in smoking cessation rate between the two groups at each follow-up visit (table 2; appendix p 3).

No difference between groups was found at either 2 months or 6 months for the remaining secondary outcomes (BMI, waist-to-hip ratio, psychosocial wellbeing, or quality of life; table 2).

In an exploratory subgroup analysis of functional capacity (6-min walk distance) at 6 months, no significant heterogeneity of the intervention effects were observed according to age (≤65 years vs >65 years); sex (male vs female participants); discharge diagnosis (acute coronary syndrome vs non-acute coronary syndrome), hypertension (yes vs no), diabetes (yes vs no), smoking history (yes vs no), and number of stents implanted during admission (<2 vs ≥2; appendix p 3).

125 (80%) of 156 participants in the SMART-CR/SP group completed the WeChat-based feedback survey at 6 months. All surveyed participants stated that they had received all the modules or messages and found the SMART-CR/SP programme useful. 119 (95%) of 125 participants indicated that they had read more than 75% of the modules and messages, 87 (70%) had shared the modules and messages with family members or friends, and 119 (95%) stated that the SMART-CR/SP programme improved their understanding of cardiac rehabilitation and secondary prevention. Furthermore, the majority reported that the SMART-CR/SP programme was helpful for healthy eating (122 [98%] participants), adherence to physical activity (120 [96%] participants), and medication adherence (123 [98%] participants) and most participants wanted to continue to use the programme beyond 6 months (123 [98%] participants; table 3).

No adverse events or SMART-CR/SP programmerelated safety issues were recorded during the study.
