# Methods
## Study design

This study used an open-labelled randomised controlled trial with parallel-group design, including two arms (intervention and control), and was conducted to compare the effectiveness of an automated SMS-based reminder system on medication adherence and health outcomes among ACS patients. The primary outcome was the ratio of adherent patients to complete cardiac medication therapy at eight weeks post-discharge. Secondary outcomes were heart functional status, and ACS-related hospital readmission and death rates.

## Study population

In this study, patients enrolled in a tertiary teaching hospital in Kuala Lumpur during an admission for ACS. The automated SMS-based reminder system was introduced to subjects prior to discharge from the cardiology ward, and subjects were registered for the study based on their acceptance. Patients were excluded from the trial if they did not have cell phones to receive related text-messages;

were not discharged during the specified study timeline or were discharged to a care facility or transferred to another health care institution; were illiterate or unable to read text-messages; were not available for the two-month period of the study (including being unavailable by phone and/or travelling out of the country); or had been diagnosed with cognitive impairment so that the informed consent process might be incomprehensible.

Sample size and randomisation. According to the literature, non-adherence to medications has been documented to occur in more than 60% of cardiovascular patients.1 Based on a previous study,14 it was considered that the true probability of adherence to cardiac medications among the intervention group would improve to 75% at the end of the study. Therefore, 30 patients should be studied in each group to be able to reject the null hypothesis that the adherence rates for both arms are equal with probability (power) 0.8. The Type I error probability associated with the test of this null hypothesis was 0.05. The study population in this study consisted of all eligible participants in the cardiology ward enrolled between 23 January 2013–23 February 2013. There were 74 ACS patients eligible, of which 62 consented to participate in this study. Sixty-two subjects were randomly assigned to control (n=31) and intervention groups (n=31).

Blinding. Due to the nature of the intervention, it was impossible to blind either the subjects or the researcher to the study group assignment. To prevent potential bias in the results of the study, all participants were visited by cardiologists and cardiac rehabilitation specialists who were unaware of the study group assignment to assess the participants’ heart function status based on the New York Heart Association Functional Classification (NYHA) at the endpoint of the study.

Control. Patients in the control group received the usual care for ACS post-discharge including cardiac rehabilitation and follow-up appointments with the cardiologist, usually occurring at six or eight weeks following discharge.

Intervention. The participants randomised to the intervention group received text-message reminders based on the following template before every medication intake, starting the day after discharge:

‘‘[Mr/Ms] [Patient’s Name], please take [Medication Quantity] tablet of [Medication Name] at [Time]’’.

The length and number of characters in the SMS had to be short and it was important to be sure that the message would be simple, practical and easy to understand. When the course of medication was completed (patients were

given a 30-day dosage), a message was sent reminding patients to come to the hospital and have their prescribed cardiac medications refilled. The SMS reminder service was continued until two months after discharge. The twomonth duration for the intervention was chosen based on findings of previous studies, which found that ACS patients are most susceptible to discontinue their medications between one and three months after discharge.4,16,17 This suggests that a reminder service to overcome barriers faced by the patient and the health care system may be most advantageous within this time period.

Automated SMS-based reminder system. The system is a web-based software with no specific hardware dependency, thus offering maximum portability and ease of use. The system consists of various parts which are responsible for gathering and managing the information related

to the patients and their medications, storing data, scheduling, sending text-messages and recording delivery reports. All these tasks are handled automatically to minimise the manual effort needed. It also provides additional features such as query, advanced search and report generation which can be exported to many standard formats.

Under the system workflow, the first step is to add patient data and notifications. The scheduler service will then execute the SMS sender program at pre-scheduled times. It will in turn access the patient information database to generate correspondent text-messages according to the desired template as well as notification data. Finally, a connection to an external SMS gateway will be established to send generated messages. The SMS sender program is also responsible for collecting delivery reports and updating the database (Figure 1).

## Data collection

Data were collected from December 2012–April 2013 including three stages:

Stage one. In December 2012, the system was developed with the aim of improving medication adherence and health outcomes by sending SMS reminders to the patients’ cell phones. During this phase, a variety of test scenarios were analysed and, consequently, the required changes and fixes were applied in order to achieve the desired functions.

Stage two. Between 23 January 2013–23 February 2013, eligible hospitalised ACS patients were recruited. Patients were randomly assigned to the control or intervention groups. The required information, including participants’ demographics and prescribed cardiac medications, was obtained at recruitment and inserted into the system. Then corresponding reminders were generated and sent to each participant in the intervention group before every cardiac medication intake in an eight-week programme. The researcher also followed up with the participants in the SMS group via telephone calls once per two weeks during the study to reassure the delivery of text-messages, to enquire whether any emergency readmission was needed as well as to show up for their appointments.

Stage three. In April 2013, at the end of the study, eight weeks post-discharge, all outcomes were assessed using the appropriate instruments during follow-up visits. In order to prevent any bias and interpretation variability in the NYHA classifications at the follow-up visits, two specialists including cardiologist and cardiac rehabilitation specialist who were unaware of the study group assignment were asked to visit all participants and categorised them according to the NYHA classification. ACS-related readmissions were defined as any readmission due to a chest pain or recurrent ACS events based on the hospital patients’ records. The patients were then asked to complete the eight-item Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8-item) that is a well-validated self-report questionnaire with good predictive validity to assess medication-taking behaviour and adherence.18,19 Selfreported adherence measurement is simple, inexpensive and the most useful method in the clinical setting.20 The MMAS-8-item instrument measured the primary outcome, which was non-adherence to cardiac medications due to reasons like forgetfulness, carelessness, feeling better, or feeling worse.21

At the endpoint of the study, participants who received SMS-reminders to take their cardiac medications completed a survey about their perception of the SMSreminders which consist of four items.

## Statistical analysis

All data were analysed using the computer program Statistical Packages for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 21. The significance level in this study was α=0.05. Patients’ characteristics were compared between the study groups (control and intervention) using independent samples t-tests for continuous variables or χ2 tests for categorical variables. The primary outcome from MMAS-eight-item provided data including high adherence (score of 8), medium adherence (score of 6 to <8) and low adherence (scores of <6). Secondary outcomes included patient’s NYHA classification (Class I: no symptoms, II: mild symptoms, III: marked limitation and IV: severe limitations), as well as death and hospital readmission rates. All outcome results were provided in categorical data. The χ2 test was used to determine the statistical significance of the observed association in a cross-tabulation. Cronbach’s alpha reliability coefficient was used to assess the internal consistency of the medication adherence questionnaire for cardiac patients with an acceptance value of 0.7.

As there were barriers like unexpected hospital readmissions and death in this trial that prevented patients from following the whole study, an intention-to-treat analysis was performed assuming that patients who were missing were categorised under the low medication adherence level as well as the last classification of heart functional status in the patient’s most recent document.

## Ethical approval

Ethical approval was obtained from Institutional Review Board, hospital ethical committee of the study setting (reference number: 956.8). Study was carried out in accordance to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent also was obtained from each participant.

# Results
## Characteristics of participants

Characteristics of all 62 participants were shown in Table 1. All variables were similar between study groups with no statistically significant differences.

## Participants flow and follow-up

During the recruitment time, a total of 74 ACS patients were discharged from the study setting. Among them, 10 patients refused to participate in the study, and two of them were excluded due to travel commitments. Hence, 62 (~84 %) eligible patients consented to participate in the study. There were two drop-outs in the control group due to death. Finally, 60 (~ 97 %) patients completed their participation in this study (Figure 2).

A total of 9066 SMS reminders were sent to all 31 patients in the intervention group. From the total number, 7946 (87.64%) successful and 128 (1.41 %) failure delivery reports were received. There were no records of delivery status for 992 (10.94 %) messages. The last follow-up visit occurred on 18 April 2013. Of the 62 patients, 60 (~ 97%) were visited almost eight weeks post-discharge

while the other two patients had died due to ACS complications. Of the 31 patients in the intervention group, all (100%) received telephone calls every two weeks during the study.

## Medication adherence

For the primary outcome of this study, a self-report of medication adherence (MMAS-8-item) was used to measure the adherence level at the endpoint of the study. The objective was to test if there is a significant difference in medication adherence level between the control and intervention groups. It was found that 65% of participants in the intervention group who received SMSreminders to take their medications had a high adherence level compared to only 13% of those in the usual care group. More than half of the patients in the control group had a low adherence level to their cardiac medications (Table 2).

There was a highly significant difference in medication adherence levels between the control and intervention groups, χ2 (2) = 18.614, p<0.001. The Cramer’s Phi (φ) or V was 0.548, and the relative risk (calculated for low and high level of adherence by study groups) indicated that the risk of being low adherent among the control group is 4.09 times greater than the intervention group (relative risk =4.09, 95% CI 1.82–9.18) which indicates a large magnitude of effect.

## Heart functional status

Another study objective was to test if there is a significant difference in patients’ heart functional status between the control and intervention groups. Around 84% of participants in the intervention group had no symptoms and no limitations in ordinary physical activity compared to 32% of those in usual care group.

There was a highly significant difference in heart functional status between the control and intervention groups, χ2 (1) = 16.957, p<0.001. The Cramer’s Phi (φ) or V was 0.523, which indicates a large magnitude of effect (Table 2).

In order to determine any significant changes in NYHA classification in each group over the study period, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test was performed. It did not elicit statistically significant changes in NYHA classification among intervention group (Z<0.001, p=1). Indeed, more than half of the patients who received reminders were categorised in NYHA class I both pre- and post-intervention. However, there were significant negative changes in NYHA classification among the control group (Z= −2.828, p=0.005). More than half of the patients in the control group who were assigned in NYHA class I at the study baseline were categorised in class II and III at the endpoint of the study.

## Hospital readmission and death rates

For the ACS-related readmissions, the p-value was 0.056, indicating the possibility of the difference in readmission rate between study groups. There were two death events among patients in the control group compared to no deaths in the intervention group. The p-value of the difference in death events between study groups for the Fisher’s Exact was 0.246, indicating there was no statistically significant difference in death events between study groups (Table 2).

## Participants’ characteristics and medication adherence level

The study of the association between participants’ characteristics and medication adherence level to adjust for confounders showed no significant differences.

## Patients’ perception of the applied system

The majority of participants in the intervention group (29 or 93.5%) said the system was useful and 64.5 % felt that it had helped them in taking their medications. They also perceived that the system was supportive and helpful in preventing misunderstanding of their medication regimen. Over 80% of participants requested for the SMS reminders to be continued (Table 3).
