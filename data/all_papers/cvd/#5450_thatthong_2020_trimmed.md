# Methods
## Population and sample selection

## Study setting

The study was carried out in a semi-urban area of Ratchaburi Province due to the high incidence of hypertension. At-risk hypertensive people in this study were both male and female, aged 19–59 years, with body mass index (BMI) ≥ 18.5 kg/m2 . They also had pre-hypertension condition [systolic BP level of 120–139 mmHg or diastolic blood BP level of 80–89 mmHg, according to the Seventh Report of the Joint National Committee on Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Treatment of High Blood Pressure (JNC VII) criteria] (Chobanian et al. 2003; [No authors listed] 1999), systolic BP level ≥ 140 mmHg or diastolic BP level ≥ 90 mmHg (according to the definition of hypertension of the Thai Hypertension Society, 2012), or had at least one family member with hypertension. The education level of the participants was secondary school or higher; those whose education was below secondary school should have basic skills in using the application (LINE) on a smartphone. The participants should

own a smartphone that was compatible with the application used in this study. All participants needed to be able to participate in the study for a 10-week duration. Exclusion criteria were subjects diagnosed with secondary hypertension, diabetes mellitus, diagnosis of cardiovascular disease, and impaired renal function; subjects with diuretic drug treatment; recipients of medication affecting urine sodium excretion or urine output volume; and pregnant or breast feeding subjects.

## Study design

The sample size was calculated by mean difference with standard deviation (knowledge improvement) at α = 0.05 (twotailed test), calculated β of 0.2, and estimated power 80%. Calculating for a 20% drop-out rate, we would need to have approximately 30 at-risk people for hypertension per group.

The control and intervention groups were randomly selected. The unit of randomization was the at-risk people for hypertension at workplaces in Ratchaburi Province. Of the 67 atrisk hypertensive people, 32 people were randomized to the intervention group and 35 people to the control group. This study consisted of a preparation phase (2 weeks duration) and an implementation phase (8 weeks duration). In the 2-week preparation phase, pre-test knowledge questionnaires were conducted in both groups. All baseline measurements were conducted for all participants: anthropometry, BP level, and baseline urine sodium excretion with adapted 24-h recall on the same day as 24-h urine collection. In the 8-week implementation phase, the control group received a general healthcare counseling program in weeks 2 and 6.

The intervention group received a sodium reduction counseling program in weeks 2 and 6. The intervention group also received sodium reduction key messages from the innovative technology (LINE) on their smartphones twice a week. The key messages used in the innovative technology show evidently good content validity (I-CVI = 0.96) and relatively high internal consistency (coefficient α = 0.958).

The post-measurements were conducted for both groups in week 10 (anthropometry, urine sodium excretion, adapted 24-h recall on the same day as 24-h urine collection, and BP level). Post-test knowledge questionnaires were also collected in week 10.

## Data collection

The instruments for data collection were questionnaires, anthropometric assessment, clinical assessment, and dietary intake. The general characteristics of participants were interviewed using the questionnaire, including sex, age, marital status, religion, education level, occupation, LINE application usage, frequency of LINE usage, and health information. Anthropometric assessment was measured by trained research assistants. The measurement was recorded twice

during the study, at pre-intervention and at post-intervention. The BMI was calculated. Clinical assessment (BP level, 24-h urine collection) was collected from participants twice, at preintervention and at post-intervention. The total urine volume was measured in both phases of data collection. The urine sodium and creatinine excretion from the 24-h urine collection was analyzed. The validity of the 24-h urine collection was assessed by the amount of total urine volume that was higher than 500 mL/day. The urine creatinine excretion should be higher than 10 mg/kg of body weight or at least 4 mmol/day in females, and higher than 15 mg/kg of body weight or at least 6 mmol/day in males (Gibson 2005; National Centre for Social Research 2011; Rhodes et al. 2013). All participants were also asked about incompletion or discarding of urine collection. Dietary intake from the adapted 24-h recall on the same day as 24-h urine collection was calculated using INMUCAL-Nutrients V.3 (database version: NB.1.4.1). The dietary records were performed twice, at pre-intervention and at post-intervention. Sodium intake was recorded to 0.01 mg/ day and energy intake was recorded to 0.01 Kcal/day.

## Statistical analysis

The samples in this study were two independent groups (intervention and control). The Chi-square test was used for nominal and ordinal data. The data of each continuous variable were tested for normality of distribution by the Shapiro– Wilk test before further analysis. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 19 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) was used for the statistical analysis and differences of p < 0.05 were considered significant. The paired t-test was used to compare the difference between continuous data with the normal distribution. The Wilcoxon matched pairs test was used for nonnormally distributed data. To test the percentage difference of variables, the t-test for independent samples was used to compare the difference between continuous data with the normal distribution and the Mann–Whitney U-test was used for nonnormally distributed data.

# Results
Sixty-seven eligible at-risk hypertensive participants were enrolled in this study. Fifty subjects completed the study protocol. Seventeen subjects were lost due to incomplete 24-h urine collection, health problems, and family reasons. A flow chart showing the recruitment and drop-out from the study population is shown in Fig. 1. There was a total of 25 subjects in each group after the study was completed. The baseline characteristics of the intervention and control groups are presented in Table 1.

There were no significant differences in the baseline characteristics of the two groups. A comparison of the variables

between baseline and at a 8 weeks of the intervention group is presented in Table 2. Twenty-five participants completed the study protocol. Seven subjects were lost due to incomplete 24-h urine collection (four subjects) and health problems (three subjects). Waist circumference after 8 weeks of intervention increased from 77.7 cm to 81.00 cm ( p = 0 . 0 2 0 )). Systolic BP level decreased from 125 mmHg to 117 mmHg (p = 0.041) and diastolic BP level decreased from 75 mmHg to 71 mmHg (p = 0.001). After the intervention phase, the knowledge score improved from 8.8 to 11.5 (p = 0.000). There were no significant differences between baseline and after 8 weeks of intervention regarding BMI, energy intake from 24-h recall, sodium intake from 24-h recall, sodium excretion, and creatinine excretion of 24-h urine collection (Table 2).

The differences in the variables between baseline and at 8 weeks of intervention in the control group are presented in Table 2. Twenty-five participants completed the study

protocol. Ten subjects were lost due to incomplete 24-h urine collection (six subjects), health problems (two subjects), and personal reasons (two subjects). Waist circumference after 8 weeks of intervention increased from 86.6 cm to 81.0 cm (p = 0.007). After 8 weeks of intervention, the knowledge score increased from 8.0 to 9.9 (p = 0.001). There were no significant differences between baseline and after 8 weeks of intervention regarding BMI, systolic BP level, diastolic BP level, energy intake from 24-h recall, sodium intake from 24-h recall, sodium excretion, and creatinine excretion of 24-h urine collection.

A comparison between the intervention and control groups after completion of the implementation phase is presented in Table 3. The results were compared by the percentage difference of variables. Systolic BP level and diastolic BP level were decreased in both groups. The BP level percentage

difference of the intervention group was higher than that of the control group. The percentage difference in the 24-h recall energy and the 24-h recall sodium intake was decreased in both groups. The 24-h recall sodium excretion in the intervention group (28.9 ± 51.2 mg/day) was higher than that of the control group (7.6 ± 61.4 mg/day). The percentage difference of the knowledge score in both groups had significantly increased [intervention group, 27.8 (9.8, 52.5) and control group, 26.7(2.0, 48.5)]. There was no significant percentage difference of all variables between the two groups.
