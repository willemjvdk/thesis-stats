# 3. Method
The study was performed as a controlled pilot. The study conforms to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki 7 and was approved by the Local Research   Ethics Committee.

We identified patients with the diagnosis of heart failure from the practice computer database. To be sure that they genuinely had CHF only patients who had the diagnosis confirmed by a hospital consultant physician were included. The study’s exclusion criteria were terminal diagnosis of patient or cohabitee, and dementia or confusion such that the patient could not comply. The eligible patients were invited to join the study. Those who consented were then asked to attend a specially organised outpatient session at the local hospital where their diagnosis was confirmed, including by echocardiogram, by a consultant cardiologist E.L. . After this assessment and optimisation Ž . of therapy, patients were either assigned to the control or telemedicine group Table 1 . Ž .

## 3.1. The telemedicine group

This group had pulse and blood pressure devices, video consultation equipment and scales installed into their homes. The study team was given the choice of two different blood pressure devices. We opted to use the wrist device - the NAIS memory Blood Pressure Watch model number EW 284 . This was done be- Ž . cause patients found it easier to apply and use. The conclusions of the comparison between this, and the alternative wrist device we were offered to use, have already been published 8 . 

We found that as long as patients had a foam wedge to place their wrist at the height of their heart during blood pressure readings that the wrist device performed well. The foam wedges were manufactured by K.M.

This equipment allowed them to monitor their vital signs. The data was transferred by radio to a box under their telephone and from there to a telemonitoring server situated at St George’s Hospital Medical School. The data collected could be viewed by clinicians at the study practice, a cardiologist at the Royal Surrey Hospital, or by a consultant clinical physiologist at the John Radcliffe Hospital in Oxford P.J. . Ž .

The study nurses reviewed the data collected, every working day, to see whether there were changes suggestive of deterioration in health. At the end of the study, all the data collected was plotted graphically, with key events emergency appointment, hospital Ž admission and other healthcare interventions over-. laid. S. de L. and P.J. then examined the data to look for patterns or trends that preceded these events.

The telemedicine group also has the ability to video consult. This was done using the Via TV-phone 8-8 inch Model number VC105 . This allowed video con- Ž . sulting via ordinary phone lines. The intended video consulting rate was: weekly for 3 months; then every 2

weeks for 3 months; there onwards monthly unless the patient was unwell.

The patient devices phoned into a secure hub at St George’s; this is connected via a firewall to a telemedicine server sitting on NHSnet, a hub established for a previous project 9 . The clinicians   gained access to the data via NHSnet.

## 3.2. The control group

This group received standard general practice treatment. In addition they had their pulse, BP and weight measured quarterly. They were evaluated in the same way as the intervention group.

# 4. Results
## 4.1. Recruitment to and leaing the study

There were 20 patients in the study: 10 in the control group; and 10 in the telemonitoring. The average age of the participating patients was 75.2 years. Two 10% of the patients lived alone. Nearly Ž . all the patients were retired; two patients in the control group and one in the telemedicine group were in work.

Patients were very willing to consent to take part in the study. Of 24 asked to consent in the trial, 20 accepted. The reasons for refusal to take part were: depression two patients ; spouse seriously ill; and Ž . ‘seeing too many doctors already’.

Only two patients subsequently dropped out. One patient requested all equipment to be removed for 2

weeks whilst on holiday but wished it to be reinstalled on her return. A second patient wished to complete the initial 3 months, as the patient found monitoring and video consulting ‘overwhelming’.

There were five deaths during the year of the study: three in the control group; and two in telemedicine group. The causes of death were a myocardial infarction and a CVA cerebro-vascular accident in the Ž . intervention group, and another myocardial infarction and two cancer deaths amongst the controls.

# 5.2. Problems with the methods used
There was a degree of selection bias. We excluded patients whose diagnosis of CHF was uncertain. We did this because within a small pilot we wished to be certain that the patients included definitely had heart failure, confirmed by a consultant. It is possible that those with an uncertain diagnosis may have stood to benefit most from inclusion in the study

Lower grades of CHF tend to be looked after in general practice, it is possible that the telemonitoring would have had more impact if introduced at an earlier stage or in the more severely ill - possibly selected from a hospital clinic.

Lack of additional benefit from videophone over conventional means of contact may simply be due to the low bandwidth and hence poor image quality. None of the users of the system felt that they could reliably note clinical signs using it. The cable link from video camera to telephone socket was regarded as an inconvenience by the patients. For many this link was to a phone point in a different room

We were not able to develop alarm criteria using the data collected or other anecdote referred to above. A study of patients with more severe CHF may be a better use of this equipment.

We were also concerned that we may be inducing dependency. Many of the patients did not want to stop being telemonitored. Hence the high number of mean days of patients in the study over the planned year!

## 5.3. Preious-other work comparisons

We are unaware of any similar study with such a long duration. We feel this study is a potential indicator of high compliance with this type of monitoring.
