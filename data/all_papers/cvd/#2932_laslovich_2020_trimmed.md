## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# METHODS
## Subjects

Thirty-eight sedentary volunteer participants (mean ± SD, 68.2 ± 9.1 yr) with PAD were recruited from local metropolitan communities. The study protocols were approved by Institutional Review Boards of the University of St Augustine for Health Sciences and Rocky Mountain University of Health Professions, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

## Study Design

This study used a before and after randomized controlled trial. Before randomization, subjects were screened using a detailed medical history, the American College of Sports Medicine risk stratification, a physician consent, an assessment of seven continuous days of PA assessment using the activPAL-3™ Micro activity monitor (AP), and a Doppler resting ankle-brachial index (ABI) evaluation. The presence of PAD was confirmed by Doppler ultrasound–assessed ABI of less than 0.9 at rest.

After screening for study criteria, an online random number generator was used for participant allocation into either an attention control group or intervention group. None of the participants reported participation in any current formal walking program and aerobic or resistance training, and no participants reported exertional lower leg pain. Inclusion criteria included

AP confirmation of eight or more hours per day of nonsleep sedentary activity and access to a personal home computer. Potential participants were excluded from the study if they met one or more of the following criteria: inability to obtain an ABI, lower limb amputation or current pedal ulceration, critical limb ischemia, inability to ambulate independently 25 feet, current symptoms of myocardial ischemia, New York Heart Association Class 2 or greater heart failure, abnormal exercise stress test within the last 6 months, any respiratory condition requiring use of supplemental oxygen, uncontrolled hypertension, uncontrolled diabetes, active cancer, current neurodegenerative disease, and previous lower limb revascularization procedures.

## Experimental Protocol

Attention control group (n = 19). The attention control (AC) group received six bimonthly online videos involving health recommendations related to PAD (general PAD facts and figures, hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular disease prevention, tobacco use, and nutrition). Participants were asked to continue normal daily activities and routines during the 12-wk study period.

PA sedentary reduction intervention group (n = 19). The PA sedentary reduction (PASR) intervention group received the same bimonthly online video series as the attention control group while participating in a 12-wk interactive homebased online sedentary activity reduction program (GRUVE Solutions™; MUVE Inc, USA). The GRUVE program incorporates self-monitoring, personal goal setting, real-time feedback, problem solving, and planning to facilitate increases in daily lifestyle PA and reductions in SB. The combination of the GRUVE™ interactive wearable activity tracker and its online self-monitoring home page dashboard provided participants with individualized personal daily, weekly, and monthly summaries of time spent sitting, standing, and walking. The GRUVE™ activity tracker worn by participants in the PASR group incorporated a triaxial accelerometer with internal proprietary software designed to continuously capture, filter, and interpret movement and postural data. Collected daily movement data were translated into five distinct incremental levels of accumulated activity for real-time visual feedback of individualized daily activity goals through a color-coded on-board indicator bar visible on the wearable tracker. The tracker also provided subtle vibration feedback after approximately 50 min of continuous inactivity. Individualized automated goal setting features of the software were used and continuously updated based on uploaded data of previous and current activities. Participants were asked to upload their activity data from the tracker each evening into the Gruve™ software program on their home computer. Visualization of the activity data to participants was provided via simple colorcoded bar graphs representing hour-by-hour progress toward their daily individual PA goals. In addition to the GRUVE program, participants were instructed to incorporate at least two or more 10-min bouts of continuous self-paced walking each day into their daily routines and asked to track these into a personal walking log.

ActivPAL. The ActivPAL is an inclinometer-based activity monitor worn on the anterior thigh, which uses proprietary algorithms to quantify free-living postural allocations involving the intensities and patterns of standing, stepping, and sedentary activities. The device samples data at a frequency of 10 Hz over 15-s epochs. When compared with direct visual observations, the ActivPAL has been shown to demonstrate strong positive correlations and adequate agreement of postural allocations and stepping parameters during free-living activities (23). At both pre- and postintervention, seven continuous days of time spent in sit/lying, standing, and stepping; incremental bands of step counts per minute; incremental bands of sitting bout periods per day; and sit-to-stand transitions were assessed in all participants with the AP. The activity profile of participants over each of the 7-d periods was visually inspected to confirm continuous 24-h collection of postural and stepping behaviors. Because the AP did not differentiate sleep from sitting or reclining while awake, the identification of individual sleeping bouts was determined through manual inspection of the activity profiles adapted from the automated protocol developed by Winkler et al. (24).

EndoPAT. Noninvasive vascular function testing was performed pre- and postintervention using the EndoPAT 2000® system (Itamar Medical Ltd., Caesara, Israel). Determination of tonometry-derived measurements of peripheral arterial tone–reactive hyperemia index (PAT-RHI) and augmentation index (PAT-AIx) using the EndoPAT® system has been shown to demonstrate adequate repeatability when testing is repeated over intervals greater than week (intraclass correlation coefficient = 0.77 and 0.83, respectively) (25) and appears to be feasible for demonstrating improvement in endothelial function as a result of acute lifestyle modifications. PAT-RHI has been associated with coronary artery endothelial function and multiple cardiovascular risk factors, and it has been found to be an independent predictor of CV events and all-cause mortality (26). PAT-AIx has also been shown to be comparable with the measurement of AIx with radial applanation tonometry for assessment of cardiovascular risk (27). Both PAT-RHI and PAT-AIx at 75 bpm were calculated using the software’s automated algorithm (software version 3.4.4).

All participants were asked to fast overnight preceding EndoPAT assessment the next morning. All procedures were completed in a quiet temperature-controlled room (70°F–75°F). After a 30-min rest period in a relaxed supine position, a blood pressure cuff was placed on the nondominant arm above the elbow. EndoPAT finger sensor probes were placed on the index fingers of each hand. An automated computer-controlled constant counter pressure in the probes (predetermined by baseline diastolic blood pressure) was applied throughout the assessment. Baseline pulsatile volume changes of the digit inducing pressure alterations in the finger cuff were collected for 5 min. Arterial flow on the nondominant arm was then interrupted for 5 min by brachial cuff inflation using whichever of the following occlusion pressures were higher: 200 mm Hg or systolic pressure plus 60 mm Hg. Occlusion of pulsatile arterial flow in the probe was confirmed by reduction of the peripheral arterial tone (PAT)

signal to near zero. After the 5-min occlusion period, the blood pressure cuff was rapidly deflated, and digital pulse amplitudes in both hands were measured for the subsequent 5 min (hyperemic period). The ratio of the PAT signal 90–150 s after cuff release was compared with baseline measures, calculated through EndoPAT’s proprietary algorithm, which normalizes for baseline signal and indexed to the nonoccluded arm (control signal). The calculated ratio was identified as PAT-RHI. AIx was acquired from pulse wave analysis during the initial 5-min baseline period. Through the averaging of multiple pulse waves, EndoPAT’s software detected volume and inflection points using a fourthorder derivative, identifying the systolic peak (P1) along with the reflective wave’s peak (P2), then incorporated these into the formula: [(P2 − P1)/P1]  100, and normalized the result to a heart rate of 75 bpm.

Six-minute walk test. The 6-min walk test (6MWT) is a frequently assessed, valid, and reliable measure used in the evaluation of walking capacity in individuals with PAD. The 6MWT was completed at baseline and after the 12-wk intervention by all participants. Participants walked up and down a 30-m indoor corridor and instructed to complete as many laps as possible during a continuous 6-min period. Participants were to report any exertional type lower extremity symptoms during and immediately after testing.

## Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were calculated for all study variables. Group sizes of 19 (total sample N = 38) were needed to achieve 80% power for detecting a group difference (10% reduction in sedentary time per day). Descriptive data were reviewed for normality assumptions using Shapiro–Wilk tests. Baseline differences in continuous and dichotomous variables were compared across groups with Student’s t-tests and chisquare tests, respectively. Bivariate correlation analyses between changes in vascular and PA measures of interest were calculated using either Pearson’s or Spearman’s rho. Withingroup differences for each dependent variable between preand postintervention assessments were analyzed using paired sample t-tests. A mixed-effects ANOVA was used to compare the independent treatment groups on their change in values RHI and AIx across observations. To ensure model adequacy, data were evaluated for normal distribution, homoscedasticity, homogeneity of variance and covariance, and homogeneity of regression slopes. Effect sizes for mean differences are expressed as partial η2 . All data were analyzed using SPSS 24.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY) with an alpha set at 0.05.
