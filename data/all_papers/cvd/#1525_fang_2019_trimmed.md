# Materials and Methods
## TRIAL DESIGN

A parallel two-arm randomized controlled trial was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Shantou University Medical College in China. Exclusion criteria were patients with diabetes, malignancy, a history of cerebral-vascular accident, severe liver or kidney disease or cognitive impairment, aphasia, mental disorder, or inability to attend the evaluation and treatment. Inclusion criteria were patients with low risk after PCI, living with at least one other person, and ability to accept, receive, and send mobile phone messages. Approval to conduct this study was obtained from the Clinical Research Ethics Committees of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shantou University Medical College (SUMC-36-2014). Figure 1 represents a flowchart of the study design.

## USUAL CARE GROUP

Recruited patients who were randomly assigned to the usual care (UC) group received our standard post-PCI care protocol, involving a paper-based and self-study CHD booklet and a biweekly outpatient review by our assigned clinicians. The contents of the booklet included physical activity counseling, diet/nutritional counseling, weight control management, blood lipid management, blood pressure monitoring, advice

on smoking cessation, and psychosocial and diabetes management.

## HBCTR GROUP

Participants in the HBCTR group were also provided with the same CHD booklet to manage their lifestyle and risk factors. In addition, they were instructed to complete outdoor walking or jogging with real-time physiological monitoring no less than thrice/week for 6 weeks. They also received two home visits by a physical therapist during a 6-week interval to enhance their training in HBCTR programs, performed inside and/or outside of their homes. In between visits, a weekly telephone call was made by the physical therapist to resolve any questions the patients might have.

## HBCTR REMOTE MONITORING SYSTEM

The innovative HBCTR program needed to use appropriate technology for real-time monitoring and rapid feedback between the patients and clinicians. Accordingly, the remote monitoring system (Fig. 2) consisted of a belt strap with a sensor, a smartphone with an application, computer servers, and a web portal.6,7 The participants wore the sensor and turned on the application on their smartphone every time they began the exercise training.

# OUTCOME MEASURES
Exercise capacity was determined, using the Six-Minute Walking Test (6MWT) test since many patients were unable to perform a symptom-limited exercise capacity test due to age

and comorbidities. An SF-36 Health Survey (SF36) was used also to evaluate the general health-related quality of life of the participants. According to this test, higher scores are indicative of a better quality of life. The Fagerstrom Test for Nicotine Dependence (FTND) questionnaire was used to test the level of nicotine dependence in participants. The Cardiac Depression Scale (CDS) is a valid and reliable screening questionnaire to evaluate depression in cardiac patients. The outcomes measures were evaluated for each patient in both the HBCTR and UC group, before and after the 6-week follow-up.

## STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

All outcome data were analyzed statistically for both intervention groups, using SPSS version 14.0. Descriptive statistics were used for sample description. The effectiveness of

randomization was performed using t-tests, to compare the baseline characteristics of the UC and HBCTR groups. Paired data on exercise capacity, modifying risk factors, and quality of life, as measured by blood pressure, 6MWT, SF-36, CDS, and FTND tests, were also examined using t-tests.

# Results
## BASELINE CHARACTERISTICS AND CLINICAL VARIABLES

A total of 80 post-PCI patients were selected, based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and randomly and equally divided into two groups for the UC or HBCTR intervention program. Thirty-four patients in the UC and 33 in HBCTR groups completed the study. The demographic and baseline characteristics of the patients are shown in Table 1. The clinical variables data are presented in Table 2, with no significant difference between UC and HBCTR groups.

# OUTCOMES BETWEEN BASELINE AND 6-WEEK FOLLOW-UP
Data for within group analysis are shown in Table 3. After the 6-week intervention, the 6MWT (distance), SF36 (PCS, MCS), FTND, and CDS in both groups had statistically improved compared with baseline data. In addition, no significant changes in blood pressure were observed in either group at 6-week follow-up compared to those for baseline.

## EFFECTIVENESS BETWEEN UC AND HBCTR GROUPS

Table 4 shows the summary of differences for improvement between the HBCTR and UC groups. After the 6-week intervention, the improvement in SF36, FTND scores, and 6MWT was significantly greater for the HBCTR than those in the UC groups ( p < 0.05). However, there was no significant difference between the two groups for improvement in systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and CDS scores between the baseline and 6 week follow-up.
