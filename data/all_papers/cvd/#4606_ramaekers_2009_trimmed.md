# Materials and Methods
The included patients originally participated in a 1-year-long randomized control trial (RCT), which considers the cost-effectiveness of telemonitoring using the Health Buddy system.16 In this article, preliminary data of 101 patients enrolled during the first 3 months of the trial were analyzed.

The intervention group consisted of 56 patients using telemonitoring on a daily basis. These patients received a Health Buddy device in their home environment; the Health Buddy is an easy-to-use device with a big screen and four buttons to answer the questions provided in the daily dialogs (Fig. 1). By means of the dialogs, the Health Buddy system aims to monitor these patients and to increase their diseasespecific knowledge. Additionally, by educating and counseling HF patients, the Health Buddy system aims to improve patients’ adherence to pharmacological and nonpharmacological recommendations.

The gathered information (using the telemonitoring device), was sent to a protected server and subsequently to the caregivers i-Care desktop. Subsequently, the data were transferred into risk profiles (low, medium, or high), depending on patients’ response to the dialogs. This was done for three categories (symptoms, health behavior/ lifestyle, and disease-specific knowledge). Consequently, care providers are enabled to quickly select high-risk patients and anticipate events. The usual care provided to patients in the intervention group by the specialized nurse and cardiologist in the hospital is to a large extent identical among the three participating hospitals (e.g., 3-monthly consultation rate), since use was made of national guidelines for the treatment of HF.17

The control group consisted of 45 patients receiving usual care without the Health Buddy system. Also in the control group, clinical care was highly standardized among the three participating hospitals. All patients received information about their disease and were followed up by a specialized nurse and a cardiologist in the hospital. However, despite these standardized procedures, there remain some differences between the three hospitals (i.e., in provided information

and in the location for follow-up). These differences may cause interhospital variation among groups. Nurses employed by the academic hospital serve as experts for the nurses in the two nonacademic hospitals, which might result in a faster access to new information for patients treated by the expert nurses. All patients treated by nurses from the hospital in Sittard were visited at their homes, while nurses from the other two hospitals met with their patients in the outpatient departments. All patients received two consultations: at the start of the study and 3 months later.

Main data were collected using the Dutch Heart Failure Knowledge Scale (ranging from 0–15),18 the Heart Failure Compliance Scale (ranging from 0% to 100%),19 and the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (depression scale ranges from 0 to 21).20 Considering the data gathered using the Heart Failure Compliance Scale, patient compliance was considered sufficient if the score was ≥75%.19 Furthermore, data were collected at two points in time, a premeasurement before randomization (T0) and 3 months after randomization (T3). Data analysis took place by means of descriptive statistics, \mathsf { \textsf { X } } ^ { 2 }tests, and multivariate regression analyses, which were performed using SPSS 12.0.1 with an apprehended α of 0.05. Furthermore, multivariate regression models were checked for possible significant interaction terms and covariates. In addition, to correct for possible demographic

differences and variations induced by the differences in care provided by the three hospitals (interhospital variation), the hospital responsible for the treatment of patients (Heerlen, Maastricht, or Sittard) was considered in all analyses.

# Results
The mean age of patients was 7 2 . 7 \pm 9 . 5years in the intervention group and 7 0 . 4 \pm \ : 1 0 . 5years in the control group. A majority of patients (51.5%) were treated in Maastricht, and for more than a third of the patients, primary school was the highest completed education. Among respondents, the majority were male (Table 1). After performing \cdot x ^ { 2 }tests, no significant differences were found in the basic characteristics indicating that the control group and intervention group were comparable.

## DISEASE-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

Disease-specific knowledge measured at T0 was high (average score of 12.5 on a 0–15 scale), and over time disease-specific knowledge increased in the intervention group. However, an interaction term emerged, which indicated that the effect of telemonitoring on the disease-specific knowledge differs among the three participating hospitals. Therefore, disease-specific knowledge was considered per

hospital (Table 2). Disease-specific knowledge differed significantly in two (Maastricht and Sittard) of the three participating hospitals. In these two hospitals, an increase in knowledge among patients of the intervention group was accompanied by a decrease in knowledge among the patients of the control group. In the hospital in Heerlen, disease-specific knowledge improved among patients of the intervention group and the control group.

## ADHERENCE

Both patient groups indicated at T0 average adherence rates for appointment keeping, taking prescribed medication, smoking cessation, and alcohol restrictions of 89% or higher. Medium adherence rates among patients were noted for daily weighing, sodium and fluid restrictions (averages between 70.0% and 75.0%), and low rates for physical exercise (on average <65.0%).

In the multivariate regression analyses concerning patients’ health behaviors, no significant interaction terms or covariates emerged. Additionally, at T3 high scores without variance were reported for medication adherence (mean = 100%). Consequently, it was not possible to perform a regression analysis for this constant variable.

The Health Buddy system significantly affected four health behaviors; patients who were using the Health Buddy system indicated on average a higher score for adhering to their fluid restriction, adhering to weighing recommendations, following the exercising recommendations, and adhering to their alcohol restrictions (Table 3).

## DEPRESSION

In the multivariate regression analyses concerning depression, no significant differences emerged. However, when comparing the mean depression scores for the control group and the intervention group over time, a decreased depression score was found in the intervention group (Table 4).
