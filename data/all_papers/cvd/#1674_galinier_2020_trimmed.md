## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods and results
OSICAT was a randomised, multicentre, open-label French study in 937 patients hospitalised for acute HF ≤12 months before inclusion. Patients were randomised to telemonitoring (daily body weight measurement, daily recording of HF symptoms, and personalised education) (n = 482) or to SC (n = 455). Mean ± standard deviation number of events for the primary outcome was 1 . 3 0 \pm 1 . 8 5for telemonitoring and 1.46 ± 1.98 for SC [rate ratio 0.97, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.77–1.23; P = 0.80]. In New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III or IV HF, median time to all-cause death or first unplanned hospitalisation was 82 days in the telemonitoring group and 67 days in the SC group (P = 0.03). After adjustment for known predictive factors, telemonitoring was associated with a 21% relative risk reduction in first unplanned hospitalisation for HF [hazard ratio (HR) 0.79, 95% CI 0.62–0.99; P = 0.044); the relative risk reduction was 29% in patients with NYHA class III or IV HF (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.53–0.95; P = 0.02), 38% in socially isolated patients (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.39–0.98; P = 0.043), and 37% in patients who were ≥70% adherent to body weight measurement (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.45–0.88; P = 0.006).

## Conclusion

Telemonitoring did not result in a significantly lower rate of all-cause deaths or unplanned hospitalisations in HF patients. The pre-specified subgroup results suggest the telemonitoring approach improves clinical outcomes in selected populations but need further confirmation.

# Methods
OSICAT (NCT02068118) was a randomised, multicentre, open-label French study. The methods have been described.18 In brief, the study comprised an 18-month study period, during which patients were randomised to a telemonitoring programme or to standard care (SC).

The protocol and amendments were approved by the French ‘Comité de Protection des Personnes’ and French competent authority. The study was conducted according to French laws and Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent.

## Study patients

Eligible patients (≥18 years of age) were subjects who had been hospitalised for acute HF within 12 months before inclusion, had access to a landline telephone or General Packet Radio Service network, and were eligible for treatment through the French social security system.

Patients were excluded from participation for the following reasons: echocardiographic evaluation not available; B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) <100 pg/mL or N-terminal prohormone of BNP (NT-proBNP) <300 pg/mL; life expectancy <12 months; dialysis; with a heart transplant or left ventricular assist device; need for inotropic treatment; unable to use the study equipment; enrolment in another clinical trial; pregnant or breastfeeding, or of reproductive age and not using contraceptives; and patients under guardianship or wardship.

## Study procedures

At the enrolment visit (managed by the investigator cardiologist), patients were randomly assigned (1:1), in a centralised manner, to telemonitoring or to SC (comprising conventional follow-up at the discretion of their general practitioner or referring cardiologist). All patients were followed up via four telephone calls, conducted by the clinical study technicians (at 15 days, 6 months, 12 months, and 18 months, all ±15 days), during which information was collected on any hospitalisations, adverse events, and concomitant treatments.The telemonitoring programme comprised two elements: electronic devices and a personalised educational component. Patients in the telemonitoring group were each provided with an identical set of electronic scales to measure body weight and a device for answering eight symptom questions (online supplementary Table S1). Measurement of body weight and recording of HF symptoms were communicated daily to a secure server and were analysed automatically by an expert system that generated alerts, as necessary, with the aim of predicting episodes of cardiac decompensation. In the event of an alert, the nurses, who had access to the alerts on working days, contacted the patient to validate its relevance. If appropriate, they advised the patient to contact their general practitioner or cardiologist, with a follow-up call 48 h later. However, it was not planned to analyse the physician’s actions.

The personalised educational component consisted of an information pack related to the management of HF, and to telephone calls every 3 weeks with the same nurses, specialised in HF management and patient education. The following educational aims were established in conjunction with the patient: knowledge of the disease and related medications, recognition of the signs of cardiac decompensation, and adherence to lifestyle advice about diet and physical activity.

## Study management

The electronic case report form was provided and managed by BEPA-TIENT (Paris, France). Patient follow-up was managed by the clinical investigational centre at the University Hospital Centre (Toulouse, France). The study centres were monitored by three clinical research organisations (Platinnes, Paris, France; HealthID, Paris, France; Clinact, Vélizy-Villacoublay, France) and by Air Liquide Santé International (Loges-en-Josas, France). Data management was done by Air Liquide Santé International. The statistical analysis was performed by a clinical research organisation (Inferential, Paris, France).

A scientific committee (online supplementary Appendix S1) validated the objectives, methodology, and scientific quality of the study, and participated in the development and validation of the protocol. A blinded clinical events committee adjudicated all deaths and hospital stays, and assessed whether they were planned or unplanned, or related to a cardiovascular origin or to HF.

# Study outcomes
The primary outcome was the composite of all-cause deaths or unplanned hospitalisations at 18 months. The secondary outcomes (all at 18 months) were: all-cause death; unplanned hospitalisations for any cause; the composite of unplanned hospitalisations for a cardiovascular cause or cardiovascular death; unplanned hospitalisations for cardiovascular cause; cardiovascular death; unplanned hospitalisations for HF; cumulative number of days in hospital for any unplanned cause; cumulative number of days in hospital for any unplanned cardiovascular cause; cumulative number of days in hospital for any unplanned HF cause; days lost due to unplanned cardiovascular hospitalisations or all-cause

death; Short Form-36 (SF-36) questionnaire scores including eight general dimensions and two composite scores: the Physical Component Summary and the Mental Component Summary. Data on adverse events were collected by investigators during patient consultations or were recorded during the four technician-led telephone interviews. Deaths and hospitalisations are reported as efficacy criteria.

The primary and secondary outcomes were analysed in three pre-specified subgroups: New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III or IV, socially isolated status, and adherence to self-measurement of body weight (telemonitoring group only). Social isolation was defined as: SF-36 Mental Health score <45; Mental Component Summary score <35; Mental Health <50 plus Mental Component Summary <40; medical history of depression or mood disorders/alterations; or use of concomitant antidepressant medications. Adherence was calculated as the ratio of the number of days with body weight measurement divided by the number of effective days (corresponding to the number of days in the study minus the number of days when weight could not be measured).

## Statistical analysis

Assuming an estimated frequency of the composite primary criterion of 35% in the control group, with a reduction in the relative risk of 30% with telemonitoring,19 it was estimated that a sample of 870 patients including a 10% loss to follow-up rate would have 90% power to detect a difference in the primary outcome at a significance level of 0.05 for a 2-sided test. Following a protocol amendment, the sample size was increased to 990 to counterbalance the predicted 10% loss of patients before the intervention set-up.

Primary and secondary analyses were done on adjudicated events in the intention-to-treat population (i.e. all randomised evaluable

patients). The primary analysis was a comparison of the number of all-cause deaths or unplanned hospitalisations at 18 months [reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD)] divided by the effective duration of follow-up between the two groups using a negative binomial regression model. All hospitalisations were included for each patient. Deaths that occurred during hospitalisation with an overnight stay were counted as two events. Time-to-event parameters are reported as medians [quartile (Q) 1, Q3], presented using Kaplan–Meier curves and compared exploratively using the log-rank test. Exploratory multiple-factor Cox model analyses were performed to identify predictors of time-to-event parameters (online supplementary Appendix S1) and to express the study group effect after adjustment for these factors. Each dimension and each composite SF-36 score were analysed separately within a mixed model to account for differences in duration of follow-up.

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 software (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC, USA).

# Results
Between May 2013 and June 2016, 990 patients were enrolled at 38 centres: 507 patients were randomly assigned to receive telemonitoring and 483 to receive SC. Fifty-three patients were excluded from the analysis, leaving 937 patients in the intention-to-treat group, 632 (67.4%) of whom completed the 18-month study period (Figure 1).

Over half of the patients (n = 501; 53.5%) were enrolled at university hospitals; 16.9% (n = 158) in other hospitals, 12.3% (n = 115) in nursing facilities, 9.8% (n = 92) in private clinics, and 7.6% (n = 71) in private practices. Most patients (73.6%; n = 690)

were enrolled while hospitalised for HF. Median (Q1, Q3) number of patients enrolled at each centre was 10 (6, 25; range 1–194). Study patients were typical of HF patients, with similar rates of baseline characteristics in both groups. Most participants were male (72.3%), with a mean ± SD age of 69.9 ± 12.4 years; 25.0%

were older than 80 years (Table 1). Sociodemographic characteristics are detailed in online supplementary Table S2. Mean ± SD overall adherence to daily self-measurement of body weight (telemonitoring group; n = 482) was 59.9 ± 35.5% (median 74.6%, Q1, 34.1, Q3, 89.9); adherence was lowest in the first trimester (50.4%),

due to study material shipping, increasing to 74.0% in the second trimester, and decreasing slightly thereafter (online supplementary Figure S1). Mean ± SD exposure during the study period was 14.4 ± 6.1 and 15.3 ± 5.7 months, respectively.

Primary and secondary outcomes are detailed in Table 2 and online supplementary Table S3. Mean ± SD number of events for the primary outcome during the 18-month study period was 1.30 ± 1.85 in the telemonitoring group and 1.46 ± 1.98 in the SC group [rate ratio 0.97, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.77–1.23; negative binomial regression P = 0.80] (Table 2). Median (Q1, Q3) time to all-cause death or first unplanned hospitalisation was 105 (30, 232) days in the telemonitoring group and 84 (29, 238) days in the SC group (log-rank P = 0.68) (Figure 2A).

Primary and secondary outcomes in pre-specified subgroups are detailed in online supplementary Tables S4 and S5. In patients with NYHA class III or IV HF, mean ± SD number of events during the 18-month period was 1.53 ± 1.98 for telemonitoring vs. 1.99 ± 2.26 for SC (rate ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.62–1.10; P = 0.18) (online supplementary Table S4). Median time to all-cause death or first unplanned hospitalisation was 82 days in the telemonitoring group and 67 days in the SC group (P = 0.03) (Figure 2B).

In socially isolated patients, mean number of events was lower in the telemonitoring group (1.30 ± 1.66 vs. 1.89 ± 2.14; rate ratio 0.60, 95% CI 0.39–0.92; P = 0.02) (online supplementary Table S4), with no difference in time to first event between study groups (P = 0.15; Figure 2C). In the telemonitoring group, patients who were ≥70% adherent to body weight measurement had a lower mean ± SD number of events than patients who were <70% adherent (rate ratio 0.58, 95% CI 0.41–0.81; P = 0.001), with a statistically significant difference in time to first event of the primary outcome in favour of ≥70% adherent patients (61 vs. 133 days) (P = 0.01; Figure 2D).

Mean ± SD number of unplanned hospitalisations for HF was 0.59 ± 1.26 for telemonitoring and 0.75 ± 1.42 for SC (rate ratio 0.84, 95% CI 0.62–1.15; P = 0.28) (Table 2). Median time to first unplanned hospitalisation for HF was 97 days in the telemonitoring group and 79 days in the SC group (P = 0.12) (Figure 3A). After adjustment for known predictive factors, telemonitoring was associated with a 21% relative risk reduction in first unplanned hospitalisation for HF (hazard ratio 0.79, 95% CI 0.62–0.99; P = 0.044).

The relative risk reduction of first unplanned hospitalisation for HF was 29% in patients with NYHA class III or IV HF (hazard

ratio 0.71, 95% CI 0.53–0.95; P = 0.02), 38% in socially isolated patients (hazard ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.39–0.98; P = 0.043), and 37% in patients who were ≥70% adherent to weight measurement (hazard ratio 0.63, 95% CI 0.45–0.88; P = 0.006). Time-to-event curves are shown in Figure 3B−D. SF-36 scores showed statistically significant improvements in vitality (P = 0.034) and social functioning (P = 0.025) for telemonitoring vs. SC (Table 3).
