# Methods
The enrolment of patients in the current study was performed between February 2019 and July 2019. The study protocol was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (Ref. 2020-SR-38) and was registered at ClinicalTrials. gov (Ref. NCT 04414007). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The present study was a single-centre, randomised, controlled trial.

The participants were inpatients at the Department of Cardiology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, a tertiary care hospital in Jiangsu Province, China. The inclusion criteria included the completion of catheter ablation for atrial fibrillation, willingness to participate in

cardiac rehabilitation and access to a smart phone. In addition, eligible patients were between 18 and 75years old. The main exclusion criteria included the presence of a pre-existing non-cardiovascular condition (orthopaedic and/or neurological) that limited the patient’s ability to actively engage in exercise training; the presence of a terminal disease, dementia or cognitive impairment that rendered the patient unable to complete the questionnaire survey, use the tele-rehabilitation equipment or attend follow-up visits and inability to undergo ablation or severe complications related to ablation.

Consecutive patients who fulfilled the inclusion criteria were informed about the study on the first day after ablation by a physiotherapist. After enrolment, they were allocated randomly to tele-rehabilitation (intervention group, n { = } 5 0 )or conventional rehabilitation (control group, n { = } 5 0 )at a ratio of 1:1 with a randomisation list. The randomisation sequence was created with SPSS (version 23; IBM) statistical software and was stratified using random block sizes of four. Due to the nature of the rehabilitation trial, it was not possible to completely mask the patients and intervention personnel. However, the investigators collecting the outcome data and performing the data analysis were blinded to the intervention allocation. Baseline characteristics, as well as physical capacity and health beliefs, were recorded immediately after enrolment, and all of the participants initiated a physical exercise program one month after the ablation procedure. After three months of rehabilitation, patients in both groups attended follow-up visits in the clinic. Exercise capacity, including the primary outcome, \mathrm { V _ { o 2 p e a k } , }was evaluated at follow-up. In addition to \mathrm { \Delta V _ { \mathrm { O 2 p e a k } } , }adherence, self-reported physical activity, health beliefs related to cardiovascular disease and exercise self-efficacy were assessed after three months of cardiac rehabilitation. The rehabilitative proposal in each group is described in detail below.

For patients who receive ablation for atrial fibrillation, the standard modality of rehabilitative care in our hospital is outpatient rehabilitation. Adherence was calculated as the percentage of the 12 weeks during which the patient completed 150 or more minutes of exercise. For the control group, the number of minutes of therapy completed at each session was determined based on the patients’ exercise diaries; for the intervention group, adherence was calculated according to the

records provided by the wearable electrocardiogram recording device. The number of minutes spent with a heart rate equal to the heart ratetarget was counted for each exercise session.

Self-reported physical activity based on the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ) was evaluated at baseline and after 12 weeks.22 The items in the long IPAQ form were structured to provide separate domain-specific scores for walking, moderate-intensity activity and vigorous-intensity activity within each of the work, transportation, domestic chores and gardening (yard) and leisure domains. The total scores were computed by summing the duration (in minutes) and frequency (days) for all the types of activities in all domains. We classified the population into three levels of physical activity, namely, low, moderate and high, according to the IPAQ results. Intergroup and before and after comparisons of physical activity were performed by comparing the

percentages of patients classified into the groups with moderate and high levels of physical activity.

The Health Beliefs Related to Cardiovascular Disease Scale was used during the 12-week study period.23 It consists of 4 subscales and 25 items, measuring four constructs of the Health Belief Model: perceived susceptibility and severity of cardiovascular disease and benefits of and barriers to having a healthy diet and engaging in exercise. The Chinese version of the scale was used, and beliefs that a healthy diet and regular exercise could prevent cardiovascular disease were evaluated on a 4-point Likert scale (1 = not at all true, 2 = rarely true, 3 = moderately true, 4 = always true). Cronbach’s alpha for this scale was 0.932 in this sample.

Exercise self-efficacy was examined with the Exercise Self-Efficacy Scale, which included 10 items.24 The scale was translated into Chinese using a two-part method that included (a) translation by bilingual investigators and (b) back translation. The two versions (English and Chinese) of the instrument were compared and verified by a qualified, bilingual expert specialising in the design and cross-cultural validation of study questionnaires. Respondents were instructed to indicate on the 10-point scale how confident they were with regard to carrying out regular physical activities and exercise. Cronbach’s alpha for this scale was 0.84 in this sample.

Atrial arrhythmia recurrence was also determined by the results of 72-hour Holter monitoring performed at the four-month follow-up after ablation. Any symptomatic or asymptomatic atrial tachycardia/atrial fibrillation episodes that lasted for more than 30 seconds were regarded as a recurrence. Participants were instructed to report any other safety issues, including injuries that occurred during rehabilitation or the need for hospitalisation due to recurrent atrial fibrillation episodes to the physicians via smart phone.

With a power of 80%, a P value less than 0.05 and a dropout rate of 10%, 50 patients were needed in each group. Descriptive continuous variables are presented as the mean (SD) or median (interquartile range); categorical variables are expressed as the frequency and percentage. Changes from baseline to follow-up within each group were compared with the paired-samples t test for normally distributed data and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for nonnormally distributed data. Changes at baseline and follow-up were analysed with the independent-samples t test and the Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. Two-sided values of P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All of the statistical analyses were performed with SPSS (version 23; IBM).

# Results
The flow of participants through the trial is presented in Figure 2. From February 2019 to July 2019, 138 patients were primarily screened for eligibility. Of these, 38 were excluded for various reasons. During the three-month intervention period, three patients withdrew from the study. Finally, the results of 48 patients in the control group and 49 patients in the cardiac tele-rehabilitation group were included in the final analysis. The reasons for withdrawing the study were personal or other unrelated health problems. Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of the randomised patients. In terms of demographic and clinical parameters, the control group and intervention group were well matched at baseline.

At baseline, the mean \mathrm { V _ { O 2 p e a k } }did not differ significantly between the control and intervention groups. After 12 weeks of exercise training, subjects in both groups had significant increases in \mathrm { V _ { O 2 p e a k } }compared to baseline.

The summed scores of 25 items on the Health Beliefs Related to Cardiovascular Disease Scale and 10 items on the Exercise Self-Efficacy Scale did not differ significantly between the control group and intervention group at baseline. Regardless of the type of cardiac rehabilitation program in which the

patients participated, the scores for both health beliefs related to cardiovascular disease and exercise self-efficacy increased significantly after 12weeks of cardiac rehabilitation.

There was no significant difference between the intervention group and the control group in physical activity according to the IPAQ survey at baseline

(P = 0.461). After 12 weeks of rehabilitation, the physical activity in each group had significant increased compared with the baseline level (Table 2). Meanwhile, subjects in the tele-rehabilitation group exhibited significantly higher levels of physical activity than those in the control group after 12 weeks of rehabilitation (P = 0.002).

Table 3 presents the changes in aerobic capacity, health beliefs and exercise self-efficacy after the intervention. Not only was the result for aerobic capacity in significant favour of the intervention

group but also the results for the other two scales, indicating that compared with patients in control group, those in the tele-rehabilitation group had more faith that they would benefit from healthy

lifestyle, felt more confident in the current exercise mode and were more likely to insist on continuing the rehabilitation program after study period.

Among patients who were included in the final analysis, those in the intervention group were adherent during 9.6 ± 3.1 of the 12 weeks ( 8 0 . 4 \% \pm 2 6 . 1 \% ), while those in the control group were adherent during 5 . 0 \pm 3 . 8of the 12 weeks ( 4 2 . 0 \% \pm 3 1 . 6 \% ). Adherence differed significantly between the study groups.

At the three-month follow-up, 6.1% (3 of 49) of the patients in the intervention group and 6.5% (3 of 46) of the patients in the control group experienced the recurrence of atrial arrhythmia after ablation, with no significant difference between the two groups. Injuries or the need for hospitalisation due to recurrent atrial fibrillation episodes did not occur in either group as a result of participation in the rehabilitation program.
