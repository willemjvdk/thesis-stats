# METHODS
## Design

A randomized controlled trial with a 2 parallel-group design was conducted. Eligible patients with cardiometabolic multimorbidity were randomly assigned to either the experimental group (EG) or the control group (CG). The EG received a 12-week home-based telehealth exercise training program. The CG maintained their usual lifestyles and routine outpatient follow-up. Outcome measures included PA amounts, exercise capacity (cardiorespiratory fitness [ \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 \mathrm { p e a k } } ]and workload), and healthrelated quality of life.

## Participants and Settings

Potential participants from outpatient clinics at a medical center in Northern Taiwan were recruited by a research assistant from April 2018 through June 2019. Those who agreed to participate were then referred for screening for confirmed cardiometabolic multimorbidity,

defined as the presence of 2 or more cardiometabolic conditions (ie, hypertension, type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia, heart disease, metabolic syndrome, or gout)3 by a medical doctor. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged 18 years or older, (2) able to speak and understand Mandarin, (3) able to walk without assistance, and (4) had mobile phones and could easily access the Internet. Exclusion criteria included a history of arrhythmia, cancer, end-stage renal disease with dialysis, being diagnosed with mental illness, recent stroke within 6 months, and inability to participate because of comorbid neurological or musculoskeletal conditions that produce moderate to severe physical disability.

G*Power (Germany, version 3.1.9) software was applied for estimating the sample size. Based on using multivariate analysis of variance-repeat measures (withinbetween interaction) with a statistical power of 0.8, an effect size of 0.4, a significance level of .05, and 2 measurements, we calculated that 26 subjects were required in each group.24 By considering a possible attrition rate of 10% to 15%, the target sample size was 29 to 30 per group.

## Study Cohorts and Interventions

Initially, 60 patients were approached. Fifty eligible participants provided informed consent and were randomly assigned by the project investigator in a 1:1 ratio to either the EG or the CG using sealed opaque envelopes, following computer-generated random serial numbers. A research nurse, blinded to the random assignments, assessed the participants' baseline characteristics and invited all of the participants to receive graded exercise testing based on the guidelines of the American College of Sports Medicine.25 Two observers (a medical/rehabilitation physician and the same research nurse) were involved with each patient throughout the graded exercise testing, performed using a motorized ergometer (Corival, Lode, the Netherlands) with a standardized ramp protocol by increasing the resistance (10 W/min) at a consistent speed of 60 revolutions per minute.25 The graded exercise testing was applied for determining a baseline for the maximum heart rate and detecting risks for potentially life-threatening conditions (eg, unstable angina).

After the graded exercise testing, participants in the EG and CG each received a single, individual, face-toface PA promotion counseling session (15–20 minutes), performed by another experienced and well-trained nurse/ researcher, who had more than 15 years of experience in exercise training, based on the guidelines of the American College of Sports Medicine.25 The EG received a 12-week home-based telehealth exercise training program (30 minutes/session, 3 sessions/week for 12 weeks, with a total of 36 sessions) combined with heart rate sensing clothes, which are sports clothes providing a more stable and reliable heart rate through skin contact

than other wireless wearables (patent/publication number I472301). In addition, the EG received weekly reminders and monthly outpatient follow-ups during the exercise program. A home-based exercise mobile app was offered combined with the heart rate sensing clothes to each participant in the EG. The mobile app provided auto-feedback of heart rate at each exercise training session through the heart rate sensing clothes and alarm function identifying individuals' appropriate exercise intensity (target heart rate) during their exercise based on their exercise prescription calculated and set up inside the app. For example, if a participant's target heart rate was 130 beats per minute, and the participant's heart rate during the actual exercise training period was lower than 125 or higher than 135 beats per minute, the app warns the participant to exercise harder or slower to reach the target heart rate. After completing each exercise training session, the record of training time and heart rate response was simultaneously uploaded to the cloud system of the medical center through the heart rate sensing clothes. The same experienced and well-trained nurse checked the cloud system data to monitor participants' adherence to exercise training. In addition, online communication was enabled using LINE software, which provided a platform for patients and researchers to interact with each other through messaging. LINE is a freeware app for instant communications that allows an exchange of texts, images, video, and audio on electronic devices, such as smartphones, tablet computers, and personal computers. The app was downloaded, and each participant in the EG was asked to register on the app before initiating the home-based telehealth exercise training program. Before the study, the heart rate measuring device of the heart rate sensing clothes with Bluetooth SMART heart rate transmit module (CARDIOsport Heart Rate Transmitter TP3) was evaluated in terms of reliability and validity, ensuring the provision of accurate heart rate measurements.

A motorized ergometer bike (Ergoselect 200 K, Ergoline, Germany) was used for the exercise training test to evaluate the reliability and validity of the heart rate measure device of the heart rate sensing clothes. During the exercise training test, a 10-minute resting and 30-minute training test protocol (5-minute warmup phase [20–30 W], a 20-minute main training phase [target heart rate: 72% of heart rate reserve], and a 5-min cool-down phase [20–30 W]) were included. The heart rate measure device of the heart rate sensing clothes and a standard heart rate monitor (erg705.081, Ergoline, Germany) were simultaneously used to record heart rate every second during the exercise training test. The heart rate measure device of the heart rate sensing clothes demonstrated good reliability (intraclass correlation coefficients: 0.99 for resting, 0.93 for warm-up, 0.97 for training, and 0.987 for cool-down) and satisfied validity, which was linearly correlated with the criterion

standard ( r = 0 . 9 9 )using Pearson correlation coefficient analysis.

The individual exercise prescription of the 12-week home-based telehealth exercise training program was as follows—frequency: 3 sessions/week; intensity: the target heart rate was 70% of the heart-rate reserve (resting heart rate +70% [maximum heart rate − resting heart rate]) based on the Karvonen method25 during the entire length of the 12-week exercise training program; time: 30 minutes per session of the main endurance training phase excluding the 5-minute warm-up and cool-down phases; type: brisk walking, jogging, or stationary ergometer exercise offered by a rehabilitation physician after the graded exercise testing and monitored by a physiotherapist and the nurse/researcher. The home-based telehealth exercise training program comprised 36 individualized home-based exercise training sessions and a weekly reminder from the same nurse for maintaining exercise schedule and providing patients with support through the LINE software, with a monthly outpatient follow-up. In addition to receiving a single, individualized PA promotion counseling session similar to the EG, the CG maintained their routine outpatient follow-up per month and usual lifestyles. For instance, participants diagnosed with hypertension, type 2 diabetes, hyperlipidemia, or heart disease received a 1-time follow-up per month to monitor their conditions at outpatient clinics.

Institutional review board approval (reference number 1-106-05-107) was obtained from the local medical center in Northern Taiwan before the commencement of the study. All participants provided written informed consent and were assured that their participation was entirely voluntary.

## Measures

Eligible participants were invited to the local medical center at baseline and 12 weeks for assessment, collected by a separate research nurse, blinded to the group assignments.

## Physical Activity Amounts

Physical activity amounts were assessed using the wellvalidated and reliable Taiwan version of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire.26 This 7-item instrument measures time (minutes) of PA including housework, transportation, leisure activity, and moderateto-vigorous-intensity PA in the past 7 days. The International Physical Activity Questionnaire scores, expressed as MET-min/wk, were calculated depending on the types of PA, including walking (3.3 METs), moderateintensity PA (4.0 METs), and vigorous-intensity PA (8.0 METs).27 The overall PA amount was the sum of walking, moderate-intensity PA, and vigorous-intensity PA. The PA level of participants in this study as determined

by weekly PA levels (scores on the International Physical Activity Questionnaire) was categorized based on the PA recommendations of the Center for Disease Control and the American College of Sports Medicine25,28: (1) active: the participant has a moderate PA—3 or more days per week of vigorous activity for a minimum 20 minutes per day or 5 or more days of moderateintensity activity or walking for a minimum of 30 min per day; (2) sedentary: the participant has a PA level of lower than a moderate PA.

## Exercise Capacity

Exercise capacity was determined by cardiorespiratory fitness (peak oxygen uptake [ \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 \mathrm { p e a k } } ] )) and workload (Watt); both indicators were assessed by the graded exercise testing.25 \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 \mathrm { p e a k } }is a measure that combines cardiovascular and skeletal muscle oxidative function. Higher \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 \mathrm { p e a k } }(cardiorespiratory fitness) and workload indicated better exercise capacity.

## Health-Related Quality of Life

The reliable and valid Taiwan version of Medical Outcomes Study Short-Form 36,29 consisting of 36 items and 8 dimensions of health (physical function, role function limitation due to physical conditions, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social function, role function limitation due to emotional problems, and mental health), was used to determine health-related quality of life.29,30 Scores ranged from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better health-related quality of life.

## Data Analysis

Descriptive statistics including means and standard deviations and percentage (%) for the study participants are presented. The last-observation-carried-forward method of missing data imputation was used for intentto-treat analysis.31 In this method, if a participant withdraws before the end of the study, the last observed score of the dependent variable is used for the subsequently missing observation points. The intent-to-treat analysis is used for analyzing results, and in this method, all randomized participants are included in the statistical analysis based on the group they were originally assigned to. Generalized estimating equations were applied to appraise the intervention effects of the 2 groups by significant interaction of group and time (grouptime).32 When evaluating the effects of the intervention, the models were adjusted for sociodemographics, clinical characteristics, and PA level covariates such as age, gender, marital status, education, occupation, numbers of comorbidities, metabolic syndrome, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, gout, and PA level, which were reported to be associated with exercise capacity, PA, and health-related quality of life.15,33,34

All of the statistical analyses were 2 tailed and considered significant at P < . 0 5.

# RESULTS
## Baseline Characteristics of Participants

Sixty patients were initially approached. Of these, 6 participants declined to participate and 4 patients did not meet the inclusion criteria. Of the remaining 50 participants, 47 (94%) completed all data collection (Figure).
