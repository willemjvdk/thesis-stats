# Patients and methods
## Study design and sampling

The present study is a quasi-experimental study with randomised control group. The study population consisted of those who were admitted to Cardiologists with a diagnosis of heart failure in 2018 in Shahid Chamran heart centre, Isfahan. Seventy-two records of patients with heart failure were randomly selected from records in the hospital medical record department that were admitted to Chamran Hospital during the past six months with a diagnosis of cardiac failure. Selected subjects were randomly assigned to two groups of experimental and control (36 subjects in each group) (Fig. 1).

Inclusion criteria consisted of age <80 years old, satisfaction to participate in the study, at least elementary education and a definitive diagnosis of heart failure at least six months ago and no heart surgery.

Exclusion criteria were not participating in educational sessions and any conditions that conflict with the criteria for entry into the study (cardiac surgery or myocardial infarction), having a prohibition of physical activity and having a special diet was advised by the doctor.

According to the Harrison (18), regarding the error factor of 5% and the test power of 90%, the sample size was estimated to be 32 for each group and, with considering a 12% drop in samples, the final volume of the sample was considered 36 patients in each group. Accordingly, after collecting information from the hospital archives and contacting the phone numbers registered in the patient file, they were invited by sending a written invitation to participate in this research.

## Ethical considerations

All patients received written consent for participation in the study. Questionnaires were completed without name and that the confidential information was provided to the participants. Educational manuals were provided to all control group members after final stage of the study. This study was extracted from Investigative Study No. 396696, Vice-Chancellor for Research, Isfahan University of Medical Sciences with the code of ethics IR.MUI. REC.1396.3.696.

## Data gathering tool

The data collection tool was a demographic questionnaire and a European Heart Failure Self-Care Behavior (EHFSCB) (19). The questionnaire consists of 12 questions. Each answer, based on the Likert scale, has five options of ‘completely’ to ‘not at all’ with a score of 1 to 5, and the total score is between 12 and 60. Points 28–12

are good self-care symptoms, 26–49 average self-care and low self-care (60–45) (19).

Self-care standard questionnaire in Iran has been validated in Momeni et al.’s study. The validity of the instrument was determined through content validity while the reliability of this questionnaire was confirmed by Cronbach’s a method (0.71) (20).

## Intervention

In this study, the continuous-care model for patients was performed as follows (Fig. 2):

1 Orientation: The goal was explaining the study protocol, identifying the facilities, limitations and types of expectations from each other (researcher and patient). At this stage, the involvement of the patient and the family began to engage in continuous behaviours. For

this purpose, a meeting was held for 60 minutes. It also describes how to contact the researcher by providing a call number to them. At this stage, forms of written satisfaction and self-care questionnaire were completed for patients in the experimental group.

2 Sensitisation: The aim was to involve the patients and their families in the emergence of continuous-care behaviours and simultaneously with the onset of orientation. The intervention consisted of holding four training sessions (each session last one hour) for the experimental group at a training centre of Shahid Chamran Hospital in Isfahan. The content of sessions include familiarity with the process of the disease, the importance of medication and the familiarity with the drugs, the way of consumption and their complications, diet, fluid restriction, daily weight control, physical activity and the control of symptoms. The vital

importance of influenza vaccination, ways to cope with anxiety and depression, familiarity with the symptoms of the disease, the importance of smoking cessation and general education were also fully explained in the booklet and pamphlet provided to patients.

3 Control: The implementation of continuous-care model was implemented on the subjects of the experimental group, followed up by telephone and sent SMS for three months and weekly. The number of contacts taken with the patient was such that a weekly contact with each patient was established. The duration of telephone calls with the patient was considered to be 10 minutes, which could be different according to the needs and educational questions of the patients. A total of 12 contacts were made per patient for 3 months. There were also 2 SMS per week sent to each patient for a total of 24 SMS messages per patient. The schedule of contact during the training sessions was given to the patient and their fellows in written form. The contents of telephone conversations include the subjects taught during the training sessions and in the educational booklet, answering the questions of the patients and their companions according to their specific needs, analysis of the reasons for the patients’ lack of consent to self-care behaviours and the provision of the patient’s solution. To help solve the problem, in addition, patients or their relatives could contact the phone number the investigator had to contact patients for any problems or questions on a day-to-day basis. The researchers did all follow-up contacts.

4 Evaluation: Finally, the success of the intervention was evaluated using a questionnaire for measuring and comparing self-care behaviours. Three months after the intervention, a questionnaire was completed for both groups.

## Data analysis

The information was then integrated into SPSS software (ver20). Chi-square test was used to compare two groups in terms of sex and marital status. The Mann–Whitney test was also used to compare the level of education between two groups. Independent t-test was used to compare the mean self-care scores between two groups in the two periods (baseline and follow-up). In addition, to compare the mean of self-care score in each group at baseline and follow-up, the paired sample t-test is utilised. \mathfrak { a } \le 0 . 0 5was considered as significant level.

# Results
In this study, the mean age of the patients in the experimental group was 59.1 11.9 and in the control group was 6 2 . 5 \pm 1 1 . 1, which was not significantly different between the two groups.

Most of the participants in this study were male (77.8% in the experimental group and 63.9% in the control group). Chi-square test showed that there was no significant difference in frequency distribution of sex (p = 0.19) and marital status (p = 0.10) between the two groups. Mann–Whitney test showed that there was no significant difference between the two groups in the level of education (p = 0.71) (Table 1).

In general, the independent t-test shows that the mean of self-care score at baseline assessment was not significantly different between the two groups (p = 0.22) but after 3-month follow-up in the experimental group, it was significantly higher than the control group (p < 0.001). In addition, the paired t-test shows that there was no significant difference between self-care scores in the control group at baseline and 3-month follow-up (p = 0.11), but in the experimental group after

follow-up, the mean score was significantly higher than baseline (p < 0.001) (Table 2).

The results of frequency response to each item of selfcare questionnaire at baseline and follow-up assessment in the two groups were presented in Tables 3 and 4. According to the questionnaire completed by both groups, it was shown that patients in daily weight control, observing the amount of fluid intake per day, allocating resting time per day, observing the use of low-salt diet, following the physician’s prescription in taking medications, injections of influenza vaccine annually and performing physical activity, their progression were very impressive (Tables 3 and 4).
