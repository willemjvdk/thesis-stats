## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# 2. Methods and materials
This study was approved by the Providence Health Care Research Ethics Board and is part of a larger parallel randomized controlled trial (RCT) comparing the impact of RT-CGM to IBGM on glycemic control. To recruit patients we approached consecutive adult patients with type 2 diabetes (treated with insulin alone or in combination with oral anti-hyperglycemic agents) presenting to an endocrinology clinic from October 2010 to January 2012 in Vancouver, BC. To be eligible for the study, patients had to have (1) a recent HbA1c > 7.0% (53 mmol/mol), (2) Internet

access, (3) received prior training in self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG), and (4) been performing SMBG at a frequency of at least once per day prior to enrolling into the study. At the clinic visit, a research assistant described details of the study and what participation entailed. Interested individuals provided informed consent and enrolled into the study.

The randomization allocation sequence used a 1:1 ratio and was produced by a computer random number generator. Patients were informed of their group assignment (RT-CGM or IBGM) by a research assistant. All patients were equipped with a glucometer (Freestyle, Abbott) and test strips to test three times daily. Patients were required to obtain a laboratory blood test and attend a clinic visit at baseline, 3-months, and 6-months. At the end of the 6- month study, patients completed an 8-item survey assessing treatment satisfaction and administered by a trained research assistant. Patients were given the option to have the survey administered face-to-face in the clinic or via telephone.

## 2.1. RT-CGM group

The Guardian REAL-Time Continuous Glucose Monitoring System (Medtronic MiniMed, Inc., Northridge, CA) produces a seven-day visual tracking report that indicates blood glucose trends and excursions as well as daily summaries of mean blood glucose with highs, lows, and standard deviations. Patients randomized to the RT-CGMS group were trained by a Registered Nurse familiar with sensor technology to use this monitoring system. An alarm activates if the blood glucose level falls below 4 mmol/L. Patients were asked to save the ‘‘sensor daily overlay’’ report as a PDF and email it to their endocrinologist every two weeks.

## 2.2. IBGM group

Internet blood glucose monitoring is a facilitated method in which patients can communicate with their physicians through an online system with no geographic restrictions [6]. Patients randomized to the IBGM group were trained by the research coordinator to upload their glucose readings every two weeks to a secure, commercially available website (ALR Technologies Inc, Atlanta, Georgia). Patients were instructed to test their glucose levels 3 times per day before mealtimes. Glucose levels were presented in table and graph formats according to time of day with automatic calculations showing the mean, standard deviation and range for specific time periods. The system allowed patients to input medications, view summaries of readings, and contact theirendocrinologist. The endocrinologist

reviewed the readings and sent feedback through the ALR messaging system.

The endocrinologist’s recommendations to both IBGM and RT-CGM groups included changes in therapy, suggestions on testing frequency, lifestyle modifications and/or encouragement to continue with no changes.

## 2.3. Measures

Treatment satisfaction, the primary outcome, was measured by the 8-item Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire (DTSQ) developed by Bradley [20]. Six of the eight items assess separate components of treatment satisfaction including general satisfaction, convenience, flexibility, understanding of the disease, likelihood of recommending to others, willingness to continue current form of treatment. Items are scored on a 6-point Likert scale with higher scores indicating greater satisfaction. Patients are prompted with this statement: ‘‘The following questions are concerned with the treatment for your diabetes (i.e., blood glucose monitoring system). Please answer each question by circling a number on each of the scales.’’ An overall treatment satisfaction score can be calculated by adding up scores on all six items. Scores for individual items can also be considered. The two remaining items assess frequency of ‘‘too high’’ and ‘‘too low’’ blood sugars as experienced by the participant. These two items are also scored on a 6-point Likert scale with lower scores indicating blood glucose levels closer to participant’s personal target.

## 2.4. Statistical analysis

Measures of central tendency (mean  SD) were analyzed for demographic and clinical characteristics as well as treatment satisfaction items. Independent t-tests and chi-square tests were performed to compare between group differences for continuous and dichotomous variables, respectively. Independent t-tests were also conducted to compare treatment satisfaction ratings between RT-CGMS and IBGMS. Bonferroni correction was utilized when making multiple comparisons (6 individual treatment satisfaction components) with a corrected p  0.008. Paired t-tests were conducted to measure within group changes for clinical outcomes and independent t-tests were conducted to compare change scores for clinical outcomes between RT-CGM and IBGM.

# 3. Results
Originally, the larger RCT recruited and enrolled n = 5 7patients. Of the ^ { 5 7 , }thirty-two patients were randomized to the RT-CGM and 25 patients were randomized to IBGMS. Over the course of the 6-month study, 12 patients from the RT-CGMS group and five from the IBGMS groups dropped out. In the former group, 7 patients dropped out immediately after randomization due to unwillingness to receive treatment. Five additional patients dropped out during the course of the study citing discomfort and inconvenience of treatment. In the latter group, 5 patients dropped out because they were unable to attend follow-up appointments citing personal reasons unrelated to treatment.

Consequently, 40 participants completed the study. Of the patients in the IBGM group, 5 patients were on single daily injections, 11 patients were on two daily injections and 4 patients were on three or more injections daily. Of the RT-CGM group, 2 patients were on single daily injections, 14 patients were on two daily injections and 4 patients were on three or more injections daily.

This study is based on the 40 patients who completed the larger RCT. Of the remaining 20 patients in the RT-CGMS and 20 in the IBGMS group, 15 of the former and 17 of the later completed the DTSQ. Not all patients could be reached after the study to complete the DTSQ. Table 1 presents the demographic and clinical characteristics of patients in the IBGM and RT-CGM groups. Of the total sample, mean age of the sample was 60 years (SD = 9.4); mean duration of diabetes was 18 years (SD = 6.6); 59% were male (n = 19). The groups did not differ significantly on any demographic or clinical variables.

## 3.1. Treatment satisfaction

Table 2 presents the scores for the DTSQ items. The mean overall treatment satisfaction score was significantly higher for the IBGM group than the RT-CGM group (33.41 vs. 24.80, p < 0 . 0 0 0 ). Ratings for individual satisfaction components including convenience (5.24 vs. 3.80; p = 0.004), flexibility (5.41 vs. 4.00; p = 0.004), likelihood to recommend treatment to others (5.94 vs. 4.00; p = 0.001), and willingness to continue treatment (5.71 vs. 3.40; p < 0.000) were also significantly higher for the IBGM versus RT-CGM group.

## 3.2. Perceived frequency of high and low blood sugars

There was no difference between the IBGMS and CGM group in perceived frequency of unacceptably high or low blood sugars.

# 3.3. Clinical outcomes
Table 3 presents the within and between group changes in clinical outcomes for RT-CGM and IBGM. Both groups significantly improved their glycemic control at 6 months, however, there was no significant difference between groups. Furthermore, there were no significant within and between group changes for body mass index, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and creatinine.
