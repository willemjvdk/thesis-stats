# METHOD
## Study Type

This is an experimental, controlled, randomized, double-blind clinical trial.

## Sample Determination

As a quality strategy, the study was based on the CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) guidelines for recruitment, randomization, tracking, and intervention data analysis (Figure S1, http://links.lww.com/CIN/ A252). A double-blind configuration was used, that is, in which the participants were not aware of the existence of different groups, and the main researcher was not aware of participant allocation. The research did not allow crossover at the end.

The sample size was calculated according to the pairing of cases with a power of proportion of 80% of the favorable results. Considering the possibility of losses, 132 participants were recruited. Of these, 51 were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria or refusal to participate. Randomized participants (n = 81) were divided into two groups: intervention group (IG) and control group (CG). After loss of followups, the sample resulted in 42 participants, allocated into IG (n = 20) and CG (n = 22).

The random allocation of participants within the two groups (IG and CG) was proportional, carried out through the Randomization Web site after recruitment.12 With the intention of minimizing biases in the research, the sample allocated to each group was proportionally adjusted at randomization, considering two variables that tend to bias the research results: sex and time of diagnosis for T2DM. Thus, each recruited group had these variables in the same 1:1 ratio.

The following group arrangements were followed: the CG underwent nursing consultations with educational interventions about self-care and self-examination of the lower limbs, such as foot hygiene, skin hydration, use of adequate footwear, correct nail cutting, and promotion of healthy habits and diabetes control, with an average duration of 30 minutes. The IG, combined with PedCare mobile app, interspersed with two reinforcement telephone calls, recommending the daily use of the app for at least 5 minutes a day.

The participants allocated to the IG received printed material with information on how to proceed and how to withdraw from the study, should they decide to do so.

## Setting

The study was conducted at an outpatient referral service for the prevention and treatment of diabetes and hypertension in Fortaleza, capital of the state of Ceará, Brazil, from August to October 2018. The institution receives patients

referred by the municipalities' basic health units and are treated by a specialized multidisciplinary team, composed of nurses, doctors, nutritionists, dentists, and others, for diagnostic and follow-up consultations, providing guidance, prescription, adjustment of medications and interventions, such as treatment of diabetic foot ulcers and glycemic control.

## Selection Criteria

Participants were recruited personally by the main researcher and four research assistants 1 month prior to the intervention. Inclusion criteria were being older than 18 years, T2DM medical diagnosis recorded in medical records, foot at risk measured by the foot risk stratification scale, availability to be accompanied at the institution, having a personal smartphone with an android operating system, and ability and familiarity with the use of apps, assessed through a smartphone handling test with the app version installed. People with diseases and conditions that constituted social dependency, cognitive deficit, and any difficulty that made handling the device and responses to the instruments unfeasible, or presenting preexisting diabetic ulcer via foot inspection were excluded. Discontinuation criteria were nonattendance to study stages, illness, death, withdrawal from participation, and not answering calls. It is noteworthy that three interspersed attempts to call participants were made prior to study discontinuation.

Research participants were recruited through prior contact, personally mediated by the researcher or team member, before or after the nursing consultation and/or group meetings held at the institution. They were informed that the research was being carried out and, those who were interested were asked to sign the Free and Informed Consent Term and set a date for answering the pretest evaluation forms.

## Instruments Used to Collect Information

The intervention consisted of the presentation and unique application of the educational PedCare mobile app, through the nursing consultation, which aims to encourage and educate patients with T2DM in the practice of foot self-care, by addressing 11 topics, namely, Why is foot care relevant? Daily care, Healthy Foot Habits, Toenails, Footwear, Signs and Symptoms, Complications, Exercises That Improve Circulation, Classification of an At-Risk Foot, Visiting the Nurse and/or Doctor, and a Quiz. The app includes basic and advanced operational functions that aim to provide the patient with an innovative final product, such as alarms, risk stratification, and videos with exercises to improve lower limb circulation.13

A tutorial explaining the app features was first presented in PowerPoint (Microsoft Inc., Redmond, WA, USA) through a tablet. Then, an average of 20 minutes was granted to each participant for app handling. The app was then installed on the participant's smartphone. After handling the app,

participants were informed that they would receive a phone call after 30 and 60 days after applying the pretest to carry out the posttest, reinforcing the daily use of the app and subsequent evaluation and confirming the scheduling of a new evaluation, with the possibility of it being carried out through a home visit and/or a place chosen by them to answer the questionnaires and perform a physical examination. At the end of each month, IG participants received reinforcement calls from team members.

Effectiveness of the app in promoting foot self-care of people with diabetes and foot at risk was evaluated by the Questionário de Atividades de Autocuidado com o Diabetes (QAD) (Brazilian adaptation of the Summary of Diabetes Self-care Activities Questionnaire)14 and Questionário de Adesão ao Autocuidado com os pés de pacientes diabéticos (QPED) (Diabetic Patient Adherence to Foot Self-care Questionnaire),15 respectively. An instrument to collect data on the socioeconomic and clinical profile and a form for clinical evaluation of the patient's feet were also used. Both instruments were applied before and after the intervention.

The QAD is a self-administered instrument, specific for the assessment of self-care activities with diabetes, and was adapted and validated for the Brazilian context, being considered reliable to measure adherence to self-care in patients with T2DM. For this, it has 15 items divided into six dimensions: general diet, specific diet, physical activity, blood glucose monitoring, foot care, and medication use. It also has three other items for the assessment of tobacco use. A Likert-type scale is used for each item, ranging from 0 to 7, in which the percentage referenced in each item is calculated. Thus, 0 means the least desirable situation and 7 the most desirable, except for the specific diet dimension, in which the values are inverted. Data on tobacco use are analyzed separately, as it is coded differently, which considers the proportion of smokers, the average number of cigarettes consumed in a day, and time since last cigarette.14

The QPED is a questionnaire that measures the self-care with their feet of patients with diabetes, consisting of 14 items, referring to the search for health services, encouragement of self-care by family members, physical activity, capillary glucose control, dietary monitoring, taking of medications, guidance for self-care, willingness to care for their feet, nail cutting, washing and moisturizing feet, and use of substances in lesions. They are completed using a Likert scale, with a variable number of points per item.15

## Data Collection

Questionnaires, physical examinations of participants' feet, and interviews were performed before and after the intervention, considering the intervention period (3 months). The assessment of the outcome took place before (at the time of users' attendance to the nursing appointments at the

institution) and afterward. Participants were reassessed 90 days after the initial contact, time corresponding to the two return calls and comprehensive assessment of treatment adherence and app use.

Data were collected by undergraduate nursing students and supervised by the main researcher, through periodic meetings and monitoring of results via online contact. They were trained to perform the clinical monitoring, as well as to apply the data collection instruments, interviews, application of the intervention, and reinforcement phone calls. The physical examination of the feet was carried out exclusively by the study's main researcher, being a specialist in nursing stomal therapy.

## Data Processing and Analysis

Data were entered into a computerized database and analyzed in IBM SPSS Statistics version 23 (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA). All data were cross-tabulated for accuracy. Descriptive statistics were used to describe patient characteristics and viability outcomes with simple, absolute, mean, median, and SD frequencies. Bivariate associations were assessed using χ2 statistics for categorical variables and Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables.

To verify the homogeneity of the groups, Fisher exact test was used because of the sample size. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare categorical versus numerical variables in both groups as the variables were not symmetrical. When the distribution of the numerical variable in the sample was asymmetric, the Mann-Whitney U test was used to associate it with a categorical variable. In case the numerical variable had a normal distribution, the t test was used for independent samples. When comparing categorical variables, Fisher exact test was used. Regarding the intragroup for comparing two samples in pairs (before and after), the Wilcoxon test was used by asymmetric distribution. In all cases, P ≤ .05 was considered for evaluation of statistical significance.

## Ethical Aspects

The research was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Universidade Estadual do Ceará and registered in the Brazilian Registry of Clinical Trials, under UTN number U1111-1202-6318. Participants were informed of the objectives and procedures of the study and agreed to participate in the research, signing an informed consent form. Privacy and confidentiality were maintained during data extraction or review through double-blind blinding, as the participants were not aware of the existence of an IG and/or CG, and the main researcher was also unaware of the group in which each participant was framed. Cybersecurity was maintained through the use of a password to access the database and monitoring and vulnerability assessment through an antivirus system.
