# 2. Materials and methods
## 2.1. Subjects

This study was a 3-month, non-blinded, randomized trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02313090) that initially recruited 100 people diagnosed with diabetes mellitus (18 with type 1 diabetes and 82 with type 2 diabetes), who were referred to the Department of Endocrinology at The First Affiliated Hospital, College of Medicine, Zhejiang University from January 2014 to February 2014. The age range of patients was 18–74 years old. No limitation for HbA1c was defined. In order to be eligible, patients had to be able to use a smart phone, have no severe complications such as end-phase diabetic nephropathy or

proliferative diabetic retinopathy, and be able to exercise. Using a random number table, interested candidates were randomized into two groups: Welltang intervention group (50 candidates) and control group (50 participants who received their usual care). This study protocol was approved by the Chinese Ethics Committee of Registering Clinical Trials, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant.

# 2.2. Methods
At baseline, all patients completed the diabetes self-care behavior questionnaire [10] and a diabetes knowledge survey [11], which was translated in Chinese language and provided in the Welltang application. Health and medical history were recorded through questionnaires. Demographic characteristics such as height and weight, waist and hip circumferences, and blood pressure were measured by the study team. HbA1c and LDL-C levels were measured in our hospital laboratory. All data were measured 3 months later at the end of the study. All patients were advised to monitor their seven-point finger capillary blood glucose with a blood glucose meter 1–3 days before each clinic attendance in the control group, and 1 day every 1–2 weeks in the Welltang intervention group. Hypoglycemia was defined as blood glucose 63.9 mmol/L. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) and 2 h post breakfast blood glucose (2h-PBG) on the first and last day were tested at the hospital laboratory and used for statistical analysis. The target levels of FBG and 2h-PBG were 67.2 mmol/L and 10.0 mmol/L.

Both groups responded to a post-study assessment survey to evaluate satisfaction with their diabetes care. Patients in the Welltang intervention group were requested to provide feedback on the usability of the Welltang application as: 1 for satisfied with the use of the application and 0 for not satisfied with the use of the application.

The Welltang application (downloaded from www.welltang.com for free), designed by Shanghai Geping Information and Technique Company Ltd (Shanghai, China), was installed on the smart phones of each patient in the Welltang intervention group. Welltang is a smart phone-based diabetes management application, which can be used by both patients and clinicians. Welltang for clinicians is associated with patients’ data. Welltang for patients consists of three main parts: knowledge, self-management, and communication between patients and clinicians.

In the knowledge component, the Welltang database stores diabetic knowledge on diet, exercise, medicine, blood glucose monitoring, and the latest guidelines for diabetes care as summaries. Therefore, it serves as a virtual educator for diabetes and a virtual endocrinologist for clinicians, facilitating the integration of diabetes care among existing resources.

In the self-management component, the application allowed diabetes patients to enter their self-care data (blood glucose values, carbohydrate intake, medications, and other diabetes management information) on their smart phone, and these data were transferred to secure servers to generate into computer-generated logbooks. Data entered into or collected by these mobile devices are stored on central servers, from which it can be retrieved in the event of mobile device failure.

Communication between patients and clinicians comprised patients receiving advice from the study team usually within the day based on their entered questions. The study team provided feedback on the blood glucose levels of patients, their target goals, and their individualized medication regimens based on the data they entered once a week or every 2 weeks. The time spent on the feedback was usually 3–10 min depending on the degree of difficulty of the questions. Predetermined safety thresholds and parameters for blood glucose and blood pressure were programmed in the database, in which readings outside threshold limits would automatically trigger a message to be sent to patients and notify clinicians. The database also triggers alerts for missed readings. These alerts are sent to the participants to encourage them to measure their data. Lastly, they received an electronic action plan as pre-visit summaries for physician office visits once a month.

Patients in the control group received their usual care once a month and did not use the Welltang application. Their physicians reviewed their blood glucose readings and logbooks when the patients made these records available, adjusting their medication regimens to the targeted goals accordingly.

## 2.3. Statistical analyses

All data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) unless otherwise indicated. The significance level was P = 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS version 12.0 (SPSS Inc, Chicago, Illinois, USA). Prior to analysis variable distributions were tested to ensure that assumptions

of normality were met. An independent t test was used to evaluate the difference between two study groups while a paired sample t test was used between changes from baseline to 3-month follow-up.

# 3. Results
Demographic characteristics of the 100 subjects that participated in the study are shown in Table 1. Demographic characteristics, baseline HbA1c, and blood pressure were similar for both study groups. Medication regimens of patients were grouped evenly as oral hypoglycemic, insulin alone, or insulin and oral hypoglycemic. Prior to study enrollment, all subjects used a smart phone.

All patients in the Welltang intervention group continued to use the Welltang application. The mean number of contacts with intervention patients over the 3-month period was 11 ± 3 times, while the minimum was 7 times and the maximum was 18. There were no treatment changes in the type of medications between the control and Welltang intervention groups. Changes in dosage of medication were 68 times in the control group (23 times on oral hypoglycemic and 45 times on insulin) and 187 times in the Welltang intervention group (63 times on oral hypoglycemic and 124 times on insulin).

The average decrease in HbA1c was 1.95% (21 mmol/mol) in patients in the Welltang intervention group and 0.79% (8 mmol/mol) in patients in the control group (P < 0.001). The average decrease in FBG was 1.89 ± 2.61 mmol/L in patients in the Welltang intervention group and 0.95 ± 1.54 mmol/L in patients in the control group (P < 0.05). The average decrease

in 2h-PBG was 4.39 ± 4.43 mmol/L in patients in the Welltang intervention group and 2.81 ± 2.69 mmol/L in patients in the control group \left( \mathtt { P } < 0 . 0 5 \right). As shown in Table 2, although diabetes knowledge and self-care behaviors improved in both groups, the improvement in the Welltang intervention group was better. Hypoglycemic events were infrequent in both groups. Differences on LDL-C, weight, and blood pressure were not statistically significant. Eighty-four percent of patients in the Welltang intervention group were satisfied with the Welltang application.
