# RESULTS & FINDINGS
A total of 150 participants were selected to enroll in this study. Initial baseline comparison showed ‘No significant difference’ between the two intervention arms and control group. Table 1 presented the detailed information of analysis.

DKT: Diabetes Knowledge Test: Number of participants who obtained a score of >19 on the diabetes knowledge test (p < 0.002 significantly improved with pharmacist intervention arm compared to telemonitoring and control arm).

DMSET: Diabetes management self-efficacy Test: Number of participants who obtained a score of > 4 on the diabetes management self-efficacy test (p> 0.000 significantly improved with Pharmacist intervention arm compared to telemonitoring and control arm).

HypAw: Hyperglycemic Awareness: Number of participants who obtained a score of < 4 on the hyperglycemia awareness test (p < 0.057 improved among telemonitoring arm and p< 0.001 improved among pharmacist intervention arm as compared to control arm). Data set is presented in Fig. 3.

The glycemic control pattern is presented in Fig. 4 & 5. Findings showed that baseline dataset have no significant change among all three-arms. However last week of study showed significant (p<0.001) improvement among pharmacist intervention arm as compared to telemonitoring and control arm.

Glycemic control seems well tolerated and managed among pharmacist intervention arm as compared to telemonitoring and control arm (p<0.001). The study findings also showed reduction of mean 2.72 value (HbA1c) in six months (Fig. 6).

A total of 473 events were reported throughout the study during six months, with 280 events in control group (n=50), 173 events in telemonitoring arm (n=50) and 20 events in the pharmacist intervention arm (n=50). There are multiple events reported by a patient so the presentation sum is not equal to 100% (Fig. 7). As the sematic distribution is: Hypoglycemic events: 147, Hyperglycemic events: 199, Emergency ward visits: 26, Hospitalization: 28, Adverse reactions: 10, missed dose: 41 and self-dosing: 22. The proportion of participants experiencing hypoglycemic/hyperglycemic events was significantly lower in the pharmacist

intervention group compared to telemonitoring and control arm (odds ratio: 2.1381; 95% CI: 3.0267-1.6059, p<0.001). Similarly other reported events were also significantly lower (odds ratio: 1.2769; 95% CI: 2.0341-0.9842, p<0.01) in pharmacist intervention arm as compared to telemonitoring and control arm.
