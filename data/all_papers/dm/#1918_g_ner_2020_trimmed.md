# 2. Method
## 2.1. Study setting and selection criteria

This pre-test and post-test control group interventional study was conducted between November 2017 and April 2019. The universe of the research was 251 patients with type-2 DM registered in a FHC in Zonguldak. The sample size was calculated by power analysis (G*Power 3.1.9.2). For one-way analysis of variance, effect size = 0.40, ˛ = 0.05 and 80% power for each group was considered to be appropriate for 48 participants. Similar studies conducted before to calculate the effect size were taken as examples [12,13]. According to the effect size, the total number of samples for both groups was 96 patients, considering probability of losses during the study, 101 patients with type-2 DM were selected to include in the study. As a result, intervention (n: 50) and control (n: 51) groups were created by a statistician using the permutation block randomization method from 145 patients who met the inclusion criteria from the universe and agreed to participate in the study. Double blind technique method was used to prevent bias. There was no loss from the groups during the study. All participant “who included to sample” stayed in study for six months.

The study included individuals over 18 years of age who had been diagnosed with type-2 DM at least one year prior, had used oral antidiabetics (OAD) for at least one year, did not have visual or hearing problems or cognitive and psychiatric problems, had not been diagnosed with malignancy, were able to use mobile phones and read SMSs, and volunteered to participate in the study. The researcher (she was also educator) attended to Diabetes Patient School’s educations for six months before starting to this research.

In this study, structured group education and SMS reminders were independent variables, and glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1C), fasting blood sugar (FBS), cholesterol, triglycerides, high density lipoprotein (HDL), low density lipoprotein (LDL), body mass index (BMI), blood pressure (BP) and scales assessments were dependent variables.

## 2.2. Data collection

The study data was obtained through the Diabetes Patient Follow-up Form which was prepared by the researcher, the Diabetes Self-Care Scale (DSCS), the World Health Organization (WHO)

(5) Goodness Scale, the wall type height measurement ruler and a digital weighing instrument for the measurement of BMI, sphygmomanometer and stethoscope for the measurement of BP and a mobile phones. Laboratory tests used to perform metabolic control evaluations are HbA1C, FBS, cholesterol, triglyceride, HDL and LDL.

## 2.2.1. Diabetes Patient Follow-up Form

This form, which was prepared by the researcher after reviewing the literature, consisted of 34 questions that examined sociodemographic characteristics, diabetic characteristics, anthropometric measurements, BP and metabolic control variables.

## 2.2.2. DSCS

The DSCS was developed by Lee and Fisher in 2005 to measure the level of self-care activities of patients with type 2 diabetes [14]. It is a Likert-type scale consisting of 35 items and has no reverse expressions or sub-dimensions. In 2008 the scale was adapted to Turkish and it was reported that the Cronbach’s alpha value was 0.81 and the test–retest value was 0.96 [15]. The scale was converted into a 4-point Likert-type which included the expressions “Never”, “Sometimes”, “Often” and “Always” in its Turkish form. The maximum score for the Turkish form of the scale is 140 points and as the score increases, the ability of patients with diabetes to perform self-care activities also increases.

## 2.2.3. WHO (5) Goodness Scale

This is a 5-item practical measurement tool that includes positive expressions about the participants’ emotions and is widely used to determine the well-being of individuals in the previous two weeks. Each item is evaluated with a 6-point Likert-type scale between 0 and 5. The raw score ranges from 0 to 25 points. “0” represents the worst possible and “25” represents the best possible quality of life.

## 2.3. Interventions

At the beginning of the study of all the patients, metabolic (HbA1c, FBS, triglycerides, cholesterol, HDL, LDL), BMI, BP (from the left arm after 15 min rest) and scales assessments were performed. The clinical data was not the self-report of the patients. The researcher herself collected venous blood from patients and sent to the laboratory for metabolic control evaluations. After the measurement and evaluation of all patients were completed, the structured group-based education program to be given to the intervention group started to be implemented. The education modules, which were given to patients in intervention group, held within the framework of the curriculum of Diabetes School by “Turkish Republic Ministry of Health”. This study was a structured groupbased education program consisting of five modules based on the “Education Program for Adult Diabetic People” and “Education Sets” [16]. The educations were given by the researcher in the FHC education room to groups of six to eight people in two sessions as power point presentations and face to face education.

The subject contents were as follows: Module 1: General Information about Diabetes/Physiopathology of Diabetes; Module 2: Medical Nutritional Treatment; Module 3: Blood Sugar Regulatory Drugs other than Insulin in Diabetes; Module 4: Acute and Chronic Complications of Diabetes; Module 5: Physical activity/Exercise in Diabetes and Life with Diabetes [16]. After the education sessions an “Education Booklet for Patients with Type 2 DM”, which was prepared by the researcher, was distributed and a “Diabetes Identity Card” which was provided by the Diabetes Association was also given out.

Immediately after the education program, SMS reminders were sent to the intervention group. According to the results of systematic reviews evaluating the effectiveness of sending SMS to patients

with diabetes in the management of diseases, weekly SMSs were reported to be more effective and showed the best compliance at the end of the sixth-month [17,18]. For these reasons, it was planned to send messages twice a week (on Mondays and Thursdays) for six months in this study. The contents of the SMS sent are based on the education module issues determined by the Ministry of Health [16] and the messages prepared by the WHO for diabetes education, which includes education, intervention and lifestyle changes for patients with diabetes [19] and a study in which these messages are used [4].

A total of 48 SMS messages (in first three months 24 SMS, in the second three months repetation of the previous 24 SMS) were sent until the completion of the study. The SMSs consisted of sentences such as “Measure your blood sugar as frequently as recommended”, “Measure your blood pressure as frequently as recommended”, “Remember to take your medication on time and as recommended”, “Do not forget to walk at least 30 min every day”, “Check your feet daily”, “Make sure there are different foods on your plate”, “Don’t forget to drink enough liquid”, “Check your weight and waist circumference once a week”, “Brush your teeth regularly to protect your oral and dental health”, “Get the flu vaccine every year in October–November”, “Do not smoke. Stay away from smoke-filled places: If you smoke, you can get help to quit”. All patients with type 2 DM, including the intervention and control groups on different days, were invited to the FHC for third- and sixth-month evaluations.

At the end of the third- and sixth-month, all patients in both groups to repeat the glycemic and lipid values, BMI, BP and scales evaluations on different days. All measurements and evaluations were recorded repeatedly. The control group did not receive any intervention during the study. They received FHC services. After the study was completed, the structured educational program applied to the intervention group was also applied to the control group.

## 2.4. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the data obtained from the study was performed using the IBM SPSS statistics for Windows, version 19.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Chi-squared tests were used for the categorical data with the suitable correction methods according such as Yates and Fisher tests. The categorical variables in the study

were shown with frequency and percentage; continuous variables with mean and standard deviation. First, the normality of the quantitative variables was assessed via Shapiro–Wilk normality tests. According to the normality results, the differences of the groups over time were evaluated using the Friedman test and binary comparisons of differences by Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon test. Also, Mann–Whitney tests were used to compare the difference between the study groups for each time. In all statistical analysis in the study, p values less than 0.05 were accepted as statistically significant at a 95% confidence interval.

## 2.5. Ethical considerations

This study was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Bulent Ecevit University (numbered 2017/16 on 20.09.2017).

# 3. Results
Participants of the study, 54.0% of the intervention group and 51.0% of the control group were women and nearly all of them stated their marital status as married. The mean age of the patients in the two groups was similar and ranged between 55 and 58 years. Some of the demographic characteristics of the groups were similar in terms of diabetes duration and BMI, and there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups (p > 0.05) (Table 1).

The primary result expected from the study is improvement in metabolic control values. Significant differences were observed in the HbA1c and FBS values of the patients in the third- and sixth-month measurements of the intervention group after the intervention (p < 0.001). It was determined that there was a significant change in HbA1c and FBS values in the sixth-month compared to the pre-intervention in the measurements of the groups over time (p < 0.001) (Table 2).

In the evaluation of the lipid profile of the participants, the thirdand sixth-month measurement results showed significant differences in cholesterol, HDL and LDL values in the intervention group (p < 0.001) and no significant difference in triglycerides (p = 0.127) (Table 2). It was determined that there was a significant change in cholesterol, LDL and triglyceride values in the sixth-month com-

pared to the pre-intervention in the measurements of the groups over time (p < 0.001) (Table 2).

Secondary results expected from the study are improvements in BMI, BP and scale evaluations. When the BMI and BP values of the patients were examined, a significant decrease was observed in the third- and sixth-month measurements of the intervention group after the intervention (p < 0.001). It was determined that there was a significant change in BMI values in the sixthmonth and in BP values in third and sixth months compared to the pre-intervention in the measurements of the groups over time (p < 0.001) (Table 3).

When the DSCS average scores were examined, a significant increase was observed in the third- and sixth-month measurements of the intervention group after the intervention (p < 0.001). It was determined that there was a significant change in DSCS average scores in the third- and sixth-month compared to the pre-intervention in the measurements of the groups over time (p < 0.001) (Table 4).

When the WHO (5) Goodness Scale average scores were examined, a significant increase was observed in the third- and sixth-month measurements of the intervention group after the intervention (p < 0.001). It was determined that there was a signif-

icant change in Goodness Scale average scores in the third- and sixth-month compared to the pre-intervention in the measurements of the groups over time (p < 0.001) (Table 5).
