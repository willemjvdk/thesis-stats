# MATERIALS AND METHODS
## Patients

The study was conducted between June 2017 and September 2018 in Xinqiao Hospital. Patients were

recruited at Yuzhou road community health service center in Jiulongpo district, Chongqing, China. To be eligible for our study, patients were required to meet the following criteria: newly diagnosed T2DM within the past 3 months7 ; glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) of 6.5-7.5%, age 40-60 years, systolic blood pressure (SBP) of 90 to 120 mm Hg, and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of 60 to 90 mm Hg. Exclusion criteria were as follows: type 1 diabetes, cancer; cardiovascular; gastrointestinal, respiratory; kidney or liver disease diagnosis or treatment; eating disorders, psychological disorders or cognitive deficit resulting in an inability to understand or comply with instructions; lactating, pregnant, or planning pregnancy before the end of the intervention; any serious illness that would make patients to be loss to follow-up; smoking; alcoholism; or attending another clinical trial; lack of informed consent; and judgment of the investigator that an individual is ineligible for inclusion in the study. More detail information of the protocol was provided in Supplementary Materials. The clinical trial was registered at http://www.chictr.org/cn/ under study number ChiCTR1900021551 and approved by the Ethical Committee of Army Medical University. All participants signed informed consent forms before beginning the study. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

## Study design

Patients were randomized into the telemedicine group and control group by using a computer-generated randomization number. A WeChat public telemedicine management platform was available to download at no cost. Patients in the telemedicine group registered in “The home of Xinqiao nutrition” platform in WeChat, which was developed by the Nutrition Department of Xinqiao hospital. Patients would get reminders for recording and uploading all daily meals and snacks, weight, fasting glucose, blood pressure and medication every day via WeChat, and our nutrition platform would check the data automatically and give an immediate warning message or feedback if the patient ate too much high glycemic index (GI) value food or saturated fat. The frequency of uploading data was recorded in WeChat. A 40-minute phone visit was conducted every month to educate patients about diet or exercise, such as eating low GI value food, moderate aerobic physical activity, or resistance exercise to increase mass of muscle and received a dietary guideline. Patients in the control group came to see the outpatient doctor per standard of care during the study period and received simple dietary advise or exercise recommendation each visit.

# Main outcomes and measures
All patients in both groups received clinical examination at the beginning of the study and by the end of the 12 months. (1) The information of sex, age, education background and household income were assessed by

questionnaire; (2) Measurements of height, weight, body mass index (BMI), waist circumference (WC) and body composition parameters (body fat percentage [BFP], body fat mass [BFM], lean body mass [LBM], body water [BW] and muscle mass [MM]) were conducted in a laboratory between 8:00-11:00 AM, after a minimum of 12 hours of fasting by using the In-Body (720) body composition analyzer (Biospace Co., Ltd., Seoul, Korea); (3) Blood samples were obtained after overnight fast. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) was measured via finger stick using blood glucose meter (Bayer, Germany). Serum was used to measure HbA1c, triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TCH), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), high density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c) by using automatic biochemical analyzer (Hitachi 7600-020, Japan); (4) Diastolic (DBP) and systolic (SBP) blood pressures were measured on the right arm after the patients had been resting for at least 5 minutes, with a standardized sphygmomanometer; (5) Quality of life (QOL) was measured with the Rand 36-Item Short Form (SF-36).8 The SF-36 has 8 subscales in total: 4 that measure mental aspects of QOL (RE, role limitations due to emotional problems; VT, vitality; SF, social functioning; MH, mental health) and 4 that measure physical aspects of QOL (PF, physical functioning; RP, role limitations due to physical problems; BP, bodily pain; GH, general health). Scores on the SF-36 range from 0 to 100, with higher scores reflecting better QOL; (6) Total costs included drug use, test strip use, advisory expense for dietary guideline or exercise recommendation, and other medications, but not cost of transportation for visit. It was estimated for 1 month before the initiation of intervention and the twelfth month during the intervention. The quality control is carried out regularly by the inspection department in Xinqiao hospital.

## Statistical analyses

The mean and standard deviation (SD) of HbA1c level were estimated as per a previous report.9 With 80% power for a two-sided and two-sample t-test at a 0.05 significance level, the sample size was set at 45 patients per group by using PASS software (version 2011). To allow for 10 % dropout for 12 months, a total sample size of 100 was sufficient.

SPSS software (version 20.0) and R software (version 3.4.4) were used for statistical analysis unless otherwise stated. An intent-to-treat (ITT) criterion was performed. The baseline variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test and Chi-square test for categorical outcomes, and unpaired t-test for continuous outcomes, as appropriate. Paired t-test was used for within group comparison of the repeated measures. Between group and within group comparisons of continuous variables were conducted using linear mixed effects models in “lmerTest” and “emmeans” packages in R. Time was treated as a categorical variable. The models included group and time as fixed covariates, as well as the group time interactions, with sex as covariates. Random intercepts for

patients accounted for the dependence of repeated measures.10 To evaluate the size of difference between and within the groups, effect size (ES) was performed, which reflects changes in SD units. Circos plot was used to exhibit the correlations among physical parameters or body composition parameters, biochemical variables, quality of life and total costs when Pearson’s correlation coefficient was bigger than 0.3 in “circlize” packages in R.11 All statistical tests were 2-tailed and, unless otherwise noted, P<0.05 was considered the level of statistical significance. Variables are expressed as frequencies with percentages and means (SD).

# RESULTS
## Participant characteristics

A total of 100 patients were included in the study. Each group comprised fifty patients. Three patients in the telemedicine group withdrew. The reason for the loss of follow-up was that patients did not upload blood glucose data or did not return to the outpatient department for blood sampling and reexamination (Figure 1). There were no significant differences between the two groups in age, sex, treatment, education and economic status before intervention (Table 1). There were no adverse events reported for all patients during the study.

## Correlations among different variables

The data of 12-month change variables revealed a different relationship among physical parameters or body composition parameters, biochemical variables, quality of life and total costs (Figure 2). Circos plot found that the FBG and HbA1c had more relationships with physical parameters or body composition parameters. Detailed correlation analysis showed that the estimated change of FBG was positively correlated with body composition parameters, like BFP, LBM and BFM (BFP: r=0.56, P<0.05; LBM: r=0.37, P<0.05; BFM: r=0.56, P<0.05, Supplementary Fig. 1) in the telemedicine group. Interestingly, the frequency of data uploading by patients in the telemedicine group was negatively correlated with weight, BMI and FBG (Weight: r=-0.79, P<0.05; BMI: r=- 0.78, P<0.05; FBG: r=-0.36, P<0.05, Supplementary Fig. 1). It also showed a negative association between frequency and body composition parameters, like BFP and BFM (BFP: r=-0.30, P<0.05; BFM: r=-0.47, P<0.05, Supplementary Fig. 1). This result highlights that the telemedicine intervention had improvement in glycemic

control correlating with improvements in body composition parameters.
