# 2. Methodology
A two-arm (usual care and intervention) randomized field trial was conducted for a period of six months among the patients attending the outpatient department of a tertiary care hospital in Mysuru city in southern India. Patients aged 18e65 years, diagnosed with type 2 Diabetes Mellitus with a minimum recorded duration of one year, receiving treatment with insulin and/or oral hypoglycemic drugs and own an Android smartphone were included in the study. Young patients aged below 18 years and geriatric patients aged above 65 years were not recruited for the study assuming that they may not be use the smartphone application effectively. Patients with severe complications, pregnant ladies and patients who were not capable of using a Smartphone application were excluded from the study. All the patients attending the General Medicine Out Patient Department with diabetes mellitus in April 2019 were assessed for eligibility and first eligible 300 patients (150 in each group), were selected conveniently for the study. The investigators were advised to make sure of the adherence of recruited patients on regular medication and monthly visit. Any patients discontinued the medication during the trial period were to be considered as lost to follow up. In order to ensure similarity between the two arms of the trial, researchers used a stratified random allocation method to allocate equal number of males and females in each group. As more males were reporting to the OPD, 90 males and 60 females were randomly distributed to each arm by concealed allocation by Sequentially numbered, opaque sealed envelopes (SNOSE) method. However, no further methods could be employed to do blinding at any level of the field trial.

The study was conducted after obtaining the relevant Institutional Review Board approval. After obtaining informed consent, details regarding socio-demographic characteristics were collected from the patients using a pre-tested structured questionnaire. The health-related quality of life of patients in both groups was assessed initially using the WHO QOL BREF questionnaire. The quality of life was assessed in four different domains called Physical Health

(Domain 1), Psychological (Domain 2), Social Relationships (Domain 3) and Environment (Domain 4). Patients in both groups received ongoing medical care as usual during the study period. Additionally, patients allocated to the intervention arm were given an android Smartphone application named ‘Diaguru’. The app provided the users with many features to help them in the management of diabetes. The values of the sugar level, insulin level and the type of food intake could be given as input. The app was automated to alert the user if the values were not in the normal range. The prescription given by the Doctor was stored using the app and was programmed to notify the user to take medicines on time. The caloric value of the food intake could be computed when the user selects the type of food he had consumed which were available under the categories such as breakfast, lunch, snacks and dinner. Based on his/her sugar level and the caloric count, the user was advised about the calories he required to take in a day. Reports and graphs were generated by the application to give them a clear idea of blood sugar levels. The app also answered FAQs and provided tips and references. The participants in the intervention arm used the mobile application for a period of 6 months from April 2019 to September 2019. The participants in the control arm received no such intervention and they were asked to continue the medication regularly. All the patients were assessed monthly as usual and adherence to medical treatment was ensured. All the participants were reassessed for the quality of life using the same WHO QOL BREF Questionnaire at the end of six months. The trial reported no loss to follow up cases in both the arms.

Data collected were entered in MS-Excel e 2010 and analyzed using a licensed version of SPSS version 22. The score of Quality of life in each domain was converted into a scale of 0e100 using the SPSS Syntax function provided in the questionnaire. Descriptive statistical measures like percentages, mean, median and standard deviation were applied. Inferential statistical tests like Wilcoxon signed rank test was done to assess the significance in difference of QOL scores before and after the study period in each group. The differences were interpreted as statistically significant at P < 0.05.

# 3. Results
The mean age of the study participants in the intervention group was 55.73 ± 10.45 years, while the mean age of the participants in the control group was 73.56 ± 11.27 years. Both the groups were composed of 90 (60%) males and 60 (40%) females. Among the group of patients who used the mobile application, 100 (66.6%) belonged to nuclear families, 40 (26.7%) belonged to joint families, and 10 (6.7%) belonged to three-generation families. In the control group of patients, the majority (135, 90%) were from nuclear families, while the remaining 10% were from joint families. Among the study participants in the intervention group, 5 (3.3%) were primary school educated, 10 (6.7%) were educated till secondary school, 10 (6.7%) had a diploma and 125 (83.3%) were graduates. In the control group, 20 (13.3%) were primary school educated, 90 (60%) were secondary school educated, 30 (20%) had a diploma and 10 (6.7%) were graduates. Of the 150 participants in the intervention group, 115 (76.7%) were doing business, and 35 (23.3%) were housewives. In the control group, 5 (3.3%) were skilled workers, 95 (63.3%) were doing business, and 50 (33.3%) were housewives (Table 1).

Among the 150 participants in the intervention group, 85 (56.7%) were on oral hypoglycemic agents during the study. While 45 (30%) were taking regular insulin, 20 (13.3%) were taking both insulin and oral hypoglycemic agents to control diabetes mellitus. In the non-intervention group, 70 (46.7%) were taking oral hypoglycemic agents, 45 (30%) were taking insulin, and 35 (23.3%) were taking both insulin and oral hypoglycemic agents. The median duration of diabetes mellitus among participants in the

intervention group was eight years with an interquartile range of 4e12 years while the median duration of illness among the participants in the control group was ten years with an interquartile range of 8e18 years. Hypertension was a co-morbidity in 45 (30%) participants in the intervention group and 80 (53.3%) participants in the control group. Of the 150 samples in the intervention group, 55 (36.7%) were consuming alcohol and 25 (16.7%) were smokers. In the control group, 20 (13.3%) participants were consuming alcohol, and 10 (6.7%) participants were smokers (Table 2).

A marked improvement in the quality of life was observed in the intervention group. Even though a little change in both directions was observed in the control group, the overall improvement in the quality of life was much higher in the intervention group. In the Physical health domain (Domain 1), the improvement in the quality of life was more in the intervention group (56 (44e63) to 63 (50e69)) compared to the control group (38 (19e56) to 38 (31e56)). The difference in the intervention group was statistically significant, with a p value less than 0.001 on the Wilcoxon signed rank test. In the psychological domain (Domain 2), the median scores of quality of life of patients who received the intervention improved from 50 (44e63) to 38 (31e56). The median score was showing a minor improvement in the control group from 44 (38e44) to 44 (44e56). Both the observations showed a statistical significance on Wilcoxon signed rank test with a p value < 0.05. The mean score of quality of life in Domain 3 of social relationships was improved in the intervention group from 50 (44e63) to 69 (56e75). This was statistically significant with a p value less than 0.001 in Wilcoxon signed rank test. However, the scores were showing a trend of decrease in the control group with the median score reducing from 50 (44e50) to 47 (31e50). However, this difference was not statistically significant. In the environment domain (Domain 4), a statistically significant difference in scores was obtained in both the groups between the initial and final assessment.

The difference was more in the intervention group (56 (50e69) to 63 (50e69)) compared to the control group (50 (38e56) to 47 (38e56)). (Table 3). The median scores of quality of life before and after the intervention, in the intervention and non-intervention group, are depicted in Figs. 1 and 2.

The change in scores of quality of life in all the domains in patients in the intervention group was compared with that of patients in the control group. A median change of scores of 6 (0e13), 7 (0e13), 0 (0e6) and 0 (0e6) was observed in domain 1 to 4, respectively, in the intervention group. However, no such change was noticed in the individuals belonging to the control group after six months. This difference in change was statistically significant in all four domains with a p value < 0.001 on performing a Mann Whitney U test (Table 4).
