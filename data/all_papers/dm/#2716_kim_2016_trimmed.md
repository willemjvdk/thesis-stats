# Research Design and Methods
## SUBJECTS

This study used a randomized, open-label, parallel group design to evaluate effect of the IBGMS among Chinese subjects with diabetes. The study subjects were male and female outpatients at the First Bethune Hospital at Jilin University, China, who had been diagnosed with diabetes for over 1 year. The inclusion criteria were subjects who had glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) levels of 7.0–10.0% at the time of admission, had an Internet connection at home, and provided their voluntary informed consent. We excluded patients who were pregnant or who had a serious medical disease, serious diabetic complication, or aspartate transaminase (AST) or alanine transaminase (ALT) levels of ‡2.5· above the normal upper limit. In addition, we excluded patients who had not taken their medication regularly in the 3 months before enrollment, those who were engaged in other clinical studies, and those without access to computers. As this study was an open clinical study, blinding was not necessary. The study design was approved by the China Research Ethic Committee, and informed consent was obtained from each patient.

## STUDY DESIGN

An open recruitment of subjects was initiated in October 2013. Subjects first signed a voluntary consent form and were then evaluated for their suitability before they were randomly assigned to the IBGMS or control groups. A block randomization design with a block size of four and a ratio of 1:1 was used to ensure a balanced distribution. The IBGMS group received blood sugar monitoring through the Internet, while the control group received the conventional treatment. However, both the IBGMS and the control groups visited the hospital every 3 months for laboratory testing and a clinical examination to evaluate the safety and efficacy of their treatment. Laboratory testing included baseline HbA1c levels, white blood cell counts with the differential counts, red blood cell counts, hemoglobin and hematocrit levels, platelet counts, fasting blood sugar, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, AST, ALT, sodium, and potassium levels. In addition, the patients’ lipid profiles were evaluated, which included total cholesterol

(TC), triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). The subjects’ height and weight were evaluated, and both groups were educated regarding the importance of maintaining a physical regimen, kinesiatrics, and pharmacotherapy. The subjects’ in both groups were also provided glucometers (My-GlucoHealth OneTouch UltraEasy; LifeScan) and 150 testing strips.

## INTERNET-BASED GLUCOSE MONITORING SYSTEM

Subjects in the IBGMS group received specific training regarding the IBGMS after registering at the Web site (www .china-diabetes.com). Using this system, the subjects measured their blood sugar using a system that was connected to their computer, and the results were automatically sent and saved to an online server. Using their computer, subjects were able to view changes in their blood sugar levels as well as receive messages from the medical team (Fig. 1).

In this group, subjects uploaded their blood sugar data according to an established schedule (below), and the medical staff checked the subjects’ health conditions on a regular basis. For the first 3 months, the nurses provided recommendations regarding blood sugar control on a weekly basis, and then biweekly recommendations were provided for the remaining 3 months. If any subject failed to upload their blood sugar data for more than 1 week, they were provided with an individualized warning message and were subsequently excluded from the study if they failed to upload data after three consecutive warning messages or phone calls.

Subjects in the IBGMS group were required to submit their blood sugar data at least twice per week, and the number of submissions was adjusted according to the severity of the subject’s condition. For patients with HbA1c levels of <7% and who were not receiving diabetes medications, two tests per week were recommended. For patients with HbA1c levels of <7% and who were receiving diabetes medications, daily testing was recommended. More than one test per day was recommended for subjects with Hb1Ac levels of 7–8% and who were receiving diabetes medications, or for subjects with HbA1c levels of >8% (regardless of medication). Subjects who were receiving insulin treatments and had <7% HbA1c were asked to take their blood sugar levels twice per day, and those who were receiving insulin treatments with >7% HbA1c were asked to take their blood sugar levels more than three times per day. Subjects who completed less than half of the recommended tests or who exhibited low blood sugar levels were provided with consultations through text messaging or phone calls.

## SYSTEM ESTABLISHMENT AND RESEARCH DESIGN

The team in Korea served as the system control center for technical support, and also as the health coordinating center, to provide the clinical research protocol and educate the Chinese medical team. A Chinese version of the IBGMS was developed based on a system that has been verified in various previous clinical studies to accommodate the local Chinese conditions. The Chinese webpage, contents, and equipment menus were developed with help from the Chinese medical team. For the education sessions, the Korean medical team visited China to conduct three education sessions regarding the Web site and operation guidelines with the Chinese doctors, nurses, and nutritionists. Preparation of the institutional review board-related material, electronic case report forms, clinical research schedule and documents, and translations was also conducted. The clinical research guidelines were planned and organized in Korea and included the subjects’ blood sugar level goals, numbers of blood sugar level tests, and medical feedback from the subjects regarding the results. The Chinese team conducted the study promotion, subject recruitment, clinical research, and

data collection according to the clinical research protocol. Two demonstrations of the sample trial period were completed before progressing to the clinical research stage. Subjects’ inquiries or complaints were received at the diabetes center in China, and these inquiries were supported through recommendations that were received from Korea.

## STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

The baseline characteristics of the two groups were compared using an unpaired t-test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. We also conducted an analysis of the mean intragroup change at each time point using a paired t-test. To improve the normality of the analysis, we performed a log transformation for several variables that exhibited a skewed distribution. The change in HbA1c between the two groups was compared at baseline and 3 and 6 months using a repeated measures analysis of variance. p-Values <0.05 were considered statistically significant, and data were analyzed using a standard statistical software package (SAS version 9.2; SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

During the 6-month study period, 20 participants were excluded from the control group and 18 participants were excluded from the IBGMS group, which left 90 participants in the control group and 92 participants in the IBGMS group for the final analysis. ## EFFECT ON GLYCEMIC CONTROL

Both groups had significantly different profiles in their changes in HbA1c levels over time (Figure 2a). At 3 months, the HbA1c levels in the control group were reduced from 8 . 0 \% \pm 0 . 8 \%to 7.3% – 1.2% \scriptstyle { \left( { p < 0 . 0 0 1 } \right) }, and the HbA1c levels in the IBGMS group were reduced from 7 . 9 \% \pm 0 . 8 \%to 6 . 9 \% \pm 0 . 7 \%\scriptstyle { \left( { p < 0 . 0 0 1 } \right) }. Both groups also exhibited significant reductions in blood sugar levels (Table 2). However, the IBGMS group exhibited a more significant 6-month decrease compared to the control group ( 6 . 7 \% \pm 0 . 7 \%vs. 7.4% – 1.3%, p { < } 0 . 0 0 1 ), although there were no significant differences in the HbA1c changes between months 3 and 6. However, the HbA1c levels in the control group tended to increase slightly over this period ( 7 . 3 \% \pm 1 . 1 \%to 7.4% – 1.3%, p = 0.605) and the HbA1c levels in the IBGMS group tended to decrease slightly ( 6 . 9 0 / 0 \pm 0 . 7 \%to 6 . 7 \% \pm 0 . 7 \% ,, p = 0.081).

In the control group, there were no significant changes in the subjects’ fasting and postprandial blood sugar levels. However, significant changes in fasting blood sugar were observed in the IBGMS group at month 3 \scriptstyle ( p = 0 . 0 0 3 )and month \scriptstyle { \bar { \mathsf { ) } } } ( p = 0 . 0 0 5 ), and a significant decrease in postprandial blood sugar level was observed at month 6 \left( p < 0 . 0 0 1 \right).

## CHANGES IN ANTHROPOMETRY, BLOOD PRESSURE, AND LIPID PROFILE

Neither of the groups exhibited any significant change in their mean BMI. A significant increase in systolic blood pressure was observed in both groups at month 3, although only the IBGMS group had significantly increased systolic blood pressure at month 6. In contrast, diastolic blood pressure tended to decrease in both groups, although the only significant difference was observed in the IBGMS group at month 6. In the control group, TC, TG, and LDL-C levels decreased significantly and HDL-C levels increased significantly, while only TG levels decreased significantly in the IBGMS group.

## EFFECT ON BLOOD SUGAR LEVELACCORDING TO AGE GROUP

To examine the effect of age and IBGMS monitoring on the subjects’ blood sugar levels, we divided each group into three subgroups according to age: <50 years old, 50–60 years old, and >60 years old (Table 3). In the IBGMS group, the subjects in all age groups experienced a significant decrease in HbA1c levels at all follow-ups, although only patients who were >50 years

old in the control group exhibited a significant decrease in HbA1c levels. In the control group, patients who were <50 years old exhibited a small decrease in blood sugar and HbA1c levels, although the decrease was not statistically significant ( p = 0.133). In the control group, patients who were >50 years old experienced a decrease in HbA1c levels at month 3, although their HbA1c levels increased slightly at month 6. In contrast, a decrease in HbA1c levels was observed at each follow-up for all ages in the IBGMS group. There were no significant differences in fasting blood glucose, postprandial blood glucose, and HbA1c levels when we compared the IBGMS age groups.
