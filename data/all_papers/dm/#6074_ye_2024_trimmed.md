# Materials and methods
## Patient selection

This study is a prospective, randomized controlled trial conducted over a period of 26 weeks. A total of 174 patients (96 males and 78 females) with coexisting type 2 diabetes and hypertension who visited the 900th Hospital of Joint Logistic Support Force between October 2022 and March 2023 were selected as participants. The study received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the 900th Hospital of the Joint Logistics Support Force (2022-094) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the revised Declaration of Helsinki. Prior to participation, written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The trial was registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (registration no. ChiCTR2300068743, 28/02/2023).

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) meets the diagnostic criteria for type 2 diabetes; (2) diagnosed with hypertension; (3) age 30–60 years; (4) able to independently and profciently use smartphones and WeChat; (5) has at least a junior high school education and is able to read and understand the

content of WeChat push articles; (6) voluntarily participates and signs an informed consent form.

Exclusion criteria: (1) patients with type 1 diabetes; (2) patients with severe acute and chronic complications of diabetes or hypertension; (3) patients with serious heart, lung, liver, and other organ dysfunctions; (4) patients who were unable to consistently cooperate with the visit were excluded from the study due to poor compliance with medication, tests, or treatment regimen; (5) patients with mental disorders or communication barriers; (6) patients and their families who cannot fully cooperate with the research.

## Intervention and follow‑up

The patients’ basic information was assessed by diabetes and hypertension specialists who established case fles. The patients were randomly assigned to either the control group or the remote access education group using the random number method. To ensure equal distribution of patients between the two groups, unique participant codes were assigned to all eligible patients. A computerised random number generator was then used to create a list of random numbers that corresponded to the codes. The patients were assigned randomly to two groups using a generated list of random numbers. The groups were strictly ordered based on the sequence of the random numbers to ensure an equal number of patients in both groups, following a 1:1 allocation ratio. In the control group, patients received treatment according to the conventional diagnostic and treatment model. This treatment involved regular check-ups supervised by medical professionals, including physicians and nurses, tailored medication adjustments for each patient, and education on daily diet and exercise. Although patients in the control group had the option to contact the physician by phone, the physician did not initiate contact with the patients and did not actively supervise their diet, exercise, or blood glucose monitoring. Both groups of patients require outpatient follow-up visits every 3 months.

The telehealth education group was established based on the existing clinic routine. The following interventions were implemented: 1) patients were added to a WeChat group that consisted of two endocrinologists, one cardiologist, and three nurses. All medical staf members received training on relevant knowledge, interventions, and follow-up measures to ensure consistent care measures; 2) doctors and nurses posted daily on WeChat regarding basic knowledge about diabetes and high blood pressure, as well as the norms of daily management for these conditions. Through the WeChat group, they also answered patients’ questions regarding any confusion or misunderstandings related to the daily management of their conditions. Additionally, guidance was provided on how to cope with issues such as hypoglycemia; 3) patients were instructed on how to adopt a proper lifestyle,

correct unhealthy behaviors, and improve their knowledge of diabetes and hypertension; 4) medical staf encouraged patients to actively share their personal experiences within the group. All the work mentioned above is based on WeChat groups. This approach aimed to improve patient motivation and compliance with treatment. These interventions were implemented to enhance patient education, support, and engagement, thereby promoting better self-management of diabetes and hypertension within the telehealth education group.

# Outcome measures
## Laboratory test index

The study collected general demographic characteristics of the patients, including age and gender. Disease-related information was also recorded, which included the duration of diabetes and hypertension, the presence of complications, and the use of hypoglycemic, antihypertensive, and lipidmodulating drugs. Baseline indicators for both the control and telehealth education groups were collected prior to the start of the study. Trained and qualifed health professionals accurately recorded the initial health status of each participant according to the established study protocol. At the end of the study, the same health professionals conducted a follow-up data collection exercise using the same methodology for both groups of participants. These indicators included body weight, body mass index (BMI), fasting blood glucose (FBG), postprandial 2-h blood glucose (2h-PBG), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), systolic blood pressure (Systolic BP), diastolic blood pressure (Diastolic BP), total cholesterol (TC), triacylglycerols (TG), HDLcholesterol (HDL-C), and LDL-cholesterol (LDL-C). These measurements provided a baseline assessment of the patients’ health status before the intervention.

The observation period for the study was 26 weeks. Changes in weight, BMI, fasting blood glucose, postprandial 2-h blood glucose, HbA1c, Systolic BP, Diastolic BP, TC, TG, HDL-C, and LDL-C were measured before and after the intervention in both the control group and the telehealth education group. BMI was calculated for each patient by dividing their weight in kilograms by the square of their height in meters. HbA1c levels were measured using the Bayer DCA2000 glycated hemoglobin analyzer. FBG and postprandial 2h-PBG levels were measured using the hexokinase method. HbA1c levels were also detected using the Bayer DCA2000 + HbA1c analyzer. TC, TG, HDL-C, and LDL-C levels were determined using the lipase method and the Olympus AU2700 automatic biochemical analyzer. Systolic BP and Diastolic BP measurements were taken in the sitting position using an electronic sphygmomanometer (BP705) manufactured by AMpall. Before measurement,

the patient was required to sit still for more than 5 min. The average of three consecutive measurements was considered the effective blood pressure. If there was a signifcant diference between the three measurements, a professional physician would take the average value of the two measurements that were more similar after an additional measurement.

## Diabetes self‑management

The Diabetes Self-Care Activity Questionnaire (SDSCA) was utilized in the study to assess participants’ selfmanagement behaviors. In studies involving such questionnaires, participants were asked to complete the questionnaire themselves, based on their own experiences and behaviors related to diabetes self-care. Upon receiving the questionnaire, participants would have read and responded to the items that asked about various aspects of diabetes self-management, such as diet, exercise, blood sugar monitoring, medication adherence, and other self-care activities. It’s important to clarify that while the questionnaire was self-administered, the health professionals overseeing the study would have provided instructions on how to complete it accurately, clarifed any questions participants might have had, and ensured that they understood each item on the questionnaire. Additionally, they would have collected the completed questionnaires for analysis in a manner that maintained the confdentiality and integrity of the participants’ responses.

The SDSCA is designed to measure various aspects of self-care, including diet, physical activity, blood glucose testing, medication use, and foot care. It consists of a total of 15 questions, with 5 questions related to diet, two questions related to physical activity, 2 questions related to blood glucose testing, 1 question related to medication use, and 5 questions related to foot care [7]. Participants were asked to indicate how many of the past seven days they had engaged in appropriate self-care activities for each aspect. Scores for each aspect were averaged to calculate total scores, which ranged from 0 (lowest) to 35 (highest). Higher scores on the scale indicate better self-management behavior. The SDSCA is a widely used tool that has demonstrated good reliability and validity in assessing diabetes self-care activities. It provides valuable insights into the participants’ adherence to self-management behaviors and their overall diabetes self-care.

## Sample size

To estimate the required sample size for the study, several assumptions were made. It was assumed that there would be no change in the control group, while the telehealth education group would show a 15% improvement in blood

glucose levels. The margin of advantage was set at 10%. The standard deviation of the mean diference was estimated to be 20%. With an 80% power condition, a type 1 error rate of 0.05, a randomization rate of 1:1, and a dropout rate of 20%, the estimated sample size was calculated to be 75 patients per group. This sample size estimation takes into account the statistical power required to detect a signifcant diference between the control and telehealth education groups, as well as the potential dropout rate. By having 75 patients in each group, the study is expected to have a sufcient sample size to achieve the desired power and signifcance level for the research fndings.

## Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses for the study were conducted using SPSS 22.0 software. The data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables and as median (interquartile range) for variables that were not normally distributed. To analyze the betweengroup diferences in baseline change for the study endpoints, an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed. The randomised group was included as a fxed efect, and the baseline measurements of the dependent variable were included as a covariate. The signifcance of diferences between means was assessed using paired t-tests for withingroup comparisons and unpaired t-tests for betweengroup comparisons. For continuous variables, including baseline indices and change from baseline to endpoint values, ANCOVA was used to compare the differences between groups. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered

statistically signifcant, indicating a signifcant diference between the compared groups or variables.

# Results
## Baseline characteristics of the study participants

In this study, a total of 174 patients with diabetes combined with hypertension were assessed for eligibility. Fourteen patients were excluded, with 8 not meeting the inclusion criteria, 3 not having a smartphone, and 3 declining to participate. Thus, a fnal sample of 160 patients was randomly assigned to two groups, with 80 patients in the control group and 80 patients in the telehealth education group. Throughout the trial, 155 patients completed the study, with 77 patients in the control group and 78 patients in the telehealth education group (Fig. 1). Baseline demographic, clinical, and laboratory data for both groups were collected and are presented in Table 1. There were no statistically signifcant baseline characteristics when comparing both groups.

The collected data included information such as age, gender, duration of diabetes and hypertension, body mass index (BMI), fasting blood glucose levels, postprandial 2-h blood glucose levels, HbA1c levels, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, total cholesterol, triacylglycerols, HDLcholesterol, and LDL-cholesterol levels. These baseline measurements provide a comparison of the characteristics of the control and telehealth education groups at the start of the trial.
