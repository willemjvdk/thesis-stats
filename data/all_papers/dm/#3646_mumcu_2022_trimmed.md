## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
Sixty-six women were randomly assigned to either an intervention group (n ¼ 30) in which web-based education was provided for 6 months or a control group (n ¼ 31) in which they received standard NP care. Patients included in the sample were selected among women between 18 and 65 years old who were diagnosed with type 2 diabetes, were using insulin, had no communication disability, participated in this study voluntarily, and could use a computer or a smartphone. All of the participants were selected from patients who applied to the internal medicine and endocrine outpatient clinic of a training and research hospital in Ankara between December 2019 and August 2020.

Data were collected in the outpatient clinic room using patient identification, metabolic control variables, Diabetes Self-Care Scale (DSCS), and Diabetes Family Support and Conflict Scale (DFSC) in type 2 diabetes forms that were administered in the outpatient clinic room by a face-to-face interview with the individuals included in the study when they came to their appointments. Toward the end of the study, some forms were administered by email or phone calls because of the pandemic and the implementation of some restrictions.

Metabolic control variables, which were routinely checked at 3 months from the participants, were obtained. No additional blood was taken from the participants, and no consultancy training was given to the control group by the researcher. At the end of the study, the control group was given the same website address as the intervention group. Usernames and passwords were also created for the individuals in the control group so that they could read the information available on the website. Thus, they received the same education as the intervention group. During the study, 2 participants from the intervention group and 3 participants from the control group left voluntarily.

## Creation of the Website

An informative website was created for the intervention group. The content of the website was brought together by scanning the literature, and the website was accessible during the study period. The content text was quoted from publicly available information and guides.5,6 The pictures on the website were obtained from license-free sources.

The content of the website was divided into 6 educational modules. These modules were published sequentially during the 6- month period by allowing access to a new module every month. There was also an educational video on how to make an insulin injection prepared by the researcher on the website. The titles of the educational modules were as follows: Diabetes Is the Disease of Which Organ, Nutrition in Diabetes, Exercise, Acute-Chronic Complications, Importance of Family Support in Diabetes, and Importance of Professional Help. A reminder message was sent twice a week to the participants regarding the subject of the training module. In the first module, Diabetes Is the Disease of Which Organ, the mechanism of diabetes mellitus in the pancreas was explained. The Nutrition in Diabetes module consisted of information on healthy and balanced nutrition that will provide weight control. The Exercise module included how to gradually increase physical activity and the exercise plan. In the Acute-Chronic Complications module, the complications were explained, especially lifethreatening hypoglycemia, the prevention of complications, and foot care. In the Importance of Family Support in Diabetes module, the duties of family members were mentioned. In the last education module, the Importance of Professional Help, information about when to apply to health institutions for people with diabetes was provided.

The website was developed using Microsoft Asp.NET MVC technology, and it was designed responsively for ease of use. The website could be accessed from computers, tablets, and smartphones. Participants were informed to visit the website regularly. Expert opinions were obtained using the DISCERN measurement tool to evaluate the reliability and quality of the training material. A different username and password were provided separately for each of the 31 individuals in the intervention group. In addition to the user content, the website had a separate management panel that was only used by the researcher. This management panel allowed the researcher to access all the logs about the visited pages and the time spent on the pages by each participant of the intervention group.

## Analysis of Data

Statistical analysis of the data was performed using SPSS Statistics 24 (IBM Corp). The independent samples t test, paired samples t test, repeated measures test, analysis of variance, and Pearson correlation coefficient were used among the parametric methods for the measurement values suitable for normal distribution, whereas the Mann-Whitney U, Wilcoxon, Friedman, and Kruskal-Wallis H tests and the Spearman correlation coefficient method were used among the nonparametric methods for measurement values not suitable for normal distribution. The Fisher exact test, chi-square cross-tabulation, and McNemar test were used to examine the relationship between 2 qualitative variables.

# Results
During the study, 2 participants from the intervention group and 3 participants from the control group voluntarily left. The majority of the intervention (65%) and control (70%) groups were between 50-65 years old. Most participants in the intervention (77%) and control (73%) groups were married. The majority of the intervention (39%) and control (37%) groups were primary school graduates. It was observed that the intervention and control groups were independent and homogeneous in terms of sociodemographic characteristics.

There was a statistically significant difference (t  8.202, P .000) between the DSCS scores based on the time (0 and 6 months) in the intervention group, and it was determined that the DSCS scores at 6 months were statistically significantly higher than at 0 months (Table 1). In the control group, no statistically significant difference was found in terms of the DSCS scores over time (P > .05; Table 1). When the difference between the DSCS scores of the intervention and control groups was examined, no significant differences in the first test were found. On the other hand, the DSCS scores at 6 months were statistically significantly higher in the intervention group compared with the control group (t 3.619, P .001; Table 1).

The family conflict subdimension scores were found to be statistically significantly higher at 6 months in the intervention group compared with the first test; this was not observed in the control group (t 2.378, P .024; Table 2). There were no differences in the family support subdimension. It was determined that the 6- month DFSC total scores in the intervention group were statistically significantly higher than that of the control group (t 2.751, P .008; Table 2). Although there was no difference in the control group in the examinations made according to education levels, the scale scores in the intervention group were found to be statistically significantly higher in high school and higher education levels compared with other education levels. The metabolic control variables based on time were examined for the intervention and control groups. The fasting blood glucose levels of the intervention

and control groups at month 0 were statistically significantly higher than the values at 3 and 6 months. Although there was a minimal decrease in the hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) variable in the intervention group, a minimal increase was observed in the control group at 3 and 6 months compared with the values before the webbased education.

A comparison of the variables of regular insulin administration, measuring blood glucose, exercise, snacking, and dietary compliance before and after the web-based education is provided in Table 3. In the intervention group, it was observed that the regular application of insulin injections (70.9%-87%) and the blood glucose measurement status (67.7%-80.6%) increased. In the control group, the status of measuring blood glucose (46.6%-50%) increased, but it was found that there was no change in the regular insulin application. At the same time, there were positive percentage increases in exercise, snack, and diet compliance rates. It was determined that there was a decrease in the number of logins to the website toward the last months of the study. During phone calls made by the researcher to the patients, it was determined that some individuals forgot the website address or their password, which could be the reason for the decrease in the use of the website. It was determined that the most frequently visited page was Diabetes Is the Disease of Which Organ, and the second most frequently visited page was Nutrition followed by How Are Insulin Injections Done. The pages having the longest visit times were the pages titled Nutrition, Complications, and Treatment, respectively. It was observed that the educational video was

watched 1,834 times. When each participant was examined separately based on the total time spent on the website, it was determined that the longest was 3,412 minutes and the shortest was 98 minutes, with an average time of 1,530 minutes.
