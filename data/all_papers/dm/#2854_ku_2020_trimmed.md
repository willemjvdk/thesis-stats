# Methods
## Study design and participants

This is a 12-week pilot randomised controlled trial involving patients with T2D. The inclusion criteria were as follows: adult patients with T2D, patients aged between 20 and 80 years, patients who received treatment between July 2014 and June 2015 at Chungbuk National University Hospital (CBNUH), and patients who were willing and able to provide informed consent to participate in this study. Patients were excluded if they had a significant cognitive impairment, were unable to operate smartphone applications, or used any medication (active anti-cancer treatment, steroids, immunosuppressants, alternative medicine containing steroids, appetite suppressants, etc.) or had any condition that might affect blood glucose control (e.g. chronic wasting disease such as malnutrition, malabsorptive diseases, tuberculosis, solid organ transplantation, etc.). The included patients were randomised into the SC and CC groups using a freely available online automated random number generator program.12

## Ethics statement

This study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of CBNUH (IRB No: CBNUH2014-06-006). Formal written consent was obtained from each participant before inclusion in the study.

## Intervention

The intervention began with individual meetings between the participants and the researcher. All patients were taught to test and record their blood glucose levels and were asked to exercise and keep a food diary. All patients were recommended the following exercise programmes: strategies to avoid hypoglycaemia, adequate types of exercise (aerobic/ resistant/flexible), intensity (at a moderate level), frequency (at least three times per week) and duration (at least 150 min per week). Both groups of patients received conventional diabetes care education from a trained nurse at baseline. Subsequently, the intervention group received the designated situational text message feedback and the privilege to access the smartphone application. The following interventions were provided: an internet program connected with a server to access the daily entries, situational individualised text feedback, an installed healthcare tool and social network group activities comprising six to eight members. Feedback text messages were sent by the medical team within a day after a comprehensive assessment of daily blood glucose profile, food intake and physical activity information registered at the website. The intervention lasted for 12 weeks. There was no direct phone call or face-to-face counselling by the nurse other than the designated situational text message sent to the intervention group. The CC group did not receive either any text message feedback or any diabetes education consultation after the baseline education. Detailed intervention protocols were described in the Supporting Information Methods S1.

## The mHealth application, ‘Noom Coach’ and the blood glucose meter, CareSens N NFC

We developed a smartphone-based integrated online real-time diabetes care system using smartphone mHealth application. Noom Coach (Noom Inc., New York, NY, USA), a mHealth application, was used as a dietary diary. This is an application intended for weight control and dietary management. Subjects in the SC group were asked to log their dietary data using this

application. This tool enables the user to calculate their dietary intake easily using a well established database of local foods. The Noom database contains more than 3.7 million food options and has up-to-date 50 000 Korean food items that are updated by registered nutritionists. Noom used an internal database to calculate automatically the calories of food items entered by users. When a meal is recorded in the application, a colour code (red, yellow and green) is presented to help patients choose healthier foods within the allotted calories per day (green, ‘It’s a good choice!’; yellow, ‘Please eat only moderate amounts’; red, ‘Sometimes, just a little’). Additionally, a pedometer function recorded the amount of daily physical activity.

All participants were provided with CareSens N NFC blood glucose meters (i-SENS Inc., Korea) and were instructed to familiarise themselves on how to use the instrument. A near-field communication (NFC)-enabled smartphone could read the blood glucose data from this glucose meter when placed within a range of 10 cm (i.e. patients swipe their smartphones over glucose meters to wirelessly transfer their glucose information). Blood glucose data were transferred daily to the database server, and researchers had online access to these data.

# Outcome measures
The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of patients who achieved a target glycated haemoglobin (A1C) level of <6.5% (48 mmol/mol) at week 12. The criteria, A1C <6.5%, was established according to the domestic guideline for T2D (Korean Diabetes Association).13 This is a target blood glucose level recommended for preventing complications through early, aggressive and strict control of blood glucose in patients with T2D. The secondary outcomes were the changes in A1c from baseline to week 12, and a validated questionnaire-based comparison of patients receiving SC versus CC in terms of their attitudes to self-diabetes care using the Summary of Diabetes Self-Care Activities (SDSCA) score. The following data were recorded at baseline: age, sex, body mass index (BMI), systolic and diastolic blood pressure, duration of diabetes, familial history of diabetes, method of diabetes treatment and presence of comorbidities. At baseline and after 12 weeks, A1C and fasting plasma glucose were measured, and SDSCA scores were assessed. A1C was measured using affinity chromatography (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA) in a National Glycohemoglobin Standardisation Program level II – certified laboratory. After a 12-h (overnight) fasting period, venous blood was sampled prior to the intervention and used in biochemical assays. The concentrations of

plasma glucose, serum total cholesterol, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-cholesterol), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-cholesterol), aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) were measured using a Hitachi 7600 chemistry analyser (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan). The fasting C-peptide and insulin levels were measured using a chemiluminescence enzyme immunoassay (Abbott, Lake Forest, IL, USA) at the first visit. The homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and β-cell function (HOMA-β) were calculated using the following respective equations: (fasting glucose (mg/dL) × fasting insulin/405) and (360 × fasting insulin/(fasting glucose (mg/dL) − 63)).14 The estimated glomerular filtration rate was calculated by the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease equation (MDRD eGFR).15

## Summary of the diabetes self-care activities questionnaire

The SDSCA is a 12-item self-report questionnaire used to assess the level of self-diabetes management.16 Study participants were asked to report the number of days in the previous week when they participated in the five aspects of diabetes management: general diet, specific diet, exercise, blood glucose testing and foot care (e.g. ‘How many times have you followed a healthful eating plan during the last 7 days?’). The SDSCA questionnaire was administered at baseline and at week 12. The total SDSCA scores were calculated by summing the item subscales. A higher total SDSCA score indicated a more frequent self-management behaviour.

## Safety assessments

The safety evaluation included a physical examination and assessments of vital signs, laboratory data and adverse experiences. Hypoglycaemia was defined as a blood glucose level <70 mg/dL, and severe hypoglycaemia was defined as altered consciousness or a requirement for hospitalisation.

## Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS for Windows (version 20.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data were presented as numbers and percentages for categorical variables and as means and standard deviations for continuous variables. All data were normally distributed, with concordant Kolmogorov–Smirnov results, histograms, box plots and normal probability

plots. The SPSS program facilitated the testing of standard data transformations (including logarithmic). The baseline characteristics were compared using Student t-test for continuous variables and the Mann–Whitney U-test for non-normally distributed data. Independence between categorical parameters was assessed using the \chi ^ { 2 }test. The variables at baseline were compared to those at the end of the 12-week study using a paired ttest. Multivariate linear regression models were conducted to assess the association between the smartphone intervention and conventional diabetes care. The data were analysed according to the full-analysis-set approach. A P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

To analyse the efficacy of the intervention, the percentage of participants in each group who achieved the target A1C level of ≤6.5% was analysed using the \chi ^ { 2 }test and Fisher exact test. Changes in A1C from baseline to week 12 were analysed using paired t-test. Changes in the SDSCA scores from before to after the intervention in each group were analysed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and differences in the

changes in SDSCA scores between groups were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U-test. To confirm the association between the changes in the A1C level and other variables, correlation analyses with age, duration of diabetes, baseline BMI, blood pressure, baseline A1C, total cholesterol, triglyceride, HDL-cholesterol, LDL-cholesterol, AST, ALT, MDRD eGFR and SDSCA scores were performed. The independent t-test was used for the categorical variables of sex and familial history of diabetes.

Of the 46 initially recruited patients, 40 (87%) were enrolled in this study. Seventeen and 18 patients who were assigned to the SC and CC groups, respectively, completed the study (Fig. 1). In addition to the A1C levels, the groups exhibited similarities in other laboratory data, including lipid profiles, hepatic function and renal function.

## Association variables of treatment efficacy and safety

In the SC group, the change in the A1C level was negatively associated with the baseline A1C level (r = −0.868, P < 0.001). There were no significant correlations of the change in A1C with age, duration of diabetes, baseline BMI, levels of cholesterol, triglycerides, AST, ALT and MDRD GFR, and the initial SDSCA scores. The changes in A1C levels in the CC group were associated with age, baseline A1C and triglyceride levels (age, r = 0.558, P = 0.016; baseline A1C, r = - 0 . 6 9 7 ., P = 0 . 0 0 1; triglyceride, r = −0.570, P = 0.033). The change in A1C was not associated with the duration of diabetes, baseline BMI, lipid profile (except triglyceride), hepatic or renal function.
