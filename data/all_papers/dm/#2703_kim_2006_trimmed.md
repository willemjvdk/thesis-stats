# RESULTS
## Participants’ Characteristics

The mean age of participants was 55.1 years (SD, 7.42). The sample consisted of 39 males (53.4%) and 34 females (46.6%). The mean duration of diabetes was 7.3 years (SD, 6.05). There were 50 participants (16 from the UC group, 17 from the WB group, and 17 from the PM group) who were taking oral hypoglycemic medications; six participants (two from the UC group, one from the WB group, and three from the PM group) taking antihypertensive medications; and five participants (one from the UC group and two from each intervention group) taking lipid-lowering medications. However, the three groups did not differ significantly in the type of medications participants were taking ( P = . 4 6 ). ## Issues and Experiences With the Web-based Intervention

A majority (82.9%) of participants who received WB intervention reported satisfaction with the intervention program. Common features they liked included promptness of feedback and information, ability to follow up on their trends of physical activity, availability of updated results, and the specific and selective nature of individualized information about physical activity. Among the Web features, participants particularly

favored graphic assessment tools for physical activity and up-to-date activity sheets.

Because there are a large number of patients with type 2 diabetes who do not have access to a computer or the Internet in South Korea, the value of a WB program was first tested as offered by healthcare providers. Only 26.9% of the study participants indicated their access to and comfort with using the Internet. Thus, two research nurses administered all intervention programs, representing healthcare providers. They found that the first session of WB intervention took 50 to 105 minutes, compared with 60 to 90 minutes for the PM intervention. However, subsequent WB sessions took 10 to 15 minutes per session, compared with 20 to 50 minutes for the PM intervention. More than half of participants were in the early readiness stages of physical activity (precontemplation to contemplation). The program providers also found that intermittent physical activity was much more effective than was continuous activity for participants who were too busy to do physical activity. The American College of Sports Medicine recommends that multiple sessions of moderate intensity physical activity, such as brisk walking, could be cumulative to a total of 30 minutes a day if each session lasts 8 to 10 minutes.21 The program providers also recommended that participants restart physical activity at the level of initial intensity if physical activity was stopped for more than 1 week.

Other issues were related to training of healthcare providers, data entry, and Web site development and maintenance. Healthcare providers had to be fully trained for the constructs and meaning of TTM and the use of the WB program before implementation the intervention. The accuracy of data entry was a significant advantage of the WB program because the data were automatically saved into a computer server without manual entry. The Web site was designed by nurse researchers with technical assistance of a Web site programmer. However, continuous efforts and resources were required to maintain the server, which needed to be clearly anticipated.
