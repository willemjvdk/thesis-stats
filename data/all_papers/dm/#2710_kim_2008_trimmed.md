# 2. Methods
## 2.1. Sample

Participants were recruited from the endocrinology outpatient department of a tertiary care hospital located in an urban city of South Korea. All enrolled participants in this study had Internet access in their homes and were at least 30 years of age. The hospital is a university-affiliated medical center that has an 800-bed capacity. The data were collected from January 2003 to December 2005. Diabetes was diagnosed according to the American Diabetes Association criteria (The Expert Committee on the Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes Mellitus, 1997). Obesity was defined as BMI N23 \mathrm { k g } / \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }(Sung et al., 2001). The selection criteria required that participants be able to perform blood glucose self-testing and self-medication, be able to input their data on the web site, and have their own cellular phone. Patients were excluded if they had a clinical history of heart failure, hepatic dysfunction, or renal insufficiency with a creatinine level N 1.5 mg/dl or if they had been using insulin pumps.

A total of 40 patients met the above criteria and agreed to participate. They were randomized by a random permuted block design using a random number table and assigned to one of two groups: intervention ( n ~ = ~ 2 0 )or control ( n \ =20). Only 34 participants (18 from the intervention group and 16 from the control group) completed the entire study. In the intervention group, two participants failed to record their glucose levels for more than 4 weeks on the provided web site (www.biodang.com). As for the control group, four participants refused to participate before completing the 6-month period.

## 2.2. Intervention

The goal of the intervention was to maintain blood glucose level within a normal range \mathrm { ( H b A _ { \mathrm { 1 c } } }value < 7 \% )Before the intervention, each patient received (from the researchers) 30 minutes of instructions on entering blood glucose level data and questions on the web site. The patients logged on whenever it was convenient for them, using their cellular phone or personal computer.

The web site was developed by endocrinologists, the researcher, and computer programmers. The screen viewed by patients and physicians on the web site (www.biodang. com) for diabetes management is described as follows: basic profile, personal history, family history, and laboratory data of the participants are shown at the top. Self-checked glucose levels are noted in the middle; these levels are recorded during fasting and postprandial (breakfast, lunch and dinner) periods. At the bottom, drug information, patient memos, and the recommendations of physicians are shown.

The participants sent data regarding their self-monitored blood glucose levels and drug information. Each participant has an electronic medical record and was routinely followed up at the hospital. The researcher obtained the participant’s blood glucose levels, medication, and details of some events. In addition to this information, the researcher also viewed the participant’s basic personal history, including historical data, family history, smoking habits, BMI, blood pressure, and baseline laboratory data.

After integrating the above information, the researcher sent optimal recommendations to each patient via the cellular phone and the Internet weekly. For example, recommendations included the following: bPlease decrease the long-acting insulin by two unitsQ; b Please add one tablet of sulfonylurea in the eveningQ; bPlease check the amount that you eatQ; bLack of exercise may be the cause of the aggravated glucose levelQ; and bYour glucose control seems to be good.Q The researcher provided the intervention for 6 months. The researcher was a nurse, a diabetic educator, and a professor at a nursing college. The intervention consisted of continuous education and reinforcement of diet and exercise, medication adjustment, and frequent self-monitoring of blood glucose levels. All medication adjustments were communicated to the participants’ physician to make changes in the patients’ medications. Modifications on medicine via the electronic medical record were transferred to the participants’ physician by the researcher. If a patient failed to forward blood glucose level data for more than 1 week, a warning message was sent. If, after four consecutive weeks, patients still fail to send their data despite the warning, they are withdrawn from the intervention group.

Participants in the control group met the endocrinologist specialist two or four times during the 6-month period. An endocrinologist provided the participants with recommendations about their medications, medication dosages, lifestyle

modifications, and other treatment modalities. Whether the doctor chose that the patient consult for special education or the patient himself or herself wished for it, the nurse or the dietitian comes to aid with more individualized and detailed information for lifestyle modification.

## 2.3. Procedure and measures

A quasi-experimental, repeated measures design was used in this study. Before the intervention, patients’ demographic variables, levels of { \mathrm { H b A } } _ { 1 \mathrm { c } } ,fasting plasma glucose (FPG), 2-hour postmeal glucose (2HPMG), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDLC) were measured as pretest data. Patients’ level of Hb \mathbf { A } _ { 1 \mathrm { c } } ,FPG, 2HPMG, TC, TG, and HDLC were measured again 3 and 6 months later.

\mathrm { H b A _ { \mathrm { 1 c } } }was determined by a high-performance liquid chromatography technique using the Variant II (Bio-Rad, Montreal, Quebec, Canada). Levels of FPG and 2HPMG were measured using the glucose oxidase method (Hitachi 7600, Hitachi, Japan). Levels of TC, TG, and HDLC were

measured using an automatic blood analyzer (Hitachi 736- 40, Hitachi).

## 2.4. Ethical considerations

The Medical Research Ethics Committee of the university hospital approved the research protocol. Written consents were obtained from those who agreed to participate in this study. Anonymity and confidentiality were guaranteed to participants. All participants used their identification and password to log on in the web site; only the researcher used the participants’ information.

## 2.5. Data analyses

The data were analyzed using the SAS statistical program (version 8.12, SAS Institute, Cary, NC). Chi-square test, t test, and Fisher’s Exact Test were used to test for the homogeneity of demographic and clinical characteristics between the intervention and control groups. Repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to determine the difference between the two groups and the

difference over time. When a significant difference was found by repeated measures ANOVA, paired t tests with Bonferroni correction were applied to identify specific differences.

# 3. Results
## 3.1. Sociodemographic and clinical findings

The characteristics of the intervention and control groups are shown in Table 1. The mean age of the control group was 48.5 years and that of the intervention group was 45.5 years. Slightly more than half (56.2%) of the participants in the control group were women; half (50.0%) of the participants in the intervention group were women. The mean BMI of the control group was 25.0 kg/m2 and that of the intervention group was 25.6 kg/m2 . The mean duration of diabetes was 7.8 years in the control group and 4.6 years in the intervention group. There was no significant difference in age, gender, BMI, duration of diabetes, treatment method, and blood glcose and serum lipid levels between the two groups at enrollment.
