## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Subjects and Methods
## System development

We developed a web-based ubiquitous information system, eMOD (electronic Management of Diabetes), for cellular phone (SK telecom [Seoul, Republic of Korea] users) and Internet users to provide diabetes self-management information. Not only smartphone but all mobile phones using the International Mobile Telecommunication-2000 (IMT-2000) system could connect to the eMOD system. This system provides real-time information about diet, dining out, hypoglycemia, sick-day management, stress management, and diabetes management. The substances were similar between websites accessed via mobile phone and computer. However, unlike a full Internet browser, the website for the mobile phone was mainly composed by texts. The benefit of this system is that it can provide educational program whenever a patient wants with mobile phone. The contents of the site were updated repeatedly during the period of the study.

## Study design

A randomized controlled trial was conducted from September 1, 2005 to March 31, 2006 at five hospitals. Patients 18– 80 years old with type 2 diabetes either drug naive or who had received prior drug therapy and had a glycated hemoglobin (A1C) level between 7% and 10% with stable glycemic control were recruited. Stable glycemic control was defined by no recent addition of hypoglycemic medications or change in insulin dosing by >10% in the previous 3 months. Subjects with severe medical illnesses including liver cirrhosis, endstage renal disease, and cancer were excluded. Persons participating in this study had Internet assess in their homes, their own cellular phone, and the ability to access the Internet and mobile web site. Forty-four volunteers were randomly assigned into either the eMOD intervention group (n ¼ 24) or control group (n ¼ 20). Patients in the intervention group received training in eMOD usage and logged in to the eMOD system whenever it was convenient for them. Diabetes educational books that have similar contents with the eMOD website were provided to patients in the control group. Subjects in both groups visited their physicians every 2 months. There were no differences in antidiabetes medication between control and intervention groups at baseline and the end of the study. Four patients in the intervention group were unable to finish the study because of difficulty accessing the web site or in using a cellular phone. Primary end points for the study were the changes in glycemic control (A1C, fasting plasma glucose [FPG], and 2-h postprandial plasma [PP2] glucose).

## Data collection and clinical evaluation

Demographic and clinical data were collected at baseline and 6 months after the intervention. Height and weight were measured in the morning with the subjects wearing light clothing but no shoes. Blood pressure was measured with a mercury sphygmomanometer on the right arm with the subjects in a sitting position after a 5-min rest. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight (in kg) divided by the square of the height (in m). Diabetes’ duration and diabetes complications were ascertained through examination of the medical

record. Diabetes complications recorded included retinopathy, neuropathy (absence of ankle jerks and reduced vibratory sensation, or impairment on nerve conduction studies or autonomic function tests), or overt proteinuria (24-h urine protein 300 mg=day). FPG, PP2 glucose, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-cholesterol), low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-cholesterol), and triglycerides were measured with an autoanalyzer (Beckman Coulter, Fullerton, CA). A1C was measured by highperformance liquid chromatography (Tosoh, Tokyo, Japan).

## Statistical analysis

Frequencies or mean  SD values of data were obtained. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 11.0 for Windows (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). Student’s t test and Pearson’s 2 test were used for comparison of differences between the two groups. Post-intervention changes in clinical outcomes were assessed using the Wilcoxon Sign Rank test for within-group analysis. Linear regression analyses were used to examine the relationship between the eMOD intervention and the change of A1C. All probability values were twotailed, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

# Results
## Characteristics of participants

The baseline characteristics of the two groups are shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences in the age,

gender, BMI, duration of diabetes, A1C, and FPG levels at baseline. The overall study population was predominantly male (77.5%) with a mean age of 42.5  10.6 years in the intervention group and 42.3  7.6 years in the control group. The A1C levels were higher than 8% in each group (intervention, 9.0  2.3%; control, 8.6  1.2%; P ¼ 0.458).

## Feasibility of eMOD system in the intervention group

The contents of the eMOD system included management of diabetes, diet, dining out, exercise, hypoglycemia, sickday management, and stress management. The eMOD system was accessed via computer 160 times during the study period. The most frequently accessed content via computer was ‘‘diet’’ and ‘‘stress management.’’ The eMOD system was accessed 191 times via cellular phone during the intervention period, and the most popular content was ‘‘dining out’’ (Table 2).

## Effectiveness of eMOD system intervention on glycemic control

Table 3 summarizes within-group differences from baseline in the outcome measures for each group of participants. The improvement in A1C for the intervention group was -1.53  1.42% (P ¼ 0.031), compared with no difference in the control group (-0.49  1.07%, P ¼ 0.257) after 6 months. However, there was no statistical difference in A1C between intervention and control groups at 6 months (7.5  0.4 vs. 8.1  0.3%, P ¼ 0.23). FPG levels did not differ significantly between the two groups or over time. The PP2 glucose level was decreased in the intervention group (-54.58  1.0 mg=dL, P ¼ 0.03) but not in the control group (-24.79  1.0 mg=dL, P ¼ 0.352) after 6 months of the intervention.

## Relationship between feasibility of the eMOD system and change of A1C

Using simple linear regression, we found that there was a significant relationship between the change in A1C and the frequency of access to the eMOD system via cellular phone (r ¼ 0.766, P ¼ 0.03; coefficient -0.147). The frequency of access to the eMOD system via computer, however, was not associated with change in A1C (P ¼ 0.658; coefficient -0.032).
