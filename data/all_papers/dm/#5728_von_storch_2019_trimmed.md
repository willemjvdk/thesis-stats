# Participants and Methods
## Study population

This is a randomized prospective study on 2441 patients with T2DM covered by the health insurance Central Kran-

kenversicherung AG (Central). Central is a private insurance company providing telemedical assistance as described below. In three consecutive phases at intervals of 3 to 5 months, participants meeting the inclusion criteria listed below were randomly allocated to either intervention or control group. They were contacted through regular mail to ask for participation in the study. The sample structure is shown in Table 1. Participants of the intervention group additionally received a personal code to register online for the LTP. After obtaining consent, both groups were contacted by mail to fill in the online baseline questionnaire. Only those patients who completed baseline and 3-months follow-up questionnaires were included in the analysis. Adults between 40 and 67 years of age with T2DM according to the diagnostic criteria of T2DM (ICD Code E11) were eligible for the study. Patients with pregnancy, under treatment for cancer or other lifethreatening illnesses, cognitive or mobility impairment, or in need of nursing care were excluded. Participants’ medical history was obtained by health insurance data of the patients.

## Telemedicine-assisted self-management program

The intervention consists of a 12-month LTP that combines telemonitoring with a structured lifestyle intervention through an individual need-based telephone coaching. The coaching is based on the Transtheoretical Model of Prochaska.16,17 It consists of a stage-matched personalized assessment, including different modules representing major problem areas with emphasis on diet, physical activity, selfcontrol, emergency, clinical, and stress management. It also involves a mental motivational training and development of daily life routines for the individual participant. The staged program enables the coach to identify patient’s needs and develop adequate support strategies. In the following 3 months, an intensive consultation phase took place with phone calls at least every month.

Patients continued to be cared for by their physicians during the study as the LTP is intended to complement usual medical care. The control group underwent care as usual, consisting of routine care by their physician without additional treatment.

At the beginning of the program the patients received a tablet computer, a glucometer, and a step counter. The tablet computer facilitated the rigorous collection of blood glucose levels, step counts, and dietary information. It also enabled secure data transfer and communication between patient and coach. Telemedical devices served as a feedback tool for the patients to provide insight into the impact of behavior changes on their blood glucose levels. Coach and participant discussed and interpreted the submitted data concerning the participant’s health behavior thereby showing the participant that he/she can actively influence the diabetes by own health behavior. In each phone-based meeting, coach and participant developed and agreed on personal health goals that helped to better control the disease step by step. Patients were encouraged to use the devices on a daily basis to actively control and manage their diabetes in daily life.

The program runs for a period of 1 year. After 3 months, results were collected and analyzed to address adequacy. The present study is a prospective study approved by the Ethics Advisory Board of the Medical Faculty of the University of Cologne (project-ID: 17-021).

## Data collection and measures

For the purpose of this study, the observations of the 3- month analysis were considered to evaluate the short-term impact of the intervention used in this study on objective measures of diabetes course control. The program was based upon the different phases of the Transtheoretical Model, which included awareness of patients’ own health problem, consideration of changing behavior, preparation of taking concrete actions, and implementing the new behavior into daily life. The first adaptation and practice of the health behavior is implemented in the first 3 months. It then becomes apparent whether the adapted health behavior can be maintained in the long-run. Therefore, all participants will be followed for an additional 9 months and complete another two online questionnaires. This follow-up allows to test if any improvement noted at the beginning can be maintained after 1 year.

Data were collected from the insurance patients’ records, coaching software, and online research questionnaires as described below. Serum levels of \mathrm { H b A } _ { \mathrm { 1 c } }were obtained from the physician’s routine laboratory. The following baseline characteristics were collected from the questionnaire: demographics, medications, medical history, health indicators, diabetes self-management, and technology commitment. The primary endpoint was serum levels of { \mathrm { H b A } } _ { 1 \mathrm { c } } ,measured at baseline and 3 months. Serum \mathrm { H b A } _ { \mathrm { 1 c } }levels reflect a patient’s blood glucose concentration during the previous 2 to 3 months.18 As shown by Nathan et al., \mathrm { H b A } _ { \mathrm { 1 c } }correlates closely with a complete measure of average glycemia.18 Therefore, only \mathrm { H b A } _ { \mathrm { 1 c } }values were taken into consideration as indicator of the course of disease. The evaluation of dia-

betes self-management and the body mass index (BMI) as assessed by the questionnaire described below was the secondary endpoint, also measured at baseline and 3 months. Diabetes-related comorbidities were considered as described in the CoDim Study.4 Additionally, hypercholesterolemia and arterial hypertension were included in the analysis.19 Participants from the intervention group measured their physical activity (step count) continuously from the first day of the intervention on. Technology commitment of the intervention group and control group was measured at baseline through the questionnaire.20,21 Technology Perception was measured within the intervention group after 3 months.

Diabetes self-management was assessed by the Diabetes Self-Management Questionnaire (DSMQ) ( \mathsf { \alpha } = 0 . 8 4 ) ^ { 2 2 }based on specific diabetes-related problems. The 16-item scale reflects different aspects of diabetes self-management components, including dietary control, medication adherence, blood glucose monitoring, physical activity, and physicians contact.

Technology commitment was measured using the validated short-scale technology commitment ( \mathsf { \alpha } = \mathsf { \overline { { 0 } } } . 7 4 ) ^ { 2 0 }developed to predict the successful use of technologies, especially for older adults. The 12-item scale measures three concepts, functioning as determinants of individual differences in readiness to deal with technology: technology acceptance, technology competence, and technology control.

A step counter indicated physical activity. Steps were automatically reported through Bluetooth several times a day.

A technology perception measure based on the findings of previous research23–25 was created by the study investigators.

These questions were developed to examine the concrete interplay with the telemedical devices in the study taking into account different components of the Technology Acceptance Model 3 (TAM3): perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness, technology self-efficacy, relevance to everyday life, perceived enjoyment, and subjective norm.25 Additionally, two categories from previous qualitative research were added: (1) feeling of being controlled and (2) sense of security, which were not covered by the \mathrm { T A M } 3 . ^ { 2 3 }For the eight items, participants were asked to rate them on a five-point scale.

## Data analysis

Summary statistics (mean [M], standard deviations [SDs], and percentages) were calculated for descriptive characteristics of the participants. Baseline characteristics of completers and dropouts at baseline as well as of intervention group and control group were compared.

Two-way mixed analysis of variances (ANOVAs) with the factors, time (baseline and 3 months) and treatment (intervention group and control group), were conducted for the primary outcome \mathrm { H b A } _ { \mathrm { 1 c } }and secondary outcomes diabetes self-management and BMI, including partial eta squared ( { \eta _ { \mathrm { p } } } ^ { 2 } )as effect size.26 Paired and independent samples’ t-tests, including Cohen’s d as measure of effect size27 were used to assess the differences for the primary and secondary outcomes, including physical activity and group differences after 3 months. The significance level was set at 0.05. In case of missing data, listwise deletion was applied. Sample sizes are indicated in the Tables. Technology perception was evaluated descriptively. Statistical analysis was conducted with IBM SPSS- Statistics 25.
