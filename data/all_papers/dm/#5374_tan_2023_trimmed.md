# Methods/design
## Study sample and randomization

The OPTIMUM trial was conducted at Sengkang Polyclinic located in a north-eastern Singapore public housing estate with a population of about 300,000 multi-ethnic Asian residents.15 Approximately 900 to 1000 patients attend this polyclinic daily during office hours each weekday.10 The flow diagram summarises the design of the RCT (Figure 1). Eligibility of patients was confirmed prior to consent taking, which includes T2DM with latest HbA1c of 7% to 10%, age between 26 and 65 years, non-smoker or quit smoking for at least 12 months, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 45 ml/min/1.73m2 or higher and absence or presence of only mild non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy without any macular involvement. The patients who have HbA1c of more than 10%, cognitive impairment, pre-existing proliferative diabetic retinopathy or maculopathy, eGFR less than 45 ml/min/1.73m2 , known peripheral vascular, cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease were excluded. The patients were assigned using random permuted blocks in a 1:1 ratio to the intervention and control arms. Computer-generated block sequence concealed within opaque envelopes. The participants randomised to the control arm received usual care. Those assigned to the intervention received OPTIMUM HTM for 6 months as described below. The complete protocol of the study is published elsewhere and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04306770).14

## Intervention

Each patient in the intervention arm was provided a set of three Bluetooth-enabled devices: a weighing scale, a glucometer and an electronic blood pressure monitor for their sole use during the RCT. The study team instructed these patients to use the three devices and synchronised them with the OPTIMUM app on their personal smartphones. The patients also used the app to answer the health assessment questionnaires, which automatically relayed responses to the study team. The app was supported by the cloud-based Philips VitalHealth® platform for population health management. The patients in the intervention arm were instructed to perform the following tasks in the 6 months during the RCT:

Tele-education. Over eight consecutive weeks after study enrolment, the patients received weekly push reminders for self-monitoring via the OPTIMUM app and to watch the educational videos on T2DM, healthy eating habits and mindset, alcohol and smoking advice and various physical activity demonstrations. These videos were intended to improve patient’s self-management of T2DM. After each video, the patients responded to a short quiz via the app to assess their knowledge based on the video content.

Tele-monitoring and tele-support. The patients monitored their body weight, blood pressure, heart rate and capillary glucose levels according to the stipulated schedule of the

trial. The study team reviewed them asynchronously using the clinician-facing dashboard in the system. These thresholds embedded in the OPTIMUM management algorithm (Figure 2) are set by the study team members based on local clinical practice guidelines.16 The patients received immediate feedback from the app if their clinical parameters deviated beyond the stipulated range. The patients were prompted to verify their measurements and screen for related symptoms and their adherence to medications. Study nurses reviewed their responses before taking appropriate actions based on pre-specified escalation protocols. These actions included nurse-led tele-consultations or arranging for physician review at the study site.

# Outcomes
The outcomes were assessed at 6 months follow-up. HbA1c was measured as a primary outcome. The following secondary clinical outcomes were assessed: weight, SBP and DBP, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein, low-density lipoprotein, triglycerides, fasting blood sugar, blood creatinine, eGFR, urine albumin-creatinine ratio and medication adherence using the Medication Adherence Report Scale (MARS-5).17 The following secondary psychosocial

outcomes were assessed: total physical activity METS (metabolic equivalent of task) using the International Physical Activity Questionnaire-Short Form (IPAQ-SF),18 Michigan Diabetes Knowledge Test (MDKT),19 diabetes distress using Problem Areas in Diabetes (PAID),20,21 Self-Care Inventory-Revised (SCIR),22 Patient Activation Measure (PAM)23 and quality of life using the European Quality of Life-5 Dimensions ( \mathrm { E Q - 5 D - 5 L } ) ^ { 2 4 }instrument. For MDKT, the percentage score was computed for items 1 to 23; 1 to 14; and 15 to 23. For the SCIR instrument, three items (checking ketones, adjusting insulin and wearing Medic Alert) were not scored since the current study only included patients with T2DM. The SCIR scores were converted to a 0- to 100-point scale for ease of interpretation.22

## Statistical analysis

Outcomes were analysed based on the per-protocol approach, such that the data available at the end of the follow-up period of 6 months was used. The sample size computed a priori for this study was 165 per arm or a total study population of 330 for the trial to detect a difference in HbA1c of 0.5% based on a two-sided 5%

significance level and 80% power, considering 30% dropout rate over a 24-month follow-up.14 The intervention and usual care group characteristics were described using mean and SD for continuous variables and number and percentage for the categorical variables. Student’s t-test and chi-squared test were used to assess the baseline differences between the two groups. The change from baseline to 6 months within the intervention and usual care group was evaluated using a paired t-test for continuous variables and the McNemar test for categorical variables.

HbA1c, the primary outcome, was assessed as a continuous variable and a categorical variable, that is, unacceptable versus not, based on a cut-off of >8%.25 The association of the treatment group (intervention versus usual care) with the primary and secondary clinical and psychosocial outcomes was analysed using multivariable linear regression and multivariable logistic regression for continuous and categorical outcome variables, respectively. This analysis was conducted in two steps. Firstly, model 1 was run with the treatment group as the independent variable and the corresponding baseline value of the outcome as a covariate. Subsequently, model 2 was run with the treatment group as the independent variable, the corresponding baseline value of the outcome as a covariate and the following additional covariates: age, gender, ethnicity, education, BMI, duration of diabetes and medical subsidy. The selection of additional covariates was guided by clinical relevance and past literature.26,27 For the outcome of medication adherence, the MARS-5 score was categorised as adherence = 1 if the total score was 25 and adherence = 0 if the total score was less than 25. Each of the five questions on the MARS-5 instrument was analysed as a categorical outcome with yes if the participant rated the question as 5 (i.e. never) and no if the participant rated the question as any of the following: 1 = always, 2 = often, 3 = sometimes or 4 = rarely. Since patients may have used more than one diabetes medication, the analysis considered the non-independence of measurements. Hence, multivariable logistic regression was used, incorporating the clustering effects due to the non-independence of measurements. The significance level was set at 5%. All analyses were performed in Stata version 14.28

# Results
The final study population comprised 319 patients with T2DM, with 159 in the intervention group and 160 in the usual care group: 6 of them dropped out from the intervention group and 5 from the usual care group due to relocation of residence, change of healthcare provider, inability to adhere to the protocol due to work or family commitments. No adverse event was reported throughout the intervention period. Table 1 shows that patients were well-matched with no major differences between the two groups across baseline sociodemographic characteristics. The average age of the patients was 52.66 years, with 40.75% males and

91.54% Singaporean. The majority of patients were married (80.56%) and employed (full-time, 66.77%; parttime, 7.84%), and more than half (55.80%) were utilising medical subsidies.

# Primary outcome
Table 2 shows that the mean HbA1c significantly decreased over 6 months in both the intervention (p < 0.001) and the usual care group (p < 0.001). Table 3 shows that the adjusted average HbA1c at 6 months for the intervention group was 7.31% and for the usual care group was 7.65%. The HbA1c at 6 months was significantly lower in the intervention group compared to the usual care group by 0.32% ( p = 0 . 0 0 5 )after adjusting for baseline value and by 0.34% ( p = 0 . 0 0 4 )after adjusting for both baseline value and additional covariates. When HbA1c was analysed as a categorical variable, the participants in the intervention group were 0.31 times less likely to be in the unacceptable HbA1c range than those in the usual care group after adjusting for baseline value and additional covariates (p < 0.001).

# Secondary outcomes
While the mean weight, BMI, SBP and DBP, triglycerides and fasting blood sugar significantly decreased over 6 months for both the intervention and the usual care group, the SCIR scores significantly increased (Table 2). Total cholesterol decreased over 6 months for both the intervention and the usual care group, but this decrease was significant only for the intervention group (p < 0.001).

The following secondary outcomes were significantly better for the intervention group as compared to the usual care group at 6 months after adjusting for corresponding baseline value and additional covariates: SBP, DBP, total cholesterol, response to the question of MARS-5 scale on using the diabetic medication less than prescribed and SCIR score. The SBP at 6 months was significantly lower for the intervention group compared to the usual care group by 3.11 (p = 0.035) after adjusting for baseline value and by 2.98 (p = 0.044) after adjusting for baseline value and additional covariates. The adjusted average SBP at 6 months for the intervention group was 130.12 mmHg and for the usual care group was 133.10 mmHg. The adjusted average DBP at 6 months for the intervention group was 72.14 mmHg and for the usual care group was 76.39 mmHg.
