## EXTRACTED SECTIONS

# Methods
Starting in October 2001, 265 consecutive patients were enrolled from four study centres: two primary care centres (in the cities of Espoo and Vantaa) and two university hospital outpatient clinics (at Jorvi and Peijas hospitals). These patients represented about 11% of those with diabetes in the study area. All centres employed identical software.

The subjects were randomized (in the ratio 2:1) to the study or control group. The study group used an e-health application (n ¼ 101) with a DMS and a home care link. The comparison was with standard care (n ¼ 74), which did not involve e-health, i.e. regular general practitioner visits about every three months. Annual visits were normal, but more frequent visits were not denied if clinically necessary. The study group had one educational visit (to set up the home care link), not included as care contact. The home care link was made available to study patients free of charge.

Before randomization, 90 patients (34%) were excluded according to the predetermined exclusion criteria (technical reasons 35 patients, other diseases or lifestyle problems 25 patients and 30 patients who either refused or their reason was unknown).

Patients downloaded their measurements directly from the blood glucose meter into the regional database (which stored the results and generated reports) using a modem. The clients were web-browsers. Joint use of patient

data was possible by mutual agreement between the organizations. The self-management system allowed the diabetes team to transmit short message service (SMS) text messages to patients with mobile phones and Internet access. Data transfer protection was handled by secure socket layer (SSL) encryption. The care team was able to access the home diary with the patient’s consent.

The study was approved by the appropriate ethics committee. Paired t-tests and analysis of variance tests were used for statistical testing (baseline versus one-year within groups and between groups).

# Results
The patients in the two groups did not differ from each other in the proportion of Types 1 or 2 diabetes. The groups were similar with respect to age and baseline measurements, but glycosylated haemoglobin \mathrm { ( H b A _ { 1 c } ) }was lower in the study patients than in the controls (Table 1).

After 12 months HbA1c decreased significantly in both groups of patients (Table 2). The differences were small, but \mathrm { H b A } _ { \mathrm { 1 c } }was significantly lower in the study group than the controls. Diastolic blood pressure, fasting plasma glucose, serum total cholesterol, serum LDL-cholesterol and serum triglycerides were significantly lower in the study than in the control group (Table 1). This was achieved with fewer visits by study patients to doctors and nurses (Table 3).

Aspirin, statin or ACE-inhibitor drug use did not differ before or after follow-up between the two groups. Process quality was improved in the study group; foot and injection site inspection, as well as ECG studies were performed significantly more frequently during follow-up in the study group than in the control group according to protocol.
