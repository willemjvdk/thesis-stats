## a b s t r a c t

Objectives: The association between the use of telehomecare technology and functional health literacy is rather unexplored in the current literature. This relationship could prove important in the future management of chronic diseases, as technology has become a more integrated part of modern healthcare systems. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to explore how the use of telehomecare technology affects the level of functional health literacy over a period of 10 months.

Study design: Randomized controlled trial.

Methods: Our sample comprised 116 patients diagnosed with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. A face-to-face interview and an objective test of functional health literacy were conducted with each patient at baseline and again at follow-up after 10 months. Twenty-six patients were lost to follow-up and thus, providing a total of 47 chronic obstructive pulmonary disease patients in the intervention group and 43 in the control group for this follow-up study. The level of functional health literacy was assessed with the Danish Test of Functional Health Literacy in Adults. The difference from baseline to followup, in both the functional health literacy score and the mean response time to the entire Danish Test of Functional Health Literacy in Adults (TOFHLA), was tested for statistical significance between the intervention group and the control group.

Results: A significant increase in functional health literacy is observed in both the groups from baseline to follow-up, but there is no statistical difference between groups (P-value ¼ 0.62).

Conclusions: A significant increase in the functional health literacy score was observed in both groups, but the findings of this present study provide no information on what causes the increase, so further research is needed to explore the increase in functional health literacy score more thoroughly and establish if the use of telehomecare technology is a part of the explanation.

# Methods
The present study was conducted as a part of a PhD study on the interaction between functional health literacy (FHL) and the use of telehomecare. The PhD study is a part of a large-scale telehomecare project, TeleCare North, which targets patients diagnosed with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). The TeleCare North project is conducted as a randomized controlled trial comprising a randomized sample of 1225 COPD patientsd647 patients in the control group and 578 in the intervention group. The TeleCare North is a full-scale, pragmatic, two-level, cluster-randomized trial comprising the entire Northern region of Denmark. The clusters are defined by the different municipality districts in the region. The overall objective of the TeleCare North trial is to evaluate whether a telehealth solution (the Telekitddescribed below), intended as a supplement to usual care, can increase health-related quality of life in patients diagnosed with COPD compared with usual practice. Furthermore, the trial seeks to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of the telehealth solution. All participants were asked to sign a written, informed consent form with enrolment in the TeleCare North trial. The randomization process and general outline of the TeleCare North trial has been described in detail before this study. 13

## Sample and setting

The participants in the present study were consecutively recruited from the TeleCare North project following the randomization. The TeleCare North project management wanted to limit the influence of the present study on the overall TeleCare North trial and, therefore, the number was decided by the management to be maximum 60 patients in each group. The recruitment process for both the overall TeleCare North study and the present study is illustrated in Fig. 1. Patients were included consecutively from a list

provided by TeleCare North project management, where patients were ordered according to their birth date, DD-MM-YY; i.e. according to the day in the month when they were born. Sixty patients in the intervention group and 60 patients in the control group gave their consent. During the data collection phase, four patients in the control group withdrew their consent and were not replaced. Consequently, the study comprised a total of 116 COPD patientsd60 patients in the intervention group and 56 in the control group.

As this study was conducted as part of the TeleCare North project, the same inclusion criteria applied for this study; participants were suitable for inclusion if they had been diagnosed with COPD by spirometry and received treatment as described in the GOLD guidelines. Further, COPD had to be registered as their primary disease by a general practitioner. Participants had to be listed with a general practitioner in the Northern Region of Denmark and have a fixed residence. In addition, at least one of the following criteria had to be met: COPD Assessment Test 10 and the Modified Medical Research Council scale 2 or 3 and have had at least two exacerbations within the past 12 months. Participants had to speak

Danish and have a phone connection. Participants were excluded if they had a cognitive impairment, were unable to understand Danish sufficiently to complete the study questionnaires or had no phone line.

The principal investigator recruited participants by telephone and provided an oral explanation of the study. On recruitment, the participants were assured confidentiality and privacy at all times during the study, and they were informed about their right to withdraw at any time. Oral informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in the study. Relevant data, that needed to be collected face-to-face, were collected in the participants' own home at the most convenient times for them. All participants reside in the area of Northern Jutland, Denmark. First, a baseline study was carried out to assess the short-term interaction between FHL and the use of a telehomecare solution, referred to as the Telekit (described in detail below). Details regarding our baseline study, which was conducted shortly after receiving the intervention described below, have been described before this study.14 The present study is a follow-up study assessing the long-term interaction between

FHL and the Telekit (a 10-month time period). Twenty-six (22.41%) of the 116 COPD patients were lost to follow up; 13 in the intervention group and 13 in the control group, providing a total of 47 patients in the intervention group and 43 in the control group for this follow-up study. ## The Telekit intervention

The telehomecare intervention used in this study is applicable to the following general definition: ‘a system of care delivery based on the acquisition and the use of information obtained by a sensor-based technology located in the residence and transmitted to’ a remote location for dispatch to care provider . 15

The 47 COPD patients in the intervention group received a telehomecare solution comprising a tablet, a Digital Blood Pressure Monitor, a Health Scale and an attached Fingertip Pulse Oximeter. The software in the tablet is developed by Silverbullit, Aarhus, Denmark (http://opentele.silverbullet.dk). The tablet can collect and transmit relevant data regarding the current health state of the patient, including these diseasespecific data: oxygen saturation, weight, pulse and blood pressure are obtained with the aforementioned equipment. Further, the tablet contains software that automatically provides instructions on how to manage and cope with COPD during exacerbations, and it also contains information on the management of COPD in general.

The intervention group received education and instructions in the use of the Telekit by a nurse from their municipality at the initiation of the TeleCare North project. The nurse demonstrated how to operate the tablet and how to conduct the physical measurements, and also went through the tablet manual with patients before letting them try the tablet alone. Healthcare personnel, primarily nurses in community care, were responsible for the monitoring and overall patient care in the intervention group during the study. Before the initiation of the TeleCare North trial, they participated in two full-day workshop sessions with focus on the technical aspects of the tablet and general disease awareness and communication with patients.

During the first two weeks (both weekdays and weekends) of the study, the COPD patients were asked to measure their vital signs on a daily basis. After the first two weeks, this was reduced to once or twice a week. The specific days for obtaining measurements were planned and programmed into the tablet in cooperation with the nurse, who also

demonstrated the monitoring procedure and provided contact information on the community nurse. A follow-up session (45-min) was planned three to four weeks after the first initiating session to ensure the correct use of the Telekit and to check if the precoded threshold values of the physical measurement needed adjustment. The control group received usual care. In Denmark, the general practitioners are responsible for monitoring and treating patients diagnosed with COPD and the municipalities are responsible for providing practical help and nursing care to these patients. It is possible for COPD patients to make appointments with their general practitioner or practice nurse to receive professional support in the management of their COPD. Community-based care and practical help/nursing care varies across the municipalities in Denmark, but generally, community care is provided at regular intervals based on a clinical evaluation of individual needs. Healthcare personnel in community care are not necessarily certified nurses or particularly skilled in the management of COPD, and they are certainly not on a telephone call basis. Healthcare professionals in the control group did not receive the training that the personnel in the intervention group did, and the individual COPD patients continued their usual care and treatment throughout the study period. Further information about the intervention in the TeleCare North trial can be found in the study protocol published before this study.13

## Data collection

The following clinical parameters were obtained for each COPD patient with their enrolment in the TeleCare North project: forced expiration in 1 s (FEV1), forced vital capacity (FVC) systolic blood pressure, pulse, height and weight. In this study, relevant patient-specific data for statistical analysis were collected at baseline and at follow-up after 10 months, where the principal investigator carried out face-to-face interviews with each COPD patient and assessed their individual level of FHL by asking them to complete the Danish TOFHLA.16 The Danish TOFHLA consists of a numeracy part and a reading comprehension part with 17 and 50 items respectivelydjust like the original American TOFHLA.17 The numeracy part focuses on the ability of the participant to understand instructions for taking medication, keep a clinical appointment, understand financial assistance and so forth. The reading comprehension part is carried out as a modified Cloze procedure in which words are randomly removed from a reading passage18; every fifth to seventh word is randomly deleted in different health-related reading passages in the Danish TOFHLA, and the participant then selects the most fitting word from a list of four possible words. The total score of the Danish TOFHLA is divided into the three following categories: inadequate (0e59), marginal (60e74) and adequate (75e100)djust like the American TOFHLA.17 The development and validation of the Danish TOFHLA has been described in detail and published before the present study. 16

## The baseline study

The data collection at baseline was initiated after the participants had been using the Telekit at home for approximately

two months to ensure that they were familiar with the equipment, so this would not affect the results. During the baseline interview the following demographic data were collected: gender, age, civil status, years of education and participation in rehabilitation or exercise programs. The Danish TOFHLA was completed by each participant.

## The (present) follow-up study

In the present follow-up study, only information on participation in rehabilitation or exercise programs was collected again, as this parameter could have changed since baseline. Each of the 90 COPD patients also completed the Danish TOFHLA again, like they did at the baseline.

## Data analysis

The demographic data, clinical parameters and results of the Danish TOFHLA were entered into the statistical software Stata version 13,19 and the statistical software SPSS version 21.20 Various statistics was conducted in Stata to describe the study group in terms of mean, frequency, percent and standard deviations. SPSS was used to compare differences in the FHL score from baseline to follow-up.

## The baseline study

As part of our baseline study, the basic characteristics age, gender, civil status, years of education and participation in rehabilitation or exercise programs, and clinical parameters height, weight, SBP, pulse, FEV and FVC, were tested for statistically significant differences between the intervention group and the control group to determine comparability. Differences in means, with regards to categorical data, between the two groups were assessed using a chi-square test and differences in means for metric data were assessed using an independent t-test. An objective was to assess the statistically significant difference in the mean FHL score between groups at baseline, which was also explored by an independent t-test.

## The (present) follow-up

In this present follow-up study, an independent t-test was conducted to investigate whether a significant difference in FHL was present between groups after 10 months. Furthermore, a paired t-test was used to explore whether a significant change in the FHL score within groups could be observed from baseline to follow-up. Finally, a multiple regression analysis was also performed to explore the association between FHL and the two groups further. Age and gender were adjusted for in the multiple regression model and since, there is a significant relationship between education and level of (functional) health literacy, this parameter was also included in the model.21

# Results
Our sample comprised 90 COPD patients (26 patients were lost to follow up, see Methods); 47 patients in the intervention group receiving the Telekit and 43 in the control group receiving usual care. The mean age in the control group was

69.5 years (range 44e87 years) with a standard deviation of 10.12 and the mean age in the intervention group was 70.2 (range 54e88 years) with a standard deviation of 9.06.

In our baseline study, the intervention group and the control group were found to be comparable, as no significant differences were observed between groups (including the level of baseline FHL), details and specific P-values have been presented in a previous publication.14 However, with the loss of 26 participants in this follow-up study, the basic characteristics are not completely balanced among the two groups any longer; as shown in Table 1, the number of women is higher in the control group (58.1%) compared with the intervention group (46.8%), wherein the unadjusted mean age is slightly higher than in the control group. Furthermore, a higher percentage reports living with a partner in the intervention group (59.6%) compared with the control group (39.5%). There are also some notable differences in the level of education; 36.1% in the intervention group reported elementary school as their

highest educational level compared with 18.6% in the control group, wherein 20.9% reported higher education as their educational level compared with 12.8% in the intervention group. In addition, the difference in the participation rate in rehabilitation or exercise programs should be noted, 51.2% in the control group vs 27.7% in the intervention group. When testing the basic characteristics and clinical parameters of the 90 in this follow-up study for statistically significant differ-’ ’ ences at baseline, both ‘pulse and ‘FVC were found to be significantly higher in the control group. Furthermore, the participation rate in rehabilitation or exercise programs was also significantly higher in the control group (P  0.022).

The distribution of FHL levels (inadequate, marginal and adequate) and the mean unadjusted FHL scores at baseline and follow-up divided on groups are shown in Table 2. There is an increase in the mean FHL scores from baseline to follow-up in both the intervention and the control group with a difference of 5.14 and 4.37 respectively. As a result, the percentage of COPD patients categorised as having an adequate level of FHL has also increased in both groups at follow-up; from 26

(55.3%) patients to 28 (59.6%) in the intervention group and from 24 (55.8%) patients to 28 (65.1%) in the control group. The independent t-test that explored the difference in the mean FHL score provides a P-value of 0.62, which indicates no statistical significant difference between the intervention and the control group at follow-up after 10 months. The paired ttest shows that the FHL score had increased significantly from baseline to follow-up in both groups: from 72.8 to 77.2 (P-value 0.003) in the control group and from 70.2 to 75.4 (P-value ¼ 0.001) in the intervention group.

The multiple regression analysis confirms that the difference in mean FHL score at follow-up is non-significant between the two groups (P-value 0.63). It should be noted that educational level and age are significantly associated with the FHL score at follow-up, see Table 3.
