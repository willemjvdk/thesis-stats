# Methods
## Participants

Patients admitted to the respiratory ward of the first hospital of Zunyi medical university for acute exacerbations of COPD from November 2017 to February 2019 during the study period were invited to participate. Inclusion criteria included age between 40 and 80 years, a diagnosis of COPD by a chest physician according to the criteria recommended by the American Thoracic Society,11 access to a smartphone and broadband internet connection, and the willingness to provide written informed consent. Patients were excluded if they failed to follow instructions or to use mobile health applications or had previously undertaken any COPD education programmes.

## Sample size and randomisation

Our target sample size was based on the previous finding of the moderate effect of a telehealth programme on hospitalisation in COPD patients.12 To achieve 80% power with a two-sided alpha of 0.05, the minimum sample size required was 35 subjects per group. To allow for an attrition rate of 10%, 78 participants (39per group) were considered necessary for the study.

Participants were randomised to the intervention and control groups following the collection of baseline data. Computer-generated random numbers were used to assign participants with a 1:1 allocation. The allocation was performed independently using blinded sealed envelopes.

This is a single-blind trial, the patients were aware of the group they were assigned, and the interventionist was aware that all participants contacted were in the intervention arm. However, the interventionist and supervisors were blinded to the baseline and follow-up measures. The assessments were performed by researchers who were blinded to the group allocation.

## Procedures

Recruitment of patients with COPD occurred during hospitalisation for exacerbations of their disease. Two experienced nurses working in the department of respiratory medicine were mainly responsible for the assessment of eligibility for recruitment. Once a patient fulfilling the inclusion criteria was identified, the researchers approached the patient and completed obtaining the consent and baseline data collection. Next, training in the use of the mobile health application was provided to each participant in the intervention group before discharge from hospital.

## Interventions

Intervention group. The participants in the intervention group (n = 39) undertook a mobile health application programme for self-management in addition to routine care. According to the theoretical basis and framework of the study, the

mobile health application programme consisted of three modules (Online Appendix):

I know COPD. This module provided knowledge and information support to participants.

Do it with me. This module provided visual aids to teach participants skills to manage the disease.

The home of the soul. This module mainly provided motivational support to participants, which included a peer support chat room and an expert support portal.

## Control group

Participants in the control group received routine care, which included health education (e.g. advice on maintaining physical activity three to five times/ week, taking medications and oxygen prescription) from a respiratory nurse at discharge.

## Measures

Measurement time points were at baseline, third, sixth and 12th months respectively. Assessments were performed by two researchers who were blinded to the group allocation.

## Health-related quality of life (HRQol)

HRQol used the COPD-Assessment Test (CAT). The CAT is a short, simple questionnaire for assessing the impact of COPD on quality of life.13 The scale consists of eight items and is scored from 0 to 40, where 0 to 10 indicates slight impact and 31 to 40 indicates very serious impact.

## Self-management behaviour

To assess self-management behaviour we used the COPD self-management scale (CSMS),14 which includes five domains (symptom management, daily life management, emotion management, information management and self-efficacy). The scale consists of 51 items scored from 51 to 255, with a higher score indicating a higher level of selfmanagement behaviour.

## Sustained behaviour change

Physical activity was measured using the questionnaire by asking participants if they exercised, the number of days per week, and the duration of each session. Smoking behaviour was evaluated by asking participants if they smoked and the number of cigarettes smoked a day through the questionnaire.

## Ethical considerations

The study was approved by the institutional review board (IRB) of the the first hospital of Zunyi medical university (no. 2018/03-7), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All eligible participants had the right to withdraw from the study at any point without any adverse effects on clinical care. The work was supported by a grant from the Science and Technology Department of Guizhou Province, China [Grant No. Qian Ke He LH (2017) 7114].

## Statistical analysis

Data were analysed using SPSS version 20.0, and the significance level was set at P < 0.05. For the description of demographic characteristics, the mean ± SD or median (IQR), frequency and percentage were used. For the equivalence between two groups at baseline, Student’s t-test, Wilcoxon’s rank-sum test, or ANOVA were used for continuous variables and chi-square test for categorical variables. Repeated measures analysis of variance (RANOVA) was used to determine whether the effect of the intervention was statistically significant within and between groups according to the health-related quality of life, self-management behaviour and sustained behaviour change. An intent-to-treat principle was applied to all analyses.

# Results
## Participant characteristics

Between November 2017 and February 2019, 78 participants were enrolled in the trial. Of the enrolled participants, 68 (intervention group, n = 35; control group, n = 33) completed the study. The main reasons for attrition are shown in Figure 1.

The average age of the participants was 63.9 (SD 6.8) years. There were more males (n = 55) than females (n = 23). The most common coexisting disease was hypertension (21⁄78), followed by heart disease (12⁄78) and type 2 diabetes mellitus (9⁄78). Baseline characteristics of the two groups are presented in Table 1. All of the group characteristics reached equivalence.

## Healthy-related quality life

The results of the CAT showed significant differences at third months (P = 0.041), sixth months (P =0.003) and 12th months (P =0.006) between the intervention and control groups. A significant difference in the overall means of the CAT scores between the two groups over time was also seen (P = 0.004). However, no significant changes were noted in the control group during the study period from baseline to 12th month (Table 2).

## Self-management behaviour

Improvements were shown in all domains of the CSMS at at baseline, third, sixth and 12th months between the intervention and control groups. Significant group-by-time interaction effects revealed group differences across time. The intervention group showed significant improvement in the total score of the CSMS (P=0.000) and in the domains of symptom management (P=0.000), daily life management (P=0.003), emotion management (P=0.006), information management (P=0.007) and self-efficacy (P=0.004) across the 12-month study period compared to the control group. Improvements in the total score by 23.01units, symptom management score by 7.28units, daily life management score by 5.08units, emotion management score by 4.5units, information management score by 5.87units and self-efficacy score by 4.98units were observed in the intervention group over the 12-month study period, whereas no significant changes were noted in the control group (Table 3).
