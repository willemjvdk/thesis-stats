# 2. Methods
This multicentre, single-blinded randomised controlled trial conducted in the Capital Region of Denmark randomised patients eligible for outpatient hospital-based PR to either PTR (intervention) or to

conventional PR (control). The trial protocol was registered at ClinicalT rials.gov (NCT02667171) January 28, 2016 and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Capital Region of Denmark (H-15019380) and the Danish Data Protection Agency (jr.no.: 2012–58-0004). The study procedure, the intervention content and components of the physical training has been explained in detail previously [6,7]. In brief, 134 patients with severe COPD were recruited from March 2016 to October 2017 from 8 hospital PR sites and randomised to either 10 weeks of conventional PR at the local site or 10 weeks supervised on-line PTR with a screen provided in the patients’ homes. This enabled the participants to see the healthcare professional/therapist as well as each other. Assessments were made before entry to the rehabilitation (baseline), at the end of the intervention, and 3- and 12 months after termination of the programme, respectively. Maintenance exercise was encouraged but not provided in either group during follow-up. Both PR and PTR were delivered according to the recently updated clinical guidelines from the Danish Health Authority [8] focusing on endurance- and resistance training as well as breathing techniques and nutritional support when needed. The primary outcome was change in 6MWD in meters walked, while secondary outcomes included other measures of physical activity, HRQoL using standard questionnaires, symptoms, and hospital admission and mortality by linkage with national registries. Physical activity level (PAL) in terms of steps per day and time being sedentary/active was measured in a subset of participants (n = 73) by wearing the ActivePAL triaxial accelerometer for 5 days (PAL Technologies Ltd., Glasgow, UK). Analyses were made with the intention to treat principle and per protocol, the latter defined as participation in≥70% of the sessions, using mixed effect models, and Cox proportional regression analyses for the healthcare outcomes. The models included adjustment for treatment group, age, sex, BMI, Charlson Comorbidity Index, FEV1 and smoking status, and a random effect for hospital allocation. In this paper only intention to treat analyses are reported. Analyses were performed using R 3.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

# 3. Results
Sixty-seven patients were randomised to each group; 55% were women, 75% were former smokers, mean age was 68.3 years (9.0), FEV % predicted 33.1 (9.4), and highest baseline 6MWD 327.3 m (102.8) [6]. Results from 12-month follow-up after completion of the programme, showing the unadjusted and adjusted between-group differences and the unadjusted within-group changes, are presented in Table 1. There was no difference in 6MWD between or within the groups at the 12-month follow-up. For the secondary outcomes there were overall also no significant between-group differences or changes within the groups. The only exceptions were within the PR group for 30-s sit-to-stand test, in which patients performed slightly better at follow-up. However, this was accompanied by a significant decrease in PAL and increase in the depression domain on HADS and could thus be a chance finding (Table 1). Of the 134 included patients, 39 and 45 patients in the PR and PTR group, respectively, were admitted to hospital during follow-up with the majority of the hospitalisations being related to a respiratory condition (data not shown). Eight patients in each group died, and the survival analyses showed no difference in risk of hospitalisation or mortality between the groups.
